i

The circle of fifths

Borkovsky gave every pitch class a colour by its place on the circle of fifths, because that circle is really the wheel of key relationship: neighbouring notes are close in colour and in sound.

ii

The spectral centroid

The average frequency of the sound, weighted by amplitude — the single number that says whether a sound is dark or bright. A bright sound gets spiky, a dark one gets round.

iii

Amplitude is radius

Every shape is radius-modulated by the sound's amplitude, restricted to lie between the longest and the shortest shape you allow, so the geometry swells and collapses with the envelope.

iv

Timbre is texture

The centroid decides whether a shape bristles, chimes or ribs. Turning the detail up adds more of each, and every bristle, chime and rib positions itself against the attack of the sound that made it.

v

Number of channels

Masks and monochrome are one channel, stereophonic is two, and a quadrant sets four colour quadrants turning against each other. The rendering follows the signal, and is no cheaper for it.

vi

Two channels at once

The left channel and the right channel are rendered as two different colour families, like looking through the same window from two sides.
