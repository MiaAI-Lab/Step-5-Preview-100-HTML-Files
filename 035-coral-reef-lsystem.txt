# REEF — A Garden That Builds Itself

## Short title
Coral Reef L-System

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive coral
reef grown from an L-system, called REEF. No external dependencies whatsoever — no
CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript
libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive,
valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a shallow tropical reef at midday. The sea is a vertical
gradient from bright #0a3a4c at the surface through #062736 and #03151c to
#020e14, with a warm sun wash in the upper right and a teal wash low on the left.
Coral is drawn in warm pink (#e88c78) and pale cream tips (#ffd6aa), against a
green-teal seabed. Seventy warm motes drift through the water column in additive
blend, so the whole frame picks up scattered light. Type is an italic serif
headline with one amber line, against 8.5–9.5px tracked monospace.

The growth: a recursive `branch()` function. Each call draws one segment from (x,y)
at angle a with a stroke width proportional to how close the branch is to the trunk
(d/depth × 4.2), then recurses twice — once at a − angle, once at a + angle — on a
child length of len × (decay/100). Branches at depth ≤ 2 get a second, narrower,
warmer stroke inside them so the tips glow. A third straight-on shoot appears at
27% of branches, decided by a hash of the branch's own x/y position rather than a
random draw, so the shape is identical every frame and only re-rolls when you press
Re-seed or resize. Five to nine root trunks are seeded across the seabed, each with
its own starting length and a small angular lean, growing up from a seabed drawn as
a sine-wobbled filled path.

The sway: each branch's angle is offset by sin(t·0.7 + generation·1.3 + x·0.01) ×
0.05 × (depth fraction + 0.3), so deeper branches swing more than the trunk and the
whole reef breathes in a wave from root to tip. Turning Sway off freezes it into a
static drawing.

The current: dragging horizontally accumulates a target current value (clamped to
±1.4) which eases toward the actual at 0.03 per frame; every mote's x advances by
current × 0.7 per frame, so dragging literally pushes the particles sideways
through the water.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas over the sea gradient with a floating UI overlay
whose pointer-events pass through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace
label above an italic serif headline with one amber line, plus a short paragraph
explaining the L-system. Top-right is a monospace meter stack showing the live
branch count, the current depth and the current value. Bottom is a glass tray with
three sliders — Depth (recursion 1–6), Angle (6–90°) and Decay (length falloff
40–90%) — plus Sway and Re-seed buttons, and a monospace hint.

Interaction: the reef grows and sways on its own. Depth, Angle and Decay reshape it
live — the branch count in the meter jumps as depth increases, since each level
roughly trebles the population. Sway freezes or resumes the breathing. Re-seed
throws away the trunks and grows a different arrangement. Dragging steers the
current and pushes the motes. On narrow screens the paragraph, meters and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A reef that draws itself: five to nine trunks on a wobbled seabed, each splitting
into two branches per level with a stroke width that tapers toward the tips and a
third shoot on a quarter of branches, all swaying on a generation-weighted sine
wave from root to tip, while a steerable current pushes seventy motes of scattered
light sideways through the water.

## Major visual techniques used
- Recursive L-system branching with angle, length decay and depth-limited
  stroke-width taper
- Deterministic third-shoot selection via a position hash (sin x·12.9898 +
  y·78.233 × 43758.5453) so the shape is stable frame to frame
- Generation-weighted sway: sin(t·0.7 + gen·1.3 + x·0.01) scaled by depth fraction,
  so tips move more than trunks
- Double-stroke tips: a wide coral-coloured stroke plus a narrower warm cream core
  at depth ≤ 2
- Sine-wobbled seabed filled with a vertical gradient
- Seventy motes in additive blend drifting on a per-mote phase and speed
- Eased current (0.03 lerp) accumulated from horizontal drag and clamped to ±1.4
- Root trunks seeded across the width with individual length and lean
- Live branch counter incremented inside the recursion
- Warm sun wash and teal depth wash as radial gradients over the sea
- Italic serif headline with one amber line against 8.5px tracked monospace
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A growing thing you can steer. Visitors watch the reef breathe, then change how
deep it branches, at what angle, and how fast it thins — watching the branch count
climb — freeze the sway to look at the drawing, re-seed for a different reef, or
drag sideways to push the current and the drifting motes through the water.
