Every district has its own clock. The market is loud at six and dead at two, the financial district is the other way round, and the park is only busy when both the weather and the daylight agree. Drag the hour and watch the whole shape change.

i

Each cell

The map is a grid and every cell holds one number between nought and a hundred. It is not drawn, it is coloured: the same palette, five stops, chosen by the value — which is what a real choropleth does.

ii

Five clocks

A district is not a colour, it is a curve. Each one has a different peak, a different width and a different baseline, and a park that is additionally suppressed by rain and cold.

iii

The event

Slide the event in and one district lights up while the others fall back slightly, which is what a real event does to a city: it does not raise the whole map, it moves people into one part of it.

iv

Find the peak

The button sweeps all twenty-four hours and all five districts and returns the busiest moment, so the peak is measured rather than asserted.

v

Weather

Rain and cold empty the streets, but not the financial district — indoors is indoors. Weather moves outdoor numbers only, which is the honest part.

vi

The day

Run the day and the hour advances on its own; every district changes every frame, and the map is a picture of a pattern rather than of a place.
