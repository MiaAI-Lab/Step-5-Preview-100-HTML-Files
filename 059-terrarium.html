A closed world you can keep

Light, water,
and nothing else

A sealed case has no soil delivery, no rain and no escape. What happens inside is the sum of what you gave it: how much light falls, how much moisture is held, and how many mouths there are to feed.

i

Water cycle

Moisture evapourates continuously and condenses on the colder glass. The droplets that form there grow, get heavy, and fall back to the soil — which is the whole rain system of a sealed case.

ii

Growth

Each plant grows toward the light at a rate set by both light and moisture. Yellow leaves mean the case is running dry; thick dark leaves mean it has everything it wants.

iii

Mouths

A fly eats, decays and is composted. Population follows the fruit supply, not a clock, so a case with too many mouths starves itself back down.

iv

Balance

Oxygen is the sum of what the leaves make minus what the mouths take. Watch the meter, not the plants: the meter is the honest number.

v

Brass

The frame is a gradient band sprayed across the top edge only — no image, no texture, just a linear gradient with three stops doing the work.

vi

Time

One second here is about a quarter of an hour in the case, and the day counter is what the whole simulation is measured against.
