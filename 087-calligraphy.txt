# BROAD NIB — pressure, angle, and one line of ink

## Short title
Calligraphy Studio

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a calligraphy sheet where every
stroke is drawn by a broad-nib rule — a chisel at an angle whose width depends on the
direction of travel — over real guidelines, with a second sheet that spells out the ductus.
No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS
libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive,
valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a desk with paper on it. Ground #f2efe6, sheet #fbf9f2, hairlines
rgba(42,38,32,.2), ink #2a2620 with muted #645c4e, navy #1f3f6b as the accent and
vermilion #8c3a2c for the ductus. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase
.2em–.4em kickers, monospace figures.

The stroke geometry is the whole page. A broad nib is a rectangle at an angle. Tracing it
along a path gives a width that is the full nib width when the travel runs across the
nib's edge and thins toward nothing when the travel runs along its axis, so drawing down
at a given angle is thick and drawing up is thin, automatically. The nib width function is
one line and the whole ductus falls out of it.

Six historical nibs, each setting an angle: foundational at 30, uncial and carolingian at
40, italic at 45, blackletter at 0 and copperplate at 52. Six inks — iron gall, walnut,
sepia, gouache, red lead, ultramarine — each its own colour and coverage. The nib itself is
drawn at the live pointer position, as the actual rectangle at the actual angle.

The sheet carries four guidelines — waist, x-height, base and descender — with the spacing
marks set by the nib width, because the pen you choose decides the size you write at.
Beneath the desk is a ductus sheet: six letters with their strokes drawn in order, and the
thick/thin rule drawn with the same nib function, so the demonstration and the drawing
share one arithmetic.

Controls: six nibs, six inks, nib width, nib angle, pressure, Guidelines and New sheet.
Readout: strokes, path length in millimetres, the family, the nib setting, the ink and the
ductus note.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the desk canvas with its caption, a dock of two chip rows,
three sliders and two buttons, the ductus sheet, a readout row, then two three-cell note
rows on three scripts, the ductus, pressure, guidelines, ink and fresh sheet, then a
footer.

Interaction: pick a nib and the angle changes, so the same drag writes a different script.
Draw on the sheet and watch the thick stroke go down and the thin stroke go up; move the
nib width and the guidelines re-space themselves; press harder to spread the nib; switch
ink to change the coverage; turn the guidelines off; and take a new sheet.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A calligraphy desk where every stroke is drawn by a broad-nib rule — a rectangle at an
angle whose width is the full nib across the direction of travel and thins to nothing
along it — over four real guidelines whose spacing is set by the nib width, with six
historical nibs from 30 to 52 degrees, six inks, a pressure that spreads the nib, the nib
drawn live at the pointer, and a second sheet that spells out the ductus of six letters and
the thick/thin rule using the same width function as the drawing.

## Major visual techniques used
- Broad nib as one function: width is the nib times one minus the absolute dot product
  of travel and nib axis, so thick and thin are geometry, not a style
- Nib angle as the single parameter that produces six historical scripts from one code
  path
- Nib drawn live at the pointer as the actual rotated rectangle, so the instrument is
  visible while you work
- Guidelines whose spacing is derived from the nib width, which is the real rule
- Ductus sheet drawn from the same width function as the desk, so the demonstration and
  the drawing cannot disagree
- Pressure multiplying the nib width, so a second route to the thick stroke exists
- Six inks as distinct colours and coverages, from opaque gouache to fugitive sepia
- Path length accumulated in pixels and reported in millimetres at 3.78 pixels per
  millimetre, so the number is a real measurement
- Point capture on the sheet so a stroke continues outside it
- Minimum travel distance of two pixels, so a stroke is not a forest of segments
- Strokes stored as point lists and re-rendered every frame, so the nib, angle and
  pressure apply retroactively to what you have already drawn
- New sheet clearing the writing and leaving the guidelines, because guides are not part
  of the text

## Intended interaction model
A sheet to write on. Visitors pick a nib, an ink and a width, draw on the sheet, and watch
the thick and thin strokes appear from the direction of travel alone — and can go back and
change the angle, the width or the pressure after drawing and watch every stroke already
on the sheet re-draw with it, because the writing is stored as points and rendered from
the nib's geometry every frame.
