# THE SCALE — one cone, five flavours, one scoop

## Short title
Ice Cream Counter

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a gelato counter where a scoop is
a foam with a real overrun, dragged out of a tub onto a digital scale that reads its actual
weight in grams. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external
images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking,
responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a warm counter. Ground #fff6ee, hairlines rgba(58,42,42,.18), ink
#3a2a2a with muted #7a6660, terracotta #e0654a as the accent and a teal #4a7a8c for the
figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers, monospace
figures.

The counter is one canvas: a cream gradient, a stainless tub with a rolled rim holding the
flavour, a digital scale with a dark display, a vertical freezer thermometer, and the scoop
itself. The scoop is a hemisphere with a curl across it — a radial-gradient body, a flat
band, a highlight ellipse, chips and ten air bubbles on the surface — and it can be dragged
out of the tub, with its position deciding how far out it is.

Eight flavours, each with a light tone, a dark tone, an optional chip colour and the mix's
real fat, sugar and protein per hundred grams. Four cone types, each drawn as a triangle
with a lattice and a rolled rim.

The physics is the overrun. Volume is the hemisphere's volume; mix volume is that divided
by one plus the overrun percentage; air volume is the remainder; and weight is mix volume
times the base density of 1.1 grams per millilitre. So the same scoop really does get
lighter as you whip more air into it, and the ticket and the scale both say so.

Controls: eight flavours, four cone types, scoop size, overrun, freezer temperature, Scoop
it and Onto a cone. Readout: flavour, weight, volume, overrun, temperature and cones
served. The ticket lists weight, volume, mix, air, overrun and price; the nutrition panel
shows fat, sugar and protein of the mix, which does not move with the overrun because it
is the mix's, not the foam's.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the counter canvas with its caption, a dock of two chip rows,
three sliders and two buttons, a two-column ticket and nutrition row, a readout row, then
two three-cell note rows on overrun, freezing, the scoop, the ticket, nutrition and cones,
then a footer.

Interaction: pick a flavour and the tub, the scoop and the bubbles all change; drag the
scoop out of the tub and watch the scale read it; set the overrun and watch the same scoop
weigh less and less; set the freezer colder than minus sixteen and the display turns amber
to say it is a brick; and drop it onto a cone to serve one.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A gelato counter on canvas where a hemisphere scoop with a curl, chips and visible air
bubbles sits in a stainless tub of one of eight flavours and drags out onto a digital scale
— with volume from the hemisphere, mix volume from the overrun, weight from mix volume
times the base density, and a ticket and nutrition panel computed from the same numbers, a
freezer thermometer that turns the display amber past minus sixteen, and four cone types
drawn with a lattice and rolled rim.

## Major visual techniques used
- Scoop as a radial-gradient hemisphere with a flat curl band and a highlight ellipse,
  which is what a scoop actually looks like from above
- Air bubbles drawn on the surface, so the foam is visible rather than described
- Chips as a golden-angle scatter clipped to the surface, per flavour that has them
- Overrun as a real division: mix volume is total volume over one plus overrun, so
  weight and the ticket follow from one number
- Nutrition bars driven by the mix's composition, which deliberately does not change with
  the overrun — only the weight does
- Digital scale as a dark display with a monospace readout that turns amber when the
  freezer is too cold to scoop
- Freezer thermometer as a vertical column with the scoopable band marked in green
- Tub as concentric ellipses with a rolled rim, and a flavour label on the front
- Cone as a triangle with two families of lattice strokes converging and a rolled rim
- Drag state derived from the scoop's distance from the tub centre, so picking it up is a
  continuous quantity rather than a toggle
- Scoop radius eased toward the slider, so changing size is smooth
- Eight palettes as light/dark pairs so a flavour change repaints the scoop, the tub and
  the chips from one switch

## Intended interaction model
A counter to work. Visitors choose a flavour and a cone, drag the scoop out of the tub,
set the overrun and watch the same volume weigh less, push the freezer down until the
display says it is a brick, and drop it onto a cone to serve one. The ticket and the
nutrition panel are computed from the same two numbers that move the drawing.
