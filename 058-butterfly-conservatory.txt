# CONSERVATORY — butterflies drawn from parameters

## Short title
Butterfly Conservatory

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a specimen case of six
butterflies, all of them vector, all of them flapping at their own beat. No external
dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries.
All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid
HTML5.

Artistic direction: a cream specimen drawer. Ground #f6f1e4, cards #fbf7ec, hairlines
rgba(34,38,46,.18), ink #22262e with muted #5c5749, cinnabar #c8553d for italics and
active controls and gold #b98a2f for the roman-numeral figures. Serif headings, genus
in italics, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers.

The insects: six species parameters (scale, wingbeat, tail, base colour, band colour,
eyespot, vein colour, tags) drive an SVG builder. Each wing is a cubic path whose
control points are multiplied by the species scale, the hindwing first so the forewing
overlaps it, mirrored by scale(-1,1), with the body, head and antennae drawn over the
top. Bands and eyespots are drawn on top of the wing and then clipped by it, so they
always follow the outline instead of floating beside it.

Wingbeat: each SVG carries its own beat as a CSS custom property and runs a flap
keyframe that squashes it horizontally on a perspective frame — the same motion a
spread wing makes when seen from above. Hovering a card pauses its wings and opens them
full width.

Three toggles: Venation reveals a hidden group of five vein strokes per wing side,
clipped to the forewing and left out of the initial render; Hold still pauses every
card's animation; Release lifts each card out of the grid into a fixed, frameless state
and drifts them on a slow sinusoid with a small rotation, wrapping at the viewport
edges. Releasing again parks them back where they were.

Layout: nav with serif brand, centred hero with cinnabar kicker and a gold italic word,
a rules row naming the collection with the three toggles, a three/two/one-column grid of
specimen cards, then two three-cell rows of notes (wings, bands, eyespots, beat, host,
paper) with gold roman numerals, then a ruled footer. Responsive at 900, 820, 600 and
780px.

Interaction: read the case, hover any specimen to stop its wings and see them open,
turn on Venation to expose the vein structure, Hold still to freeze every card, and
Release to let the collection leave the drawer and wander the window. Nothing is loaded
from anywhere and nothing is an image.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A cream specimen drawer holding six butterflies whose bodies, wings, bands, eyespots and
wingbeats are all generated from per-species parameters in SVG — each wing a cubic path
scaled by its species factor, each band and eyespot clipped by the wing so it follows the
outline, each insect flapping at its own rate until you hover it, turn on its venation,
hold the whole case still, or release the collection to drift around the window.

## Major visual techniques used
- Wing silhouettes from cubic paths whose control points are multiplied by a species
  scale, so one curve family produces six different shapes
- Hindwing drawn before forewing so the overlap reads as depth, then mirrored with
  scale(-1,1) rather than duplicating coordinates
- Bands and eyespots drawn through the wing and clipped by it, so pattern always
  tracks the outline
- Eyespots as concentric rings plus a pale pupil, offset toward the hindwing margin
- Wing gradient built from a white highlight into the base colour into the band colour
- Venation as a second clipped group rendered at opacity 0 and revealed on demand
- Flap animation as a horizontal squash on a perspective frame, which is the true
  top-down projection of a spreading wing
- Per-card wingbeat written as a CSS custom property and consumed by the animation
  duration, so no two insects share a rhythm
- aria-pressed used as the state of record for all three toggles
- Release mode that measures each card, re-anchors it at the origin and drives it with
  transform, then restores it by taking the same class off
- Drift along a sinusoid with a small rotation and viewport-edge wrapping
- A paper palette with hairlines and italics instead of any dark-ground treatment

## Intended interaction model
A drawer you open and play with. Visitors read the cards, hover a specimen to hold its
wings open, switch Venation on to see the vein structure underneath, Hold still to
freeze the case, and Release to let the butterflies wander the window until they are
recalled. Every specimen is drawn from numbers, so changing a parameter would change
the insect rather than swapping a picture.
