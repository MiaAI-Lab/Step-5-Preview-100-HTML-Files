# SPIRAL — A Galaxy, Set Turning

## Short title
Spiral Galaxy

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive spiral
galaxy model called SPIRAL. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web
fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries. All CSS
and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: deep space, mostly dark. A CSS void (#03040e → #05061a →
#02030a) with a violet glow at the disc's centre and a pink bloom high right. On
the canvas, a wide two-stop halo sits over the whole disc; a warm core bulge —
a four-stop radial from warm white through amber to transparent, squashed vertically
by the tilt — sits at the centre; and up to three thousand stars are drawn in
additive blend.

The galaxy's arms are a true logarithmic spiral. Each star carries a fraction f
from core to rim, an arm index, and a spread offset. Its angle is
log(radius)/winding + armOffset + spread + time, which is the actual equation of a
logarithmic spiral, so the Winding slider genuinely changes how tightly the arms
curl. Each star's radius uses a power curve so stars cluster toward the rim, and
crucially the rotation term is the same for every radius — a flat rotation curve,
which is what real galaxies do, and which is why the arms wind tighter with distance
while the material turns together. One hundred and sixty dust particles are drawn
in multiply blend as elongated ellipses inside the arms, so the lanes read as dark
rather than bright, and they rotate at a slower rate than the stars which produces
a genuine shear between the light and the dust.

Each star's hue is chosen from three (a blue-white, a cooler blue, and a warm amber
— thirty per cent of stars, so the arms have a colour gradient), its size from a
random range, its alpha from its distance from the core, its twinkle from its own
phase and speed, and the brighter stars get a five-times-radius halo gradient.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas over the void with a floating UI overlay whose
pointer-events pass through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace model
label above an italic serif headline with one line in warm amber, plus a short
paragraph about density waves. Top-right is a monospace meter stack showing star
count, arm count and rotation speed in km/s. Bottom is a glass tray with four
sliders — Spin (0–100), Arms (1–5), Winding (10–90) and Stars (200–3000) — plus a
Dust toggle and a Core burst button, and a monospace hint.

Interaction: dragging vertically tilts the disc between 0.05 (edge-on) and 1.0
(face-on), easing toward the target, which is the single most dramatic control —
the galaxy opens from a line to a full disc. Scrolling zooms between 0.5× and 2.6×.
The four sliders reshape it live: more arms, tighter winding, more stars, faster
spin. Dust toggles the dark lanes off. Core burst flashes a decaying warm wash over
the body. On narrow screens the paragraph, meters and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A galaxy that obeys the real equation: up to three thousand stars placed on a
logarithmic spiral (angle = log(r)/winding) with a flat rotation curve so the arms
wind tighter outward while the material turns together, one hundred and sixty dark
dust lanes in multiply blend rotating slower than the stars to produce genuine
shear, a warm squashed core bulge, and three star colours — tiltable from edge-on to
face-on and zoomable.

## Major visual techniques used
- True logarithmic spiral geometry: angle = ln(radius)/winding + armOffset + spread
- Flat rotation curve — the same rotation term at every radius, as in real galaxies
- One to five arms via a shared angular offset of 2π/armCount
- Power-curve radius distribution so stars cluster toward the rim
- Additive blending ('lighter') for stars and halos so overlaps bloom
- Multiply blending for dust lanes so they read as dark, not light
- Slower dust rotation than star rotation, producing real shear between light and dust
- Per-star hue from three values (blue-white, cool blue, warm amber at 30%) giving a
  colour gradient across the arms
- Per-star twinkle phase and speed, and alpha from distance to the core
- Five-times-radius halo gradients on the brightest stars
- Core bulge as a four-stop radial squashed vertically by the tilt factor
- Wide two-stop disc halo behind everything
- Vertical drag tilt from 0.05 (edge-on) to 1.0 (face-on), eased toward target
- Wheel zoom clamped between 0.5× and 2.6×
- Decaying core-burst flash driven by an interval that writes to the body background
- Live star count, arm count and derived rotation speed
- Italic serif headline with one amber line against 8.5px tracked monospace
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A galaxy you can turn over. Visitors drag vertically to tip the disc between
edge-on and face-on — the one gesture that transforms the page — and scroll to
zoom. The four sliders reshape it live: more or fewer arms, tighter or looser
winding, more or fewer stars, faster or slower spin. Dust toggles the dark lanes,
and Core burst flashes the nucleus. Everything recomputes from data each frame.
