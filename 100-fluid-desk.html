Six fluids, one page

A desk of them, each in its own tray, each running the equation that actually describes it. Drag in the tray and the fluid answers.

Ink diffuses and curls by solving the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on a grid: advect, add forces, project out the divergence, diffuse. A smoke plume adds buoyancy, so hot rises and a vortex street forms behind an obstacle. Watercolour bleed is the same diffusion with a pigment-loaded field and granulation where the edge dries. Ferrofluid solves magnetic dipoles in a container and spikes them along the field. Lava lamp ink is a buoyant blob in a warmer fluid. A soap bubble is a thin film minimising its area under pressure difference.

A drop of ink in still water: the velocity and dye fields are both advected, and the pressure projection keeps the flow divergence free.

i

Ink diffusion

Velocity and dye advected on a staggered-free grid, forces splatted by the pointer, then a Jacobi pressure projection removes the divergence. Dissipation is the friction you can see.

ii

Smoke plume

The same solver with a buoyancy term and a fixed temperature: hot cells rise, cold sink, and a vortex street sheds behind anything in the way. Turbulence is not drawn, it is what the equations give you.

iii

Watercolour bleed

Pigment concentrations for three inks, each with its own diffusion rate and granulation. Edge darkening appears because the paper dries at the border and the pigment stops there.

iv

Magnetic ferrofluid

A container of dipoles and a magnet you move with the pointer. The fluid spikes along the field because that is the shape that lowers the magnetic energy, and the Rosensweig instability decides when a spike is allowed.

v

Lava lamp ink

A buoyant blob in a slightly warmer fluid: heated ink rises, cools at the top, falls, and meets the rising column. The period is set by the Rayleigh number.

vi

Soap film

A bubble is a thin film minimising its area with a pressure difference across it. Young–Laplace says small bubbles push harder, so they shrink and the big one grows, until only one is left.
