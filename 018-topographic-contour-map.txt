# CONTOUR — A Survey of Imaginary Ground

## Short title
Topographic Contour Map

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive
topographic survey map of imaginary ground called CONTOUR. No external dependencies
whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no
JavaScript libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by
double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a printed Ordnance Survey sheet. Warm paper #efe9dc, deep
green-black ink #1d2b24, teal #0f766e for one italic word in the title and the
slider values, and rust #b45309 for the sheet label, summit triangles and hover
readout. Everything is linework on paper: an 80px graticule at 8% ink, isolines in
two weights, and small triangles marking named summits with their heights.

The terrain: a heightfield sampled into a Float64Array on a 4-pixel grid, computed
from five stacked sine waves at different frequencies, amplitudes and directions
(so the ground has ridges running both ways plus fine detail), plus two Gaussian
bumps placed at fixed screen-relative positions so the map always has a proper
summit with a marker. The seed offsets every sine phase, so "New ground" produces
genuinely different country.

The rendering: a full marching-squares isoline tracer. For each 4×4 sampling cell,
the four corner heights are compared against a level to build a 4-bit case index,
and the appropriate edge segments are emitted with linear interpolation along the
cell edges. Every fifth level is a major contour at 62% ink and 1.3px; the rest are
minor at 26% ink and 0.8px. The contour interval slider re-derives the level list,
and the level count and segment total are reported in the stats stack.

A "Shading" toggle adds real hillshading: it reads the canvas back with
getImageData, computes per-cell gradients from the heightfield in x and y, builds a
surface normal with a relief-scaled z, takes a dot product against a north-west
light, and darkens each sampled pixel by (0.62 + lambert × 0.55) — so slopes facing
the light brighten and slopes facing away sink into shadow, over the whole sheet.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas with a floating UI overlay whose pointer-events pass
through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace sheet label ("Sheet 14 · grid
NH42 · scale 1:24000") above a serif headline with one italic teal word, plus a
paragraph explaining the method. Top-right is a monospace stats stack showing the
contour interval, the number of isoline segments drawn, and the total elevation
range in metres. Bottom is a glass tray with three sliders — Ridge (sine frequency
1–9), Relief (amplitude 10–100) and Interval (contour spacing 5–60 m) — plus "New
ground" and "Shading" buttons, and a monospace hint.

Interaction: hovering the sheet draws a rust crosshair through the cursor and prints
the elevation at that point in metres, computed live from the same height function.
The Ridge and Relief sliders rebuild the heightfield and re-trace every isoline; the
Interval slider re-derives the level list. "New ground" reseeds everything. "Shading"
toggles the hillshade pass and its button fills teal when active. On narrow screens
the paragraph, stats and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A printed survey sheet of country that does not exist: a layered-sine heightfield
sampled on a 4px grid and traced as marching-squares isolines with indexed major
contours every fifth line, two named summits with heights, an optional real
hillshade pass, and a crosshair that reads the elevation under your cursor.

## Major visual techniques used
- Heightfield built from five stacked sine waves plus two Gaussian peaks, seeded by
  a single phase offset
- Marching-squares contour tracing with a 4-bit case index and linear interpolation
  along cell edges
- Two-tier contour styling: indexed major lines (62% ink, 1.3px) and minor lines
  (26% ink, 0.8px) every fifth level
- Float64Array grid sampling on a 4px lattice for speed
- Real hillshading via getImageData: per-cell dz/dx and dz/dy, a relief-scaled
  surface normal, and a Lambert term against a north-west light
- 80px graticule at 8% ink for the printed-grid feel
- Hover crosshair with a live elevation readout from the same height function
- Summit triangles with heights placed on the Gaussian peaks
- Live segment count and computed elevation range in the stats stack
- Monospace sheet metadata, serif headline with one italic teal word, and rust
  accents
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A survey instrument. Visitors move the pointer to read elevations anywhere on the
sheet, then reshape the country with the Ridge and Relief sliders, change how finely
it is contoured with the Interval slider, ask for a completely different landscape
with "New ground", and turn the hillshading on to see the same country in light and
shadow. The map is entirely redrawn from data on every change.
