# TYPEWRITER — Studio 14, 1968

## Short title
Studio 14 Typewriter

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a working typewriter for a
fictional 1968 scriptwriting office, Studio 14. No external dependencies whatsoever —
no CDN, no web fonts, no external images (except the inline data-URI grain filter),
no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking,
responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: warm off-white paper and black ink, with one red accent used
sparingly. Paper #f6f2e6 at 2 line-height, ink #211d18, red #a8281f. The typewriter
body is a light grey metal gradient (#dcd7cd to #bdb8ad) with a long soft shadow so
it sits on the desk, and an inset top highlight like a rim catching the lamp. The
paper sheet is fixed top and bottom with a grain overlay from an inline
SVG feTurbulence data-URI at 35%.

The keys are generated by JavaScript from a three-row QWERTY layout, so the keyboard
is data, not markup. Each keycap is a radial gradient with a hard 2px drop shadow
beneath it, giving it thickness; when pressed (click or physical key) it
translateY(2px), loses its hard shadow and gains an inset dark shadow — it actually
travels into the body. Space is a six-column bar; shift and return are two-column
wide bars. Two letters (R and T) are struck in red, as if the ribbon had just turned.

A blinking Caret-like underscore sits at the end of the text, animation a 1.05s
steps(1) blink. Each keystroke briefly rings the paper with a red inset outline — a
type-bell flash — that clears after 120ms.

Layout: nav with serif brand. Centred hero with red eyebrow and an italic red word.
Then the machine: paper, platen roll, keyboard. Then three ghost/solid buttons
("Type the opening", "New sheet", "Smudge a line"). Then a three-cell notes row with
large red serif figures (72 characters across, 1 ribbon, 4 copies). Then a ruled
monospace footer.

Interaction: the physical keyboard types. Single characters strike the matching keycap
and append to the page in uppercase (a real typewriter has one case per key).
Backspace rubs out the last character. The keycap row is clickable too. "Type the
opening" types out a passage one letter at a time at 110ms, pressing each keycap as
it goes, and its label becomes "Stop typing" while running. "New sheet" clears.
"Smudge a line" duplicates a nine-character slice in red as a struck-over correction.
The keyboard grid drops from 11 to 7 columns at 820px and 5 at 520px.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A 1968 scriptwriting office machine on a warm desk: three generated QWERTY rows of
thick radial-gradient keycaps that visibly travel two pixels into the grey body when
struck — by click or by the real keyboard — adding uppercase letters to a grainy
sheet of paper with a blinking caret and a red type-bell flash on every strike, plus
a self-typing opening passage, a new sheet, and a red smudge-over correction.

## Major visual techniques used
- Keycap roundness: radial-gradient disc with a hard 2px bottom shadow, removing it
  and adding an inset dark shadow to sell travel depth
- Keyboard generated from a three-row string layout (QWERTYUIOP / ASDFGHJKL /
  ZXCVBNM) in JS
- Physical keyboard to on-screen keycap pairing through a character map
- Paper grain from an inline SVG feTurbulence data-URI
- Caret blink using steps(1) at 1.05s so it clicks rather than fades
- Red type-bell flash: a transient inset outline on the paper, cleared by a 120ms
  timeout
- Metal body from a vertical grey gradient with an inset top rim light and a long
  soft drop shadow
- 11-column keyboard grid dropping to 7 at 820px and 5 at 520px
- Two-coloured "ribbon" hinted at with two red keycaps
- Smudge-over correction reusing an existing slice of the text in red
- Warm off-white palette, black ink, single red accent
- Self-typing demo with a button that rewords itself and stops the interval

## Intended interaction model
A page that types back. Visitors strike the on-screen keycaps or just use their real
keyboard, watching each cap travel and the paper take uppercase letters with a red
bell flash. Backspace rubs out a character. The three buttons offer a guided
self-typing opening, a fresh sheet, and a deliberate over-correction in red.
