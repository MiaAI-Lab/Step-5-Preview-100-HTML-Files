Six wheels, one spring

The whole time is
held in brass teeth

Nothing here is a picture of a clock. Every wheel on that plate is the right size for its neighbour, turns at exactly the ratio its tooth count demands, and is driven by a balance wheel that is only ever two impulses from stopping.

Numéroté · 1908

Wheels

01

The train

Six wheels roll in pairs. Two meshed gears turn against each other at the ratio of their teeth, so a 42-tooth driven by a 14 loses exactly a third of the speed and gains three times the moment.

02

Escapement

The balance wheel swings on a hairspring and gives the escape wheel one push each way. Its amplitude is the only thing keeping the train honest, and it is the number a watchmaker reads first.

03

Hands

Motion work divides again at the centre: twelve to one for the hour hand, sixty for the minutes, and the small seconds that quietly makes the arithmetic visible.

04

Jewels

Every pivot ends in a synthetic ruby. It is not decoration — polished corundum holds its shape where brass would wear an oval hole in a decade.

05

Torque

A mainspring does not push evenly, so the dial starts strong and fades through the day. The drum teeth are sized to make that curve survivable.

06

Case back

Open it and you get the maker's rosette. On a real watch that surface is engine-turned by hand, one pass of the rose engine per ring.
