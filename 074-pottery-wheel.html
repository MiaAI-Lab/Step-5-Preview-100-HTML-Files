i

The profile

A pot is stored here as a list of radii at even heights, which is exactly what a turner reads off the wheel. Drag any point and the vessel changes at that height and nowhere else.

ii

The revolution

The drawing is an ellipsed silhouette with a specular highlight on the left and a shaded falloff to the right, plus a shadowed interior visible inside the mouth. No mesh, no projection — just the projection a pot actually makes.

iii

Wall

The interior is the profile pulled inward by the wall thickness, so a thick-walled pot has little room and a thin one has a lot. That is the whole relationship between volume and weight.

iv

Glaze

Five glazes with real behaviour: tenmoku breaks to brown on the edges, celadon greens in reduction, shino blisters, and a clear glaze that just shows the clay. Firing deepens all of them.

v

Firing

Cone 06 to cone 10. A low firing leaves a pot porous and bright; a high one makes it dense and dark. The slider moves the whole palette one way.

vi

Collapse

Go too far and the wall gives: the profile sags, the vessel loses its neck and becomes a puddle. Every potter has done this and every potter has done it by going too far.
