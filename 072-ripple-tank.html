There is no animation here and no curve. The surface is a grid of numbers, and each frame every one of them is replaced by the average of its neighbours, which is the shortest honest description of a wave.

i

The update

New height is the mean of the four neighbours, minus where it was. Two arrays, one swap, no trigonometry — and yet the result refracts through a gap and interferes with itself, because that is what the equation means.

ii

Damping

Nothing here is lossless. Multiply by a factor just under one and the ripples fade; set it to exactly one and they never stop, which is the difference between a tank and a perfect simulation of one.

iii

Interference

Two sources beating against each other make a pattern of lines, not a mess. The spacing of those lines is set by the wavelength and the distance between the sources, and nothing else.

iv

Edges

The walls reflect, so the tank behaves like a basin of a fixed size. That is why a single drop becomes a whole pattern rather than one expanding ring.

v

Shading

Colour is not the height. It is the difference between neighbours — the slope — so the picture reads as a lit surface with a small specular highlight, which is what makes a flat grid look like water.

vi

Two sources

Add a second and the pattern changes everywhere at once, including between them. The lines that appear are the directions where the two wave trains arrive in step.
