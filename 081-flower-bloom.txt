# BLOOM — a flower that opens petal by petal

## Short title
Flower Bloom

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a flower built from real
phyllotaxis — primordia placed at a fixed divergence angle — with the angle adjustable so
the packing can be seen to work or fail. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no
web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works
offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a garden border. Ground #f7f1ee, hairlines rgba(51,39,42,.18), ink
#33272a with muted #6d5c60, rose #c4596b as the accent and a leaf green #4d7a54. Serif
headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers, monospace figures.

The flower is one canvas: a gradient ground, a shadowed head, a bezier stem with two
gradient leaves on it, and then the head itself. The head is a stack of primordia, each at
an angular distance of the divergence angle from the last, whose radius is the square root
of its index over the total — the Vogel spiral, which is the real distribution a plant
uses. Each primordium is drawn as an ellipse rotated along the radius, with a linear
gradient from tip to base and a vein down the middle, shaded by its order so the oldest are
darker at the centre and the youngest palest at the rim.

Five species with their own palette and primordium count — sunflower, rose, daisy, poppy,
lavender — and the two with ray florets get a rotating ring of cream petals outside the
disc. The disc is a radial gradient with forty seeds laid on the same spiral, which is what
a sunflower's seeds actually do.

Controls: five species swatches, a divergence slider from 90 to 190 degrees, petal size, an
open slider, Grow it (which adds primordia one per frame) and Back to a bud. Readout:
primordia placed, the same number expressed as whorls of five, coverage — the fraction of
petals whose centres land within a petal width of an earlier one — the current divergence
angle, the head species and the opening percentage.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the bloom canvas with its caption, a dock of swatches, three
sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on the golden angle,
symmetry, opening, coverage, growth and colour, then a footer.

Interaction: the head is already full. Visitors drag the divergence off 137.5 and watch the
petals line up into spokes and holes; drag it back and the packing closes; change the petal
size to see how many a head can hold; open and close it; take it back to a bud and grow it
again primordium by primordium; and switch between five species.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A flower on canvas whose head is a Vogel spiral of primordia, each placed at the divergence
angle from the last and at a radius from the square root of its index, drawn as gradient
ellipses shaded by order over a bezier stem with two leaves and a disc of seeds on the same
spiral — with five species palettes, a divergence slider from 90 to 190 degrees that visibly
breaks and repairs the packing, petal size and opening sliders, a grow mode that adds
primordia one per frame, and a coverage figure that measures how well the head packs.

## Major visual techniques used
- Vogel spiral: radius as the square root of index over total, which is the real
  distribution and the reason the packing is optimal at the golden angle
- Divergence angle as the single parameter that produces spokes at rational angles and
  perfect packing at 137.5
- Petals rotated along their own radius, so the whole head reads as a head seen from above
- Per-petal gradient from tip to base with a vein, shaded by order so age is visible
- Coverage measured geometrically: the fraction of petals whose centres fall within a
  petal width of an earlier one
- Ray florets as a ring that rotates slowly, only for the species that have them
- Disc seeds placed on the same spiral as the petals, which is what real seeds do
- Stem as a single bezier with two leaves placed on it from their own offset
- Shadow behind the head offset down and right, matching a light from the upper left
- Grow mode adding one primordium per frame so the pattern can be watched falling into
  place and then breaking when the angle is moved mid-growth
- Five palettes seeded from real flower colours, each driving two petal tones and a core
- Whorls as the primordium count divided by five, so the number reads in botanical terms

## Intended interaction model
A head to open and to break. Visitors drag the divergence angle and watch the packing fall
apart into spokes and close again at the golden angle, set the petal size and the opening,
take the flower back to a bud and grow it primordium by primordium, and switch between five
species. The coverage figure tells you what your eyes already worked out.
