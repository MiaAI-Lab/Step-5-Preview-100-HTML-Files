# THE FLUTE — bubbles that are not round

## Short title
Champagne Bubbles

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a flute of sparkling wine where
every bubble rises at its own terminal velocity, wobbles under drag, carries a wake, and
pops at the surface — with four glass shapes and the temperature in the equations. No
external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS
libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive,
valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a dark cellar. Ground #0f0c06, hairlines rgba(244,234,218,.14, body
#f4eada with muted #9c927c, pale gold #e8cf8a as the accent and a deep amber #e0b060 for
the figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers,
monospace figures.

The glass is four shapes — flute, coupe, tulip and white wine — each with its own rim
radius, base radius and height, drawn as a trapezoid of wine behind a translucent glass
outline, with a stem and foot. The wine's colour is the cuvée's own, from the pale straw of
a blanc de blancs to the pink of a rosé.

Bubbles nucleate at etched points along the inside wall and rise at a terminal velocity
proportional to the square of their radius, which is why the big ones are always the ones
you notice. As they rise they wobble, and the wobble amplitude grows with size — so a large
bubble is drawn as an ellipse that squashes and rotates with a white highlight on its
leading face, which is exactly how a rising bubble is not a sphere. A big bubble also sheds
a wake: a scatter of small bubbles that rise and fade beneath it.

The Reynolds number is computed for the mean bubble from its radius, its velocity and the
kinematic viscosity of water at the current temperature, so raising the temperature really
does drop Reynolds. The dissolved gas volume falls to zero when it is flat, and the
pressure readout goes from six atmospheres to one.

Controls: five cuvées, four glasses, effervescence, temperature, etched points, Top up and
Let it go flat. Readout: bubbles rising, mean bubble size, Reynolds, dissolved gas in litres
per litre, state and pressure.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the glass canvas with its caption, a dock of two chip rows,
three sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on nucleation,
the wobble, pressure, the flute, temperature and flat, then a footer.

Interaction: the flute is already fizzing. Visitors pull the effervescence down and the
chains thin out, raise the temperature and the bubbles grow and Reynolds drops, change the
glass and the whole column changes shape, pour a fresh flute for a burst, and let it go flat
until the chains stop and the glass is only wine.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A flute of sparkling wine on canvas where bubbles nucleate at etched points on the wall and
rise at a terminal velocity proportional to the square of their radius, wobbling as they go
with an amplitude that grows with size so a large bubble is drawn as a rotating squashed
ellipse with a leading-edge highlight, shedding a scatter of small wake bubbles beneath it —
across four glass shapes drawn as trapezoids of cuvée-coloured wine behind a translucent
outline, with a Reynolds number computed from radius, velocity and the temperature's
kinematic viscosity, a dissolved-gas volume that falls to zero when flat, and a pressure
that goes from six atmospheres to one.

## Major visual techniques used
- Terminal velocity proportional to radius squared, so the size of a bubble and its speed
  are one relationship
- Wobble amplitude as a function of radius, so the big bubbles are the non-spherical ones
- Bubble drawn as a rotated, squashed ellipse with a white highlight on the leading face,
  which is the actual shape of a rising bubble
- Wake as a scatter of small bubbles spawned under large ones, rising and fading
- Four glass shapes as rim radius, base radius and height, drawn as a wine trapezoid
  behind a translucent outline
- Reynolds computed from the mean bubble's radius, its velocity and the kinematic
  viscosity of water at the set temperature
- Temperature in the equations twice: in the nucleation rate and in the viscosity
- Dissolved gas as a volume that only reaches zero when flat
- Pressure readout derived from the effervescence, dropping to one atmosphere when flat
- Wine gradient across the glass in the cuvée's own hue
- Top up as an immediate burst of bubbles inside the glass
- Etched points drawn on the wall as the origin every chain comes from

## Intended interaction model
A glass to watch. The flute is already fizzing; visitors pull the effervescence down until
the chains thin, raise the temperature and watch the bubbles grow and Reynolds fall, switch
glass shape and see the column change, pour a fresh flute for a burst, and let it go flat
until the chains stop and the glass is only wine. Every number in the panel is computed from
the same bubbles the drawing uses.
