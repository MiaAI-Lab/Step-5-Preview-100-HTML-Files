# TECTONICS — The Plates, Drawn Slowly

## Short title
Tectonic Plates

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a planetary tectonic
plate simulation called TECTONICS. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN,
no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries.
All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid
HTML5.

Artistic direction: a cross-section through a living planet. Behind the canvas, a
night-purple room with a warm mantle glow at the bottom edge and a cool violet wash
at the top left. On the canvas, the mantle is a radial gradient from molten orange
#ff8a3d through #c8442a and #5a1a2a into dark #1a0a1e, drawn at 1.2 times the plate
radius. Seven convection bands rotate through it at different rates, each a chain of
forty warm dots whose alpha pulses on a triple-sine, scaled by the Plumes slider. On
top of that, five mantle plumes — radial warm glows whose height breathes on a sine,
also scaled by the slider — sit at their own polar positions.

Each plate is a dark slab: a sixteen-point noisy polar blob filled with a diagonal
gradient in its own hue (180–300), stroked with either an amber rim (a ridge, where
the plate is pulling apart) or a violet rim (a trench, where it is subducting). The
ridges get a second wide additive stroke so they read as hot, glowing seams. Plates
drift on their own velocities scaled by the Speed slider, reverse direction when they
leave the mantle, and each carries a slow self-rotation term. Rings of warm light
(a quakes list) pop at random plate positions, expand and fade.

Clicking near a plate splits it: a smaller sibling blob is pushed out with reversed
velocity and the opposite boundary type, the original flips its boundary type, and a
quake ring fires immediately — so the visitor can genuinely fracture the planet.
Dragging horizontally applies a slow accumulating rotation offset to the hand-panned
sphere.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas over the room gradient with a floating UI overlay
whose pointer-events pass through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace
label above an italic serif headline with one line in ridge amber, plus a short
paragraph. Top-right is a monospace meter stack showing plate count, ridge count
and the current drift rate in cm/year. Bottom is a glass tray with three sliders
(Speed, Plumes, Plates 5–18), a Quakes toggle and a Reset button, plus a monospace
hint.

Interaction: the plates drift continuously. Speed scales all velocities and the
quake rate; Plumes scales the convection bands and the plume height; Plates adds or
removes plates live (each new plate generated with its own noisy blob, hue and
boundary type). Clicking near a plate splits it. Dragging spins the view. Quakes can
be switched off. On narrow screens the paragraph, meters and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A molten cross-section of a planet: seven rotating convection bands and five
breathing mantle plumes over a hot radial gradient, with up to eighteen dark
violet-and-orange drifting plates whose rims glow amber at ridges and violet at
trenches, popping quake rings as they move — fractureable by clicking, and retunable
live through speed, plume and plate-count sliders.

## Major visual techniques used
- Mantle sphere as a four-stop radial gradient from molten orange to near-black,
  drawn at 1.2× plate radius
- Seven convection bands, each a chain of dots on an ellipse, rotating at different
  rates with triple-sine alpha pulsing
- Five mantle plumes as breathing radial gradients on individual phases
- Noisy polar blob plates: sixteen points with random radius, filled with a diagonal
  gradient in their own hue
- Two boundary types: amber ridges with an extra wide additive stroke for heat,
  violet trench rims
- Drift with boundary reversal when a plate leaves the mantle, plus a slow
  per-plate self-rotation
- Quake rings: expanding circles whose life decays, spawned at random plate positions
- Plate splitting on click: smaller sibling with reversed velocity, opposite boundary
  type, original flips its boundary type
- Live plate count, ridge count and derived drift rate (speed × 240 cm/yr)
- Additive compositing for convection, plumes and quakes
- Italic serif headline with one amber line against 8.5px tracked monospace
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A planet you can fracture. Visitors watch the plates drift and the plumes breathe,
then click near one to split it apart and generate a new boundary, use the three
sliders to change how fast the world turns, how much heat it has and how many plates
it has, and drag to spin the view. Quakes and Reset give control over the
bookkeeping. The simulation runs continuously whether or not anyone touches it.
