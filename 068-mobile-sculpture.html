Nothing here is animated by a timeline. Every arm is a pendulum with its own period and its own damping, and the whole piece only settles when the air does. Touch a shape and give it something to recover from.

i

The pivot

Each arm hangs from the end of the one above it and turns on its own axis, so the balance of the whole depends on where every weight sits along its parent. Nothing is independently animated.

ii

The period

A longer arm swings slower, which is why a mobile reads as a set of unrelated rhythms rather than one. The gravity slider is what all of them are dividing by.

iii

The air

A current pushes each arm at its own frequency, so the piece never repeats a pose. Damping is what would eventually stop it — and why still air makes it die.

iv

Balance

Real mobiles are balanced by moving weights until each arm hangs level. Here the offsets are chosen and then disturbed, which is the honest version: it only balances until something touches it.

v

Colour

Sheet metal in four muted primaries — vermilion, ultramarine, ochre and black — on cream. Calder's own palette, and the only saturated thing in the room.

vi

The touch

Click or drag a shape and you add angular momentum to its arm, which then propagates down the branch. It will not come back exactly where it was, and neither will you.
