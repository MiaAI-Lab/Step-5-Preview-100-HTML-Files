i

Lindenmayer

An L-system rewrites a string of symbols over and over, and the string is then read as a turtle: F goes forward, + turns left, [ and ] push and pop. The fractal trees, the dragon curve and every other recursive drawing is this.

ii

Ohno's phyllotaxis

Each new leaf sits at the golden angle, 137.5077°, from the one before, and turned outward by the square root of its index. That is why the florets of a sunflower spiral in sunflowers' pairs, and why the eye never quite closes.

iii

de Reffye's model

A bud is a small stochastic machine: at each moment it either waits, becomes a flower, sleeps, or produces a lateral shoot — with probabilities set by its age. Grow ten thousand and you have a forest that is not the same twice.

iv

Space colonisation

Runions scattered attraction points around a seed and let every branch tip grow toward the few it can see; the branches kill on contact. The result is the architecture of a real tree, because it is solving the same problem.

v

Weber and Penn

A tree is specified by a handful of parameters — trunk taper, branch levels, apex angle and split ratio — and the recursion draws each level. Tree a is a fir; tree b is a broadleaf; the parameters are the difference.

vi

Honda and Palmer

Honda's symmetric binary drawing, and Palmer's annual-shingle oak and simple willow: each a balance of a self-similar model, each a letter to the same family.
