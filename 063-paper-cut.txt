# PAPER CUT — a landscape in six layers

## Short title
Paper Cut

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a shadow-box landscape built
from six sheets of flat colour, each one a separate layer that separates from the others
as you move the pointer. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no
external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by
double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: cut paper on cream. Ground #f3ece0, board #f7f2e7, hairlines
rgba(42,38,32,.2), ink #2a2620 with muted #6b6455, terracotta #c2562f and sage #5f7a52
as the two accents. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em
kickers, no gradients anywhere on the sheets.

Six sheets, each its own absolutely-positioned SVG on a 1600 × 900 viewBox with
preserveAspectRatio slice, so they cover a 16:9 board at any width: the sky and its
horizon wash, the moon with its bite cut out of the board colour, a hill profile built
from a seeded random walk, a row of pine silhouettes, a field of cut stems with heads,
and a front row of grass strokes with tips. Every shape is a flat palette colour with
one darker stroke, which is what a paper cut actually looks like.

The landscape is generated from one seed with a mulberry32 generator, so Cut another
gives a genuinely different board: different moon position, different hill profile,
different pine count and height, different stems. Three palettes — dusk, meadow and
ink — re-draw the whole board in a new set of six tones.

Depth: each layer is translated by the eased pointer position times its own index and
scaled 1.07 so no gap opens at the edge, and carries a drop-shadow that reads as paper
thickness. Flatten collapses the whole thing to one colour, kills the parallax and drops
the shadows, leaving the single silhouette a paper cutter would hold to the light.

Dock: a chip per layer that lifts that sheet out of the board, a depth slider, three
palette chips, Flatten and Cut another. Readout: sheets on, shapes drawn, current
parallax distance, palette and mode. Layout: nav, centred hero, the board, the dock, the
readout row, two three-cell note rows on the knife, the depth, the cut, Flatten, layer
off and paper, then a footer. Links hidden under 780px.

Interaction: move or drag across the board and the stack separates, with each sheet
further from the one behind it; lift a sheet out with its chip and see what the board
was doing under it; change the depth or the palette; flatten the stack into its
silhouette; and cut another board from a new seed.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A shadow-box landscape on a cream board, built at load from one seed into six flat
SVG sheets — sky, moon with a cut bite, hill profile, pines, stems, grass — that each
move at their own rate as the pointer crosses the board so the stack separates like real
paper, with per-layer chips that lift a sheet out, three palettes that redraw everything,
a depth slider, and a Flatten mode that collapses the whole thing into the single
silhouette a paper cutter would hold to the light.

## Major visual techniques used
- Flat colour with one darker stroke per shape, no gradients, so the sheets read as
  paper rather than vector art
- Six separately positioned SVGs over a 16:9 aspect board, each with
  preserveAspectRatio slice so they always cover
- Each layer translated by the eased pointer times its index and scaled 1.07, so the
  parallax never opens a gap at the rim
- One drop-shadow per sheet, which is the entire depth cue for stacked paper
- A mulberry32 seeded generator, so a single integer produces a whole landscape and
  Cut another changes everything at once
- Hill profile as a clamped random walk, so it never leaves the band it belongs in
- The moon's bite filled with the board colour rather than a mask, which is how a
  cut-out actually works
- Grass strokes drawn with quadratic bends and dots for tips, so the front row is not
  a repeated clip
- Pointer target eased toward the actual pointer rather than following it, so the
  stack separates smoothly
- Flatten that rebuilds the same scene with shadows, strokes and parallax off
- Per-layer chips as aria-pressed toggles that set display on the layer element
- Palettes as arrays of six tones indexed by layer, so a palette change is a re-run of
  the same generator rather than a recolour pass

## Intended interaction model
A board you tilt. Visitors move the pointer across it to see the sheets separate, lift
one out entirely, move the depth to change how far apart they sit, switch palettes to
redraw the scene, flatten the stack into its silhouette, and cut another board from a
new seed. Everything is one file, nothing is loaded, and every board is generated where
it stands.
