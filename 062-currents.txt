# CURRENTS — a flow field you can stir

## Short title
Currents

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a flow field visualisation
where thousands of particles are advected by a direction field and paint the sheet they
cross, with every parameter of the field exposed. No external dependencies whatsoever —
no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript
inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a deep current at night. Ground #060912, canvas #04060d, hairlines
rgba(232,238,247,.13), body #e8eef7 with muted #98a4b5, cyan #5ad9e4 for readouts and
sand #ffb37a for figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .1em–.4em
kickers, monospace figures.

The field is a scalar angle built from two sines at different scales plus a
turbulence-weighted pair of cross terms, which is the cheapest honest turbulence there
is. Direction is that angle times the golden angle, so consecutive directions are never
repeated. Two thousand particles each read the direction where they stand, take one
step of flow×0.12, and draw a hairline; the picture is the accumulated history of those
decisions, and nothing in it is a preset curve.

Every frame the sheet is washed with a translucent ground so old streams fade at one
rate everywhere rather than per particle. Segments are bucketed by speed into four
Path2D objects and stroked additive, one colour pass per bucket — so line colour is a
speed map rather than a particle property, and a filament changes hue as it accelerates.

Four sliders: Flow (step length), Turbulence (weight of the cross terms), Scale (the
spatial frequency of the field) and Drag (how strongly particles within 220 units are
pulled toward the cursor). Three palettes — thermal, ice and ink. Show the field turns
on a 48-unit lattice of arrows sampled from the same function, so the numbers can be
seen rather than only traced. New sheet wipes the canvas and reseeds. Clicking the
canvas injects two hundred particles at the pointer.

Readout: live particle count, whether the arrow lattice is on, mean speed across the
field, and total seeded. Layout: nav, centred hero, the full-width field, a dock of four
sliders, palette chips and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell rows of notes
on advection, the field, drag, palette, arrows and decay, then a footer. Two-up dock
under 820px, links hidden under 780px.

Interaction: the field runs immediately. Visitors drag through it to leave a wake that
keeps moving after the pointer has gone, click to inject a burst of dye, nudge the scale
and turbulence sliders to change the topology of the whole sheet, and switch palettes to
read the speed map in a different key.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A flow field on canvas where two thousand particles each read a direction built from
summed sines, step along it and leave a hairline behind, so the sheet fills with
streams that are the history of the field rather than drawn curves — coloured by speed
in one of three palettes, washed each frame so trails fade evenly, stirrable by dragging
a cursor that pulls nearby particles toward it, injectable by clicking, and readable as
an arrow lattice on demand.

## Major visual techniques used
- Direction from a scalar angle multiplied by the golden angle, so consecutive
  directions are never the same and the field never looks periodic
- Turbulence as a weighted sum of cross terms at double the spatial frequency, which
  costs nothing and vortices the sheet
- Advection at a fraction of the unit direction per step, so speed is a property of the
  field and the flow slider together
- Segments bucketed by speed into four Path2D objects, then stroked in four colour
  passes additive — a speed map with four state changes instead of two thousand
- Additive compositing so overlapping filaments brighten instead of covering
- One translucent ground wash per frame, which fades everything at the same rate
- Particles recycled in place when they age out or leave the sheet, so the count is
  stable and no allocation happens mid-frame
- Cursor drag as an inverse-distance pull inside a 220-unit radius scaled by the drag
  slider, which leaves a visible wake
- Click seeding that injects a burst of particles at the pointer in a 60-unit disc
- Arrow lattice drawn from the same direction function as the particles, so the
  debug view and the picture cannot disagree
- Fixed internal canvas resolution with CSS scaling, keeping the field's spatial
  frequency stable at every width
- Live mean speed computed from the same per-particle speed value used for colour

## Intended interaction model
A sheet you stir. The field is already moving when the page opens; dragging through it
leaves a wake, clicking injects dye, and the four sliders change the physics of the
whole field rather than a single trace. Show the field exposes the underlying direction
as arrows, and New sheet starts again from black.
