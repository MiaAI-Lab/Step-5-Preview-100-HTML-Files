# THE WALL — a cave you have to light

## Short title
Cave Painting

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a cave wall of ochre and
charcoal paintings that is dark until you carry a torch across it, with four pigments you
can add with. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external
images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking,
responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: ochre and umber. Ground #150f0a, panels the same family, hairlines
rgba(240,226,204,.14), body #f0e2cc with muted #a89579, one accent orange #d98a3c and a
sage #8fc7a8. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers,
monospace figures.

The wall is drawn once into its own canvas at load: a rock face with horizontal strata,
nine thousand specks of grain, twenty-six calcite streaks running down, a shaded ledge at
the bottom, and then the paintings — a deer, two horses, a bison, three hand stencils, a
scatter of blown dots and a row of dots along the ceiling. The animals are drawn from
packed body outlines with the head low, the legs as vertical strokes and the mane as a few
lines, because that is what a Palaeolithic panel looks like: not a drawing of an animal
but a set of marks that resolve into one when you are close with a light.

The hand stencils are drawn as a palm plus five rounded strokes, which is the negative
space a blowpipe leaves.

The torch is a hole cut in a field of black. Every frame the wall is drawn, then a radial
gradient whose centre is transparent and whose edge is almost completely opaque fills the
whole canvas, so only a warm pool around the pointer is lit. The radius is the glow slider
times the remaining fuel, modulated by two out-of-phase sines so the light breathes the way
a flame does, and a small warm pool is added with a lighter composite.

Fuel drains at a fixed rate and Light a new torch refills it; below two percent the lit
area collapses to an ember. Picking a pigment and dragging adds strokes to the wall canvas
itself with the same four colours, so what you paint is permanent and behaves exactly like
what is already there. Stencil a hand drops a hand stencil at the torch.

Readout: fuel, the selected pigment, strokes made, the lit radius in pixels, and the panel's
age.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the cave canvas with its caption, a dock of pigments, three
sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on pigment, the
stencil, overdrawing, light, flicker and paint, then a footer.

Interaction: move the pointer to carry the torch and the animals appear one at a time;
drag to paint with the same four pigments; make a hand stencil; and light a new torch when
the old one dies.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A cave wall drawn once into an offscreen canvas — strata, grain, calcite streaks and a
panel of ochre deer, horses, bison, hand stencils and blown dots — revealed frame by frame
through a radial hole cut in a field of black that follows the pointer, its radius set by
a glow slider, remaining fuel and two out-of-phase sines so the light flickers like a
flame, with four pigments that paint permanently onto the same wall and a stencil button
that drops a hand where the torch is.

## Major visual techniques used
- The wall drawn once into an offscreen canvas and never cleared, so the torch is a
  reveal rather than a repaint
- Darkness as a radial gradient from transparent at the centre to almost opaque black at
  the edge, filled over the whole frame, which is the cheapest honest torch
- Flame as two out-of-phase sines modulating the lit radius, so the pool breathes and
  never settles
- A second warm pool composited with 'lighter' right at the flame, which is what makes
  the light read as fire rather than as a hole
- Fuel as a monotonically decreasing number that collapses the lit radius to an ember at
  the bottom, so the light really does fail
- Rock built from strata bands, thousands of one-pixel specks, calcite streaks and a
  shaded ledge, all drawn once
- Animals as packed outlines with vertical-stroke legs and stroked manes, which is how
  the original panels resolve rather than as illustrated creatures
- Overdrawing by painting animals on top of each other at different alphas, so the panel
  has the layered history a real one does
- Hand stencil as a palm plus five rounded strokes — the negative space of a blowpipe
- Visitor strokes written to the wall canvas itself, so they are permanent and lit by the
  same torch
- Blown dots as scattered circles of varying radius in the same pigments
- Four pigments as aria-pressed swatches with the real paste colours

## Intended interaction model
A wall you light and then add to. Visitors carry the torch with the pointer, watch the
animals appear, drag to paint in ochre, charcoal, kaolin or manganese, stencil a hand, and
light a new torch when the old one dies. Everything painted is permanent, and everything
painted is revealed only by the light you carry.
