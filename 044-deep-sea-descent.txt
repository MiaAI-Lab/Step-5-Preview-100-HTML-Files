# DESCENT — Abyssal Diver, 4 200 m

## Short title
Deep Sea Descent

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a deep-sea submersible
descent simulation called DESCENT. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN,
no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries.
All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid
HTML5.

Artistic direction: the lightless ocean. Behind the canvas, a CSS sea runs from
#03222f through #021a26, #01121c and #010a11 with a faint cyan wash at the top,
and its brightness is recomputed every frame from the current depth so the page
itself darkens as you sink — from a bright 1.0 at the surface to 0.25 on the bottom.
The submersible is built in CSS: an elliptical hull of pale grey-blue radial
gradient with paired inset shadows and its own turbulence-data-URI speckle, a dark
viewport ring, a thruster pod, a manipulator arm, a landing skid, and an amber lamp
whose drop-shadow makes it a real light source.

The water has three populations, each behaving differently. One hundred and sixty
marine-snow particles drift on per-mote phase sine, and are pushed upward whenever
the descent rate is positive, so they stream past as you sink. Eighteen
bioluminescent animals swim on their own velocities; each is drawn in additive blend
as a radial-gradient halo whose alpha is multiplied by (1 − ambient), so they are
completely invisible in the upper sunlit zone and only begin to glow once the ambient
light has died — half at hue 190 (cyan) and half at 290 (violet). Each has a burst
state that flares to five times its size and decays at 0.94 per frame, triggered at
random, so the dark punctuates with brief individual flashes. The lamp casts a wide
warm radial gradient whose radius scales with the Lights slider.

Depth drives everything. As depth rises from 0 to 4 200 m, the ambient term falls to
zero, the depth gradient overlay darkens, the CSS sea brightness drops, the gauges
change (depth in metres, pressure in MPa, temperature falling from 18.4 °C), and the
bioluminescent population lights up. Dragging horizontally thrusts the submersible:
a target x accumulates, eases toward the actual at 0.04, and the hull pitches with
the accumulated velocity, so the vehicle leans as it moves.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas over the sea, the CSS submersible fixed near the
bottom, and a floating UI overlay whose pointer-events pass through except on the
controls. Top-left is a monospace label above an italic serif headline with one line
in pale cyan, plus a paragraph about light and pressure. Top-right is a monospace
gauge stack. Bottom is a glass tray with three sliders (Descent 0–100, Lights 0–100,
Marine snow 0–100) plus Dive and Surface buttons, and a monospace hint.

Interaction: set the descent rate with the slider or press Dive to dive at full
rate; Surface stops the descent and rises automatically back to zero, resetting the
environment. The Lights slider changes the lamp cone's radius and brightness, and
Marine snow changes particle density. Dragging sideways thrusts and banks the
submersible. On narrow screens the paragraph, gauges and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A submersible in the lightless ocean: as depth climbs, the CSS sea dims, the depth
overlay darkens, the gauges shift toward hundreds of atmospheres, and eighteen
bioluminescent animals — cyan and violet halos in additive blend, alpha gated by
ambient light, flaring on random bursts — begin to glow, while one hundred and sixty
marine-snow particles stream upward past a lamp cone whose size you control.

## Major visual techniques used
- CSS sea whose brightness is recomputed every frame from current depth
- Depth-driven overlay gradient that darkens as ambient light falls
- Bioluminescence gated by (1 − ambient): invisible in daylight, glowing in the dark
- Radial-gradient halos in 'lighter' blend with burst states that flare and decay at
  0.94 per frame
- Two-colour life palette (cyan hue 190, violet hue 290) split across the population
- Marine-snow particles on per-mote sine phase, pushed upward by descent rate
- Lamp cone as a wide warm radial gradient whose radius scales with the Lights slider
- CSS submersible: elliptical hull with paired inset shadows, turbulence speckle,
  dark viewport ring, thruster, manipulator, skid and amber lamp with drop-shadow
- Horizontal thrust with eased target/actual separation and hull pitch from
  accumulated velocity
- Live gauges: depth, pressure (0.1 + depth × 0.0102 MPa) and temperature
- Dive/Surface buttons that write the descent slider and manage their own lit states
- Italic serif headline with one pale-cyan line against 8.5px tracked monospace
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A dive you control. Visitors set a descent rate or press Dive, then watch the world
go dark and the animals begin to light up, drag sideways to move and bank the
submersible, adjust the lamp and the snow, and press Surface to come back up. The
environment responds to depth continuously, so the page is as much a reading of a
curve as it is a scene.
