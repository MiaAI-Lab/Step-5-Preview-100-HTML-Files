i

Clues

The clock is stopped, not broken: it stopped at the moment the room was sealed, and the four digits it shows are the four the safe wants — in the order the note under the blotter tells you to read them.

ii

Red herrings

The book on geology, the sealed envelope and the picture of the dog are all real objects and none of them is the key. An escape room is a test of what you can leave alone.

iii

The exit

The door needs a brass key, and the brass key is in the safe, and the safe needs the code. Three locks, three objects, one chain — and the whole thing is solvable in under a minute if you read the note first.

iv

Notebook

Every action writes a line. Nothing changes silently, which is the difference between a puzzle and a guess — you always know what you have already tried.

v

Hints

Three hints, each one vaguer than the last, and using one is recorded. There is no penalty for taking one except the record of it.

vi

State

Inventory, locks and the notebook are one object, and every drawing decision reads from it. Nothing in this room is a picture of a state; it is the state.
