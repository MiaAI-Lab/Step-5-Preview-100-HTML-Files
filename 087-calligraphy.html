i

Three scripts

Foundational holds the nib at 30 degrees, Uncial and Carolingian at 40, and Italic at 45 with a faster hand. The angle slider moves continuously between them, so the same stroke can be a Roman minuscule or a Blackletter.

ii

The ductus

Every historical hand has a prescribed order of strokes. Draw down and the nib lies flat and the stroke is thick; draw up and it stands on its edge and the stroke is thin. That is the whole grammar, and the sheet below spells it out.

iii

Pressure

Rubbing a flexible nib harder spreads it, so the thick stroke can be made by leaning on the pen rather than by direction alone. Two ways to the same letter, and the pressure slider is the second one.

iv

Guidelines

Four lines: x-height, waist, base and descender. Everything is drawn between them, and the nib width sets the spacing, because the pen you choose decides the size you write at.

v

Ink

Iron gall is nearly black and bites into the sheet. Walnut and sepia are warm and fugitive. Gouache is opaque and sits on top. Each ink here has its own colour and its own level of coverage.

vi

Fresh sheet

A new sheet is a new piece of paper: it clears what you drew and leaves the guidelines, because the guide lines are not part of the writing.
