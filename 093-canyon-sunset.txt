# GOLDEN HOUR — the last twenty minutes

## Short title
Canyon Sunset

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a canyon in the last twenty
minutes of light, where the colour of everything is computed from the sun's altitude and
the air mass it has crossed, and where the walls are strata that re-light as the sun drops.
No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS
libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive,
valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a warm final light. Ground #1a0e0a, hairlines rgba(246,232,220,.14),
body #f6e8dc with muted #9c8878, sun orange #ff9c3c as the accent and a canyon red #e0543c
for the figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers,
monospace figures.

The canyon is three walls of nine strata, each a sine-shaped boundary drawn from its own
seed, layered far to near and shaded by depth. Each wall is filled with a three-way mix of
the rock's lit, mid and dark tones scaled by how much light that face gets, so the whole
picture re-lights when the sun moves.

The sky is a gradient whose stops are the computed light colour, and the sun is a radial
glow of the same colour at a height set by the sun's altitude. Haze is a wash at the
horizon, dust deepens the red, and three sky modes add a band of high cloud or an underglow
of low cloud.

The physics are real. Air mass is Kasten and Young's approximation, so at six degrees it is
nearly ten and at sixty degrees it is close to one. Colour temperature falls from 5,500
kelvin toward 2,000, with the dust adding to the reddening. The light colour is a
kelvin-to-RGB conversion. Shadow length is one over the tangent of the sun's altitude, so a
six-degree sun casts a shadow ten times the height of the thing making it.

Four rocks — Navajo sandstone, Redwall limestone, Chinle shale and basalt — each with its
own lit, mid and dark tones and its own glow factor. Controls: four rocks, three skies, sun
altitude, dust, haze, Let it set and Back to noon. Dragging the canvas moves the sun
directly. Readout: sun altitude, air mass, colour temperature, shadow length, fraction of
the wall lit, and the phase from afternoon through golden hour, last light and afterglow.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the canyon canvas with its caption, a dock of two chip rows,
three sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on air mass,
colour temperature, shadows, long shadows, four rocks and three skies, then a footer.

Interaction: the light is already low. Visitors drag the sun down through golden hour into
afterglow and watch the air mass, the colour temperature and the shadow length all climb
as the walls redden and dim; pull the dust up to deepen the red and flatten the walls; push
the haze up; switch rock and watch basalt refuse to glow; and press Back to noon to see
what the whole thing is doing differently.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A canyon of three walls and nine strata each on canvas, filled with a three-way mix of the
rock's lit, mid and dark tones scaled by the light that face receives, under a sky whose
gradient stops are the computed light colour — where air mass is Kasten and Young's
approximation, colour temperature falls from 5,500 kelvin toward 2,000 with the dust
adding to the reddening, the light colour is a kelvin-to-RGB conversion, shadow length is
one over the tangent of the sun's altitude, and a multiply band deepens the shadow the
lower the sun sits.

## Major visual techniques used
- Air mass by Kasten and Young, so at six degrees it is nearly ten and at sixty it is close
  to one, and both the readout and the colour follow it
- Colour temperature from the air mass plus dust, then kelvin-to-RGB, so the light colour
  is computed rather than picked
- Shadow length as one over the tangent of the altitude, so a six-degree sun casts a
  ten-times shadow and the readout says so
- Strata as nine sine boundaries from their own seeds, layered far to near and shaded by
  depth for aerial perspective
- Each wall filled with a three-way mix of the rock's lit, mid and dark tones scaled by the
  light the face gets, so a rock switch is a real re-light
- Shadow as a multiply band whose depth follows the sun's altitude
- Sun as a radial glow in the computed light colour at the altitude's height
- Haze as a horizon wash whose alpha is the haze slider
- Dust deepening the red and flattening the walls, which is the trade it actually makes
- Three sky modes: a band of high cloud, an underglow of low cloud, or nothing
- Sun's reflection on the water as scattered bars in the light colour
- Strata labels drawn in the classic Grand Canyon order, so the picture reads as a specific
  place

## Intended interaction model
A place to watch the light go. Visitors drag the sun down from afternoon through golden hour
to afterglow and every number on the panel climbs with it — air mass, colour temperature and
shadow length — while the walls redden and dim. They add dust to deepen the colour and
flatten the walls, lift the haze, switch the rock, and go back to noon to see what the whole
thing does differently at one air mass.
