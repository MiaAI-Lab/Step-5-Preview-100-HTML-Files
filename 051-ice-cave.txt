# GLACIER — Inside the Blue

## Short title
Ice Cave

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive glacier
ice-cave scene called GLACIER. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web
fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries. All CSS
and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: deep blue ice. Behind the canvas, a CSS cave (#0a2a3d → #062131
→ #04141f) with a bright cyan radial in the upper left and a deep azure wash lower
right; its brightness and saturation are recomputed every frame from the Clarity
slider, so low clarity means murky, cloudy ice and high clarity means glass-clear
glacier blue. On the canvas, a light shaft follows the pointer as a wide radial
whose radius scales with the Light slider, and a deep azure shadow gradient covers
the bottom thirty per cent so the floor always reads as furthest away.

The facets are the substance of the page. Up to one hundred and twenty long thin
rhombi — the classic ice facet — are placed at random positions and depths, sized by
random width and height, and rotated by their own angle. Each facet's angle eases
toward the direction of the light source at 0.008 per frame, with the shortest-path
wrap handled properly, so every plane in the cave slowly turns to face the light as
you move the pointer. Each facet's brightness is a Lambert-like term: the absolute
cosine of the difference between its own angle and the light direction, multiplied
by the Clarity slider and its depth, filling a three-stop gradient whose lightness
is lifted by the same term — so facets facing the sun are almost white and facets
edge-on are nearly invisible. The lit ones (above 0.5) get a bright 0.9px edge
stroke. A Shimmer toggle adds a per-facet slow self-rotation, which is what makes
the whole cave glitter.

Meltwater is a second mode: seventy droplets that fall, wrap to the top when they
leave, drift on per-drop sines, and draw a 22-pixel streak behind them in additive
blend so they read as running water.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas over the cave with a floating UI overlay whose
pointer-events pass through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace
coordinate label above an italic serif headline with one line in ice cyan, plus a
short paragraph about why glacier ice is blue. Top-right is a monospace gauge stack
showing ice temperature, facet count and a refraction index derived from clarity.
Bottom is a glass tray with three sliders (Clarity, Facets, Light) plus Shimmer and
Meltwater toggles, and a monospace hint.

Interaction: the pointer is the light source — everything refracts toward it. The
three sliders retune the ice live: Clarity changes transparency and the page's
brightness, Facets adds or removes planes, Light widens the shaft. Shimmer adds the
slow per-facet rotation; Meltwater turns on the falling water. On narrow screens the
paragraph, gauges and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A glacier cave of long thin ice facets, each slowly turning to face the light as
you move it, its brightness a Lambert term from the angle between its own normal and
the light so facing planes glow almost white and edge-on planes vanish, scaled by a
clarity slider that also drives the whole page's brightness and saturation — with
seventy meltwater streaks and a deep azure floor shadow.

## Major visual techniques used
- Lambert-like facet brightness: |cos(ownAngle − lightAngle)| × clarity × depth,
  feeding both the gradient's lightness and its alpha
- Per-facet angle easing toward the light with shortest-path angle wrapping
- Long thin rhombus facets at random positions, sizes, depths and rotations
- Sorted-once depth ordering (far facets first) so the loop does not sort per frame
- Three-stop gradient fills indexed to the facet's own hue (190–220)
- Bright 0.9px edge stroke only above a brightness threshold
- Per-facet slow self-rotation for the Shimmer mode
- Pointer-following light shaft as a wide radial whose radius scales with Light
- Deep azure floor shadow over the bottom thirty per cent
- CSS cave whose brightness and saturation are recomputed per frame from Clarity
- Meltwater: falling droplets with 22px additive streaks and sine drift, wrapping
  to the top
- Live gauges: ice temperature (which changes when Meltwater is on), facet count,
  and a refraction index derived from clarity
- Italic serif headline with one ice-cyan line against 8.5px tracked monospace
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A cave you can light. Visitors move the pointer and every facet in the chamber turns
to catch it, shimmering if they ask it to; the three sliders change how clear the
ice is, how many facets there are and how wide the shaft is, and Meltwater starts
the water running. The scene is entirely driven by the light angle, which is the
pointer.
