i

The golden angle

137.5 degrees is 360 divided by the golden ratio squared. It is the one angle that never lines up with a previous primordium, which is why a sunflower head packs the most seeds into the same space as anything else.

ii

Symmetry

Daisies are ray florets on a golden-angle disc, roses are a golden-angle spiral seen from above, and a buttercup's five petals are the same primordia with a much smaller number. The angle is the whole design.

iii

Opening

A bud is not a small flower. The primordia are all there, stacked, and opening is each one moving outward and rotating the head until the youngest are on the outside and the oldest are in the middle.

iv

Coverage

The number in the corner is the fraction of the head covered by petals. At the golden angle it approaches the best packing there is; pushed away from it, holes appear and the head stops looking like a flower.

v

Growth

Grow it adds primordia one at a time at the current angle, so you can watch the pattern fall into place and then fall apart when you move the slider mid-growth.

vi

Colour

Petals are shaded by their order, not by a single hue, so the head reads as a three-dimensional head with the oldest at the centre and the youngest at the rim.
