# STORM LIGHT — a coast that has to be watched

## Short title
Lighthouse Storm

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a lighthouse in bad weather,
where a single rotating beam is the only thing that finds anything, and where the weather
sliders decide whether it finds anything at all. No external dependencies whatsoever — no
CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline,
works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a night coast. Ground #050a14, canvas #04060e, hairlines
rgba(232,240,251,.14), body #e8f0fb with muted #93a4bd, lamp-amber #ffd479 as the accent
and a cold cyan #7fd4ff for readouts. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase
.2em–.4em kickers, monospace figures.

The scene is one canvas at 980 × 540. The sea is three layers of summed sines whose
amplitude and speed rise with the swell slider, each layer darker than the one behind, with
foam marks drawn only on the crests of the front layer. A tapered tower with two red bands
sits on a rock outcrop, with a lantern room and a lamp that glows from a radial gradient.

The beam is a wedge from the lamp, drawn by translating to the lamp and rotating by the
beam angle, with a linear gradient that fades out along its length. It makes one revolution
every ten seconds, and the light's character is three flashes per revolution. Press Signal
SOS and the beam is gated by a real Morse timing — dot units of one, dashes of three, the
gaps between them — so the wedge itself blinks ··· ——— ··· on the sea.

The boat is the point of the whole picture. It is drawn dark, and it only becomes visible
when the beam's angle agrees with its bearing from the lamp within three degrees, at which
moment a soft spotlight appears around it. Rain is a set of streaks blown sideways by the
wind, and sprayed foam speckles the crests. Steam the glass lays a condensation wash and a
set of blurred blobs over the whole scene and halves the reported visibility.

Controls: wind, rain, swell, Signal SOS and Steam the glass. Readout: wind in knots, sea
state by name with wave height, visibility in kilometres derived from the rain and the
steam, the beam's character, the signal state and the current bearing in degrees from
north.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the scene with a live signal overlay, a dock of three sliders
and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on character, sea state,
visibility, steam, bearing and storm, then a footer.

Interaction: the beam turns from the moment the page opens and the boat is found and lost
every ten seconds. Visitors set the swell to build or flatten the sea, blow the rain
sideways, signal SOS to watch the light spell, and steam the glass to lose half the
visibility and see how little that leaves.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A night coast on canvas where three sine layers of sea, a tapered tower with two red bands
and a single rotating beam that makes one revolution every ten seconds with a three-flash
character — gated into real Morse timing when SOS is signalled — reveal a boat only in the
three degrees where the beam's angle agrees with the boat's bearing, with wind blowing the
rain sideways, swell setting the sea state and height, and steamed glass halving the
visibility and washing the scene.

## Major visual techniques used
- Sea as three layers of summed sines with per-layer amplitude, speed and wavenumber, so
  the swell slider changes depth as well as size
- Foam drawn only where the crest exceeds a threshold, which is what crest foam is
- Beam as a radial wedge by translating to the lamp and rotating, with a gradient along
  its length rather than a solid colour
- Beam visibility gated by a Morse timing sequence built from dot and dash units with
  gaps, so the light itself spells
- Boat visibility from the angle between the beam and the boat's bearing, so the reveal is
  geometric rather than scripted
- Lamp glow and boat spotlight as radial gradients, so nothing needs a filter
- Rain as streaks whose slant is the wind and whose speed is the drop's own, recycled at
  the bottom
- Spray as speckle on the crests, positioned from the same wave function
- Steamed glass as a translucent wash plus blurred radial blobs, halving the reported
  visibility rather than only decorating
- Bearing read every eighth frame from the same beam angle the drawing uses
- Sea state named from the swell and paired with a wave height derived from it
- Reduced-motion honoured by starting the weather calm rather than by stopping the light

## Intended interaction model
A coast to watch. The beam is already turning and the boat is already being found and
lost; visitors build the sea, blow the rain sideways, make the light spell SOS, and steam
the glass to see how much of the view that costs. Nothing here is aimed or scripted — the
beam reveals what it happens to cross, exactly as a real one does.
