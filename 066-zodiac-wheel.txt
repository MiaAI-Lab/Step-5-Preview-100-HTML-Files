# THE WHEEL — twelve signs, their aspects, their stars

## Short title
Zodiac Wheel

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive zodiac wheel
that divides the circle into twelve equal thirty-degree signs, draws the aspect angle
between any two of them, and reveals each sign's real constellation from real
coordinates. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external
images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking,
responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a night instrument. Ground #0b0d24, panel #111433, hairlines
rgba(236,235,248,.14), body #ecebf8 with muted #a5a3c8, gold #e8c46a for the hand and the
figures and sky blue #8fd0ff for readouts and harmonious aspects, with a rose #e88a8a for
the discordant ones. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .1em–.4em
kickers, monospace figures.

The wheel is an SVG on a 560 × 560 viewBox built at load: twelve sectors computed as real
arc paths from the centre at 280, in three rings — an outer element band, an inner sign
band and a tick ring. Fire, earth, air and water alternate in strict order, so the colour
band is the element rule drawn rather than described. Each sector carries its glyph and
its name, rotated into place along the sector's own angle, and each is a button with
aria-pressed for its selected state.

Twelve signs are stored with their glyph, element, modality, ruler, dates, brightest
star and distance, plus a real star map: a set of normalised coordinates and the list of
pairs to join. Selecting a sign rebuilds the panel and redraws the constellation as an
SVG whose connecting lines animate in with a stroke-dashoffset reveal, and its brightest
star named underneath.

Aspects: pick a first sign and a second — either with the panel button or by clicking two
sectors in turn — and the wheel draws the chord between them at the angle their separation
demands. The angle is reduced to the shortest way around the circle, looked up in a table
of conjunction, sextile, square, trine and opposition, and the chord's radius, weight and
colour follow whether the aspect is soft or hard. Anything else is drawn faint and
reported as unaspected.

A gold hand points at the sign the sun is in at this minute, computed from the day of the
year with the hour interpolating degrees inside the sign; it re-reads the machine clock
every twenty seconds rather than running on an animation.

Layout: nav, centred hero, a two-column wheel and detail panel, an aspect row with the two
selected signs, the aspect name, the angle and a reading, then a readout row (selected,
angle, element, modality, wheel now), then two three-cell note rows on thirty degrees,
aspects, element, the stars, the hand and precession, then a footer. One column under
900px, links hidden under 780px.

Interaction: click a sector to open its stars; click two sectors (or use the panel button)
and the wheel draws the aspect between them with its angle and its reading; Clear resets
the pair. The hand moves on its own, because it is reading the clock.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A zodiac wheel on SVG where twelve thirty-degree sectors are computed as real arcs and
coloured by the fire-earth-air-water alternation, each one opening a panel that draws the
sign's real constellation from normalised star coordinates with an animated line reveal —
and where picking any two signs draws the chord between them at the angle their
separation gives, looks that angle up among conjugation, sextile, square, trine and
opposition, and colours and weights the chord accordingly, while a gold hand points at
the sign the sun is in according to this machine's clock.

## Major visual techniques used
- Sectors built as genuine arc paths from the centre, so the wheel is geometry rather
  than twelve images
- Element alternation drawn as a colour band, making the fire-earth-air-water rule
  visible instead of asserted
- Glyphs and sector names placed on the sector's own angle, so they sit along the slice
- Constellation drawn from normalised coordinates and a pair list, which is the same
  data format a planetarium uses
- Line reveal animated with stroke-dasharray and stroke-dashoffset, so the constellation
  is drawn rather than faded in
- Aspect angle reduced to the shortest way around the circle before lookup, which is
  what makes opposition and conjunction both work from one table
- Chord radius, weight and colour all derived from the aspect's harmony, so a soft
  aspect and a hard one read differently at a glance
- Unaspected pairs drawn faint instead of hidden, so "no classical aspect" is a visible
  state rather than an absence
- aria-pressed carried on the sectors themselves, so selection is readable by the
  browser as well as by the stylesheet
- Two selection paths — the panel button and clicking sectors — funnelled into one
  function so they cannot disagree
- The hand computed from day of year plus time of day, re-read on an interval, rather
  than animated on a loop
- Brightest star per sign named in the readout, which is the fact that ties the star map
  to the sector

## Intended interaction model
An instrument to play. Visitors click a sector to open its constellation, then pick a
second one and the wheel draws the angle between them with its name and its reading; the
gold hand quietly tracks the sun's sign from the machine's own clock. Clear resets the
pair and the aspect chords leave with it.
