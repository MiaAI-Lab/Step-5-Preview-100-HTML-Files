寂

The beauty of the used

Six vessels, thrown one to a morning and fired once, in a wood kiln that decides everything. Where the flame licks longest, the clay gives up and turns to glass. We do not correct it.

Tsuki

No. 一 · 2026

A moon jar that never quite closed. The seam runs slightly off true, which is the only way to tell it was made by a person.

Hira

No. 二 · 2026

Flat-bellied, thrown in one pull. The ash glaze pooled where the wall was thickest and ran thin where it was not.

Bin

No. 三 · 2026

A narrow bottle for one stem. Fired closest to the fire mouth; it carries the kiln's red signature on one shoulder.

Hachi

No. 四 · 2026

A wide bowl for washing brushes. The first thing we threw each morning and the last thing we washed.

Tsubo

No. 五 · 2026

Shouldered jar for tea. Two cracks, both from the reduction, both left exactly where they happened.

Kame

No. 六 · 2026

The last of the firing. The kiln was cooling, so the glaze crept and froze mid-slide, which we have decided is its best feature.

In the kiln's mouth the clay is forgiven for everything it was not able to be on the wheel.
— Tsubaki Rin, master potter, b. 1948
一

Throw

One hundred grams of foot clay, wedged twice, centred until it stops arguing.

二

Dry

Eight days under cloth. Turning once, on the fourth day, when leather hard.

三

Fire

Thirty-six hours of red pine. The kiln, not the potter, applies the glaze.

四

Keep

No piece is corrected, filled or recoated. What leaves the kiln is finished.

The studio opens on the first Saturday of each month, from the hour the sun reaches the kiln door until it leaves it.
