# SOUND CITY — a town you can hear

## Short title
Sound City

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a musical city on a grid,
where buildings are instruments and a playhead walking left to right decides when
each one sounds. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no
external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by
double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a night town. Panel #12151f on ground #0e1017, hairlines at
rgba(220,230,255,.11), body text #dfe4f5 with muted #9aa4c8. Two accents, amber
#ffd166 for the playhead and spires and sky cyan #7fd4ff for readouts and parks, and
a rose #ff8fa3 for works. Serif headings, monospace figures, small-caps uppercase
eyebrows at .2em–.42em tracking.

The grid is 20 columns by 12 rows on canvas, sized to the container width with DPR
scaling and centred with computed offsets. Lots are drawn as dark panels with a
2.5px-coloured outline, a 4×4 field of lit windows whose on/off pattern is derived
deterministically from the lot coordinates, and a cross-hatch for spires. Parks are
a translucent cyan wash with four drifting dots. The playhead is a horizontal amber
gradient band with a 2px leading edge; behind it sits the whole grid stroked at 7%
alpha.

Build menu: house (marimba, high), spire (bell, long), works (bass drum, low),
park (chime, drift), and bulldoze. Every instrument is synthesised with the Web Audio
API: houses are triangle waves on a pentatonic scale chosen by lot coordinate,
spires are two sine partials an octave apart with a two-second decay, works are
filtered noise with a fast lowpass sweep, parks are short detuned triangle chimes.
Play/Stop, a tempo slider from 60 to 180, and a Town button that lays out a preset
plan. The readout counts blocks, houses, spires, works, parks and the current bar.

Layout: nav with serif brand and three links, centred hero with amber eyebrow and an
italic cyan word in the headline, the bench as a two-column grid of menu and canvas,
then two three-cell rows of instruments and notes with large cyan serif figures, then
a footer with the grid size in mono. One column under 900px with the menu border moved
to the bottom; the menu links drop out under 760px.

Interaction: pick a tool, then press and drag on the grid to paint lots of that type;
drag with the right button or choose bulldoze to clear. Pressing Play resumes the
AudioContext (which browsers hold closed until a gesture asks for it) and starts a
sixteenth-note scheduler with a lookahead guard so the playhead cannot run away when
a tab is backgrounded. A preset Town button fills the grid so there is something to
hear immediately, and the readout tally updates as you build.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A night town on a 20-by-12 grid where every lot you lay down is an instrument — houses
sound as marimba, spires as bells, works as a bass drum, parks as chimes — and an
amber playhead sweeping left to right plays whatever column it crosses, so the layout
you draw is literally the arrangement you hear, with a live count of what you have
built and a tempo slider that moves the whole thing.

## Major visual techniques used
- Canvas grid sized from the container width with DPR scaling and computed offsets,
  so the grid stays aligned at every viewport
- Lots as dark panels with a 2.5px coloured outline and a 4×4 field of lit windows
  whose pattern is derived deterministically from the lot coordinates
- Spires crossed with a thin amber hatch, parks as a translucent wash with drifting
  dots animated off a shared clock
- Playhead as a horizontal amber gradient band with a 2px leading edge, plus the
  full grid stroked behind at 7% alpha
- Web Audio synthesis with no samples: triangle-wave marimba, two-partial sine bell,
  noise buffer with a swept lowpass, and a detuned triangle chime
- Pentatonic pitch selection from lot coordinates so neighbouring houses harmonise
- Sixteenth-note scheduler with a lookahead guard that caps catch-up steps
- AudioContext created and resumed only inside the Play click, which is what keeps
  a browser from refusing to start
- Tool menu using aria-pressed for its selected state rather than a class toggle
- Pointer capture plus last-cell tracking so a drag paints lots without repeats
  and keeps working when the pointer leaves the canvas
- Right-click bulldozing that prevents the context menu instead of being a
  separate tool
- Two-column bench collapsing to one column under 900px, menu border moved to the
  bottom, menu links hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A toy you play by ear. Visitors choose a building from the menu, drag across the
grid to lay out a town, press Play and listen to the columns as the playhead crosses
them, drag the tempo to change the feel, and bulldoze or right-click to clear lots
that do not sound right. The preset Town button is there so the first press of Play
always has something to say.
