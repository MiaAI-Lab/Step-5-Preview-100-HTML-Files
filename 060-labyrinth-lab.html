This one is not a picture. It is carved one cell at a time by a stack, and then solved one cell at a time by a queue or by a heuristic — and you can put the heuristic aside and walk it yourself.

i

Backtracker

A stack of cells. Take the newest, carve into a random unvisited neighbour, and when a cell has none left, pop back. This is what makes long corridors: the stack never skips.

ii

Prim

A frontier list instead of a stack. It grows outward like a crystal, so the result is a bush of short branches joined by a few long walks.

iii

Braid

Take a finished maze and knock out one wall at every dead end. Fewer wrong turns, more loops, far harder to hold in your head.

iv

Queue

Breadth-first spreads evenly and is guaranteed to find the shortest path in a maze where every step costs the same. The visited cells you see are literally the queue being drained.

v

Heuristic

Weight the frontier by how far each cell still is from the exit and it stops exploring the half it does not care about. Fewer cells visited, same answer — in a maze, the same answer for free.

vi

Bitmask

Walls are not booleans per side. Each cell keeps four bits — north, east, south, west — and carving a passage is one XOR, so a maze is a typed array rather than a thousand objects.
