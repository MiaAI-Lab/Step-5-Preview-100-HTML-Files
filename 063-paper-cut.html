Knife, six sheets, one moon

A landscape
cut in layers

Every sheet here is a separate piece of paper. The back one is the sky, the front one is a field of cut stems, and each of them moves a little further than the one behind it — which is the whole trick behind a shadow box.

i

The knife

Nothing is drawn with a gradient. Each sheet is flat colour with a 1px darker outline and a single drop of shadow under its long edge, which is exactly what paper looks like stacked.

ii

The depth

Each layer is translated by the pointer times its own index, so moving across the board separates them. A shadow box is a photograph of this relationship taken at one fixed moment.

iii

The cut

Silhouettes come from a seeded generator: hills, pine rows, a wheat field of single stems, birds. The seed changes with every Cut another, so no two boards are the same.

iv

Flatten

Flatten collapses the stack: every sheet goes one colour, the parallax stops, and the scene becomes the single silhouette a paper cutter would actually hold up to the light.

v

Layer off

Each sheet can be lifted out of the board entirely. Take away the hills and the moon sits on the sky; take away the wheat and the front edge is bare.

vi

Paper

The board is a cream ground with a hairline frame and an inner shadow at the rim, and a touch of grain from a repeating gradient — enough to read as stock without a texture file.
