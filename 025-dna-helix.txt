# HELIX — Chromosome 7, Region 14

## Short title
DNA Helix

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a genome-browser-style
page with a rotating DNA double helix, called HELIX. No external dependencies
whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no
JavaScript libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by
double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a scientific journal plate. Warm off-white #f4f6f2 ground with
a faint 48px squared grid baked into the body background, ink-green text #16211c,
and a four-colour base palette used consistently everywhere: green #1f7a5a
(adenine), red #c2452d (thymine), blue #2b4b8f (guanine), ochre #c8971f (cytosine).
Typography is a high-contrast serif for headings and numbers, a monospace at
9.5–11px for labels, metadata and sequence, and a system sans for paragraphs.

The helix: ninety vertical stations, each placed along a taper that fades the
molecule at both ends, at an angle advancing 2.6 full turns over the length plus a
time term that makes it spin. Each station has two backbone positions 180° apart
and two z-depths from the sine of their angles. All ninety stations are collected
into an array and sorted by average depth each frame, so the back of the molecule
draws first and the front draws over it — genuine fake-3D without WebGL. Backbone
beads are drawn with radius scaled by depth and taper and alpha from
0.30 + depth × 0.55. Each rung carries three beads interpolated along it, coloured
blue or ochre by which side of the midline they fall on, with a faint connecting
line behind them. A soft two-sided gradient wash behind the model suggests the
major and minor grooves. Pressing R reverses the spin.

Below the helix, a sequence strip of two hundred vertical blocks, each one of the
four base colours chosen at random, each with a tooltip giving its base name and
its genomic coordinate. Hovering a block rewrites a caption line with that base
name and its chr7 coordinate.

Layout: a nav with a monospace brand and three links. A hero split between a serif
headline (one word in italic green), a paragraph about two metres of DNA per cell,
a four-item colour legend, and the helix canvas on its square stage. Then a
bordered sequence panel with the 200 blocks and the hover caption. Then a
three-cell panel of large serif green numerals (3.4 nm per turn, 2.0 metres per
cell, 20 ångströms wide) with explanatory paragraphs. Then a mirrored detail
section about the CFTR gene with a monospace key-value list (location, exons,
length, protein, strand). Then a ruled monospace footer.

Interaction: the helix spins continuously and reverses on pressing R; the sequence
blocks respond to hover. There are no other controls. The hero and detail sections
collapse to one column under 880px.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A genome-browser plate: a depth-sorted DNA double helix of ninety stations with
tapered ends, two coloured backbones and three-bead base rungs spinning behind a
groove wash, above a 200-block sequence strip that names its base and coordinate
under your cursor, in the four-colour palette of a scientific journal.

## Major visual techniques used
- Fake-3D by depth sorting: all helix stations collected per frame and sorted by
  average z before drawing
- Tapered station placement (sin(f·π)) so the molecule fades at both ends
- Two backbone positions 180° apart, with bead radius and alpha driven by sine of
  the angle (depth) and the taper
- Three-bead base rungs with colour split at the midline by interpolated z
- Faint rung lines drawn behind the beads
- Major/minor groove wash from a horizontal two-sided gradient behind the model
- 200-block sequence strip with random base assignment and coordinate tooltips
- Hover caption rewritten from the block's data attributes
- Four-colour base palette applied consistently to legend, sequence and rungs
- Scientific-journal typography: serif display, 9.5px monospace metadata, sans body
- 48px squared grid baked into the page background
- 1px-gap panel grids showing the hairline colour underneath
- Two-column hero and detail collapsing to one under 880px

## Intended interaction model
An explanatory plate that runs itself. Visitors watch the helix turn (and press R
to reverse it), then hover across the sequence strip to identify individual bases
and their genomic coordinates. Everything else is static reference material — the
three structure figures and the gene panel — presented as a journal would present
it.
