# THE CHART — parchment, rhumb lines and a route

## Short title
Portolan Chart

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a portolan-style chart on
parchment — invented coastline, compass rose with named winds, rhumb lines to the edge of
the paper, a dashed route and a log — where the ink weight and the fading are the
controls. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external
images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking,
responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: parchment and sepia. Ground #2b2418, panel a cream gradient, ink
#4a3520 with verdigris #2f6b6b and cinnabar #a8452c as the two accents, land #cfe0cf.
Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .1em–.4em kickers, monospace figures.

The chart is an SVG on a 900 × 620 viewBox built at load from one mulberry32 seed. The
sea is paved with wavy hairline graticules. The coast is one closed path built from a
smoothed random walk of fifty-four points — the same point cloud that gives a coastline
its bays — plus a smaller second island. Mountains are hachures, not contours: nine short
strokes per peak, getting longer at the summit, which is how height was read before
contours existed. A river runs as a verdigris polyline, and twenty-six little conifer
marks stand in for woodland.

The compass rose at 150, 470 has thirty-two spokes — cardinals, ordinals and half-winds
at different lengths — with the eight-point star overdrawing them, the four wind letters
outside the ring and the sixteen named Mediterranean winds (Tramontana, Greco, Levante,
Sirocco, Ostro, Libeccio, Ponente, Maestro) printed inside it. Sixteen rhumb lines run
from the rose to beyond the edge of the paper: a constant bearing, drawn as a ruler
across the sea, which is what a navigator actually followed.

The route joins the places with a dashed path that crawls along itself with a
stroke-dashoffset animation — portolan courses were dashes, and the dash length was a
rough measure of a day's sail. Five places are clickable and each writes a titled log
entry with an italic quotation into the side panel; the selected place is marked, and only
one can be open at a time. In the margin sits a sea monster and the words hic sunt
dracones.

Controls: Ink weight and Faded both re-weigh and re-fade the whole sheet. Every stroke
records its relative weight and its own opacity when it is drawn, so raising the ink
makes the coastline heavier than the hairlines by the same ratio it started with, and
fading the paper ages everything at once without flattening the hierarchy. Chart the
route and Draw the rose toggle their layers.

Layout: nav, centred hero, a two-column chart and log panel, a readout row (places,
rhumb lines, route, compass, ink), then two three-cell note rows on the rose, rhumb lines,
the route, faded, mountains and the blank, then a footer. One column under 900px, links
hidden under 780px.

Interaction: click a place to read its log, toggle the route and the rose, and drag the
ink and faded sliders to watch the sheet age under your hands. Nothing here is a picture
of parchment: it is a CSS radial gradient, a seeded coastline and one ink colour.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A portolan chart drawn in SVG from a single seed — smoothed-random-walk coastline, hachure
mountains, verdigris river, forest marks, a thirty-two-spoke compass rose with the sixteen
named winds printed inside it and sixteen rhumb lines running off the paper — with five
clickable places that write titled log entries into a side panel, a dashed route that
crawls along itself, a sea monster in the margin, and ink weight and fading sliders that
re-weigh and age the whole sheet without flattening the drawing's hierarchy.

## Major visual techniques used
- Coastline from a smoothed random walk over fifty-four points, which is what gives a
  coast its bays and headlands without drawing them by hand
- Hachures instead of contours: nine short strokes per peak, longer toward the summit,
  so height is read from density of marks
- Compass rose built as thirty-two spokes at three lengths, with an eight-point star
  overdrawing them and the sixteen named winds printed in the ring
- Rhumb lines as straight bearings to beyond the viewBox, one per point of the rose
- Dashed route animated with stroke-dashoffset, so the course crawls the way a
  portolan's dashes were meant to read
- Every stroke records its relative weight and its own opacity at creation, so the ink
  and faded sliders can reweigh and age the sheet without flattening hierarchy
- Land as one flat tint under the ink, which is how a chart worked: colour under, ink over
- Parchment as a CSS radial gradient plus an inset shadow, with no texture file
- Five places as one group with a single open selection, each writing to the same panel
- Sea monster and hic sunt dracones in the margin, drawn as two bezier paths
- Graticule hairlines generated from summed sines so they read as drawn rather than
  plotted
- Log entries written into the existing paragraph rather than replacing the panel, so
  the panel never rebuilds

## Intended interaction model
A chart to read and to age. Visitors click the places along the route and read what the
log says about each one, turn the route and the rose on and off, and drag the ink and
faded sliders to make the sheet heavier or older. The coastline, the rose and the route
are all generated from one number, so the whole chart is reproducible.
