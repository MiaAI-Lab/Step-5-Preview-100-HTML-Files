A chord is a vector.
Its name is where it lands

Paul Hindemith put the twelve notes on a circle ordered by the closeness of their interval to the root, so a chord has a direction and a pull — and ends up resting on the player it can relax into. Every instrument here is drawn, and its timbre is built from real partials.

i

Ordered by interval

Hindemith starts at the root and walks out by the intervals that sit closest to it — fifth, major third, minor third, then the further ones — so distance on the circle is how far a note is from home.

ii

A chord has a vector

Sum where each note sits on the circle and you get a direction. A major chord points one way, a dominant seventh another, and the direction is the pull the chord has.

iii

Additive timbre

Every instrument here is a stack of partials. A clarinet's even harmonics are almost silent, which is the hollow sound; a flute has almost nothing above the second harmonic, which is the pure one.
