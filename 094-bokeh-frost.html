the light came through

An out-of-focus point of light is not a soft blob. It is the shape of the aperture, projected — which is why a bright window becomes a ring at f/2 and a hexagon at f/16, and why the frost's own edges pick up colour.

i

Circle of confusion

How far a point spreads depends on the aperture, the focus distance and how far the point is from the plane of focus. Wide open, a streetlight fills a ring; at f/16 the same light is a small hexagon.

ii

The shape of the hole

Diaphragm blades straighten into a polygon. Five blades give a pentagon, six a hexagon, nine a nine-sided figure that reads as a circle and eleven as a rounder one. The bokeh is the aperture's silhouette.

iii

Frost

Water vapour landing on cold glass grows as hexagonal plates, then dendrites, because ice's crystal structure is hexagonal and growth follows its own structure. Every edge is a hexagon at a different scale.

iv

Diffraction

The thin coloured fringe on a bright edge is the light bending round the same crystal edges it grew past. Colour there is physics, not decoration.

v

Depth of field

Change focal length or aperture and the near and far limits of sharpness move with them. The numbers in the corner are the ones the blur circles are generated from.

vi

Wipe

Wipe a patch and the frost clears in the shape of the drag: the rings behind it come into focus, which is what wiping frost off a window actually does.
