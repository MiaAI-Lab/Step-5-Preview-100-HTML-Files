# HOLO FOIL — light that is printed, not lit

## Short title
Hologram Foil

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a holographic foil card, where
the diffraction grid is CSS gradients and the tilt is real 3D, so the colours come from
geometry rather than from a picture of iridescence. No external dependencies whatsoever
— no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript
inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a dark print shop. Ground #0b0910, panel #120f1a, hairlines
rgba(241,236,247,.14), body #f1ecf7 with muted #a49cae, mint #8be9d0 and pink #ff9ecb as
the two accents. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers,
monospace readouts.

The card: a 5:7 rounded panel inside a perspective stage, rotated on X and Y by three
custom properties written from the pointer, with transform-style preserve-3d. Under the
foil sits the art: a conic gradient in mint, violet, pink and amber that reads as a
printed hologram, with two rings and a serif title over it.

The foil itself is three stacked repeating gradients — the screen — composited with
mix-blend-mode: color-dodge so it adds light to the art instead of covering it. Its
background-position is driven by the same custom properties as the tilt, at 180 and -240
pixels per unit, so moving the pointer moves the diffraction pattern. Over that, a
specular radial-gradient blob follows the pointer under mix-blend-mode: screen, scaled by
the gloss slider. Over that again, a one-pixel repeating linear-gradient at half opacity
with overlay gives the printed stock its tooth.

Five foil patterns — linear, cross, radial, lenticular and shatter — each a different
stack of repeating-linear, repeating-conic and conic gradients, at the number of lines
per unit a real screen would use. Emboss drops the same layer to overlay at a quarter
opacity, which is what embossing does to a foil: it stops glinting and starts reading as
part of the stock.

Panel: five pattern chips with captions, a tilt slider (0–30 degrees), a gloss slider
(0–100), Emboss, and Spin the press which turns the card continuously on a slow
Lissajous so the foil does under a lamp what it does under a moving eye. Readout: current
angle, elevation, foil name, gloss and finish.

Layout: nav, centred hero, a two-column stage of card and panel, a readout row, two
three-cell note rows on the grid, the blend, the tilt, the shine, embossed and stock,
then a footer. One column under 900px, links hidden under 780px.

Interaction: move the pointer across the stage and the card tilts with the foil offset
against the rotation, which is what makes the colour sweep. Choose a foil to change the
screen; move tilt to change how far it swings; move gloss to change how much specular
the lamination gives; press Emboss to flatten the foil into the stock; press Spin the
press to let the card turn itself.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A holographic foil card on a dark print-shop page: a 5:7 panel inside a perspective stage
that tilts on two axes to the pointer, with the printed art under three stacked repeating
gradients composited with color-dodge and offset by the same variables that drive the
tilt, a specular blob under a screen blend, and a one-pixel stock gradient over the top —
five switchable foil screens, a tilt slider, a gloss slider, an Emboss mode that drops
the foil to overlay, and a spin mode that turns the card itself.

## Major visual techniques used
- Three CSS custom properties (--mx, --my, --rx, --ry, --shine) as the single source of
  truth for tilt, foil offset and specular position
- Real 3D with perspective on the stage, rotateX/rotateY on the card and
  transform-style preserve-3d, rather than a skew fake
- Foil as stacked repeating-linear and repeating-conic gradients at print-screen
  densities, composited with mix-blend-mode color-dodge so it adds light
- The same custom properties used inside the foil's background-position, so the foil
  moves against the tilt exactly as a diffraction grid does
- Specular blob as a radial-gradient under mix-blend-mode screen whose opacity is the
  gloss slider
- Printed stock as a one-pixel repeating-linear-gradient under overlay, for substrate
  tooth without a texture
- isolation: isolate on the card and the stack so the blends contain themselves
- Five foils as arrays of gradient strings joined into one background-image, so a
  pattern change is one property write
- Emboss as a class that swaps the blend mode and drops the opacity, which is the
  physical difference between foiled and embossed stock
- Spin driven off the frame delta on a Lissajous pair, so the turn is smooth and
  cheap, and pointer input takes over the moment it comes back
- Angle and elevation read back from the same numbers that generate the transform,
  so the readout cannot disagree with the picture
- Card width in min() with an aspect-ratio, so the panel keeps its proportion at any
  viewport without a resize handler

## Intended interaction model
A card you turn over in the light. Visitors move the pointer across the stage to tilt the
card and sweep the diffraction pattern, pick a foil to change the screen, move tilt and
gloss to change how physical it looks, emboss it into the stock, or press Spin the press
and watch it do the same thing on its own under a lamp.
