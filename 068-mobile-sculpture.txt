# MOBILE — a sculpture that answers the room

## Short title
Mobile Sculpture

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a hanging mobile built in the
DOM, where every arm is an integrated pendulum rather than a timed animation, and where
the air current is a control. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web
fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline
by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: sheet metal on cream. Ground #f2efe6, room a cream gradient with an
inset shadow, ink #26241f, vermilion #c0392b and teal #1f5f6e as the two accents with
ochre #d8a326 and black for the metal surfaces. Serif headings with italic accent words,
uppercase .1em–.4em kickers, monospace readouts.

The sculpture is nested DOM, not canvas. Each arm is a pivot — an absolutely positioned
div at a coordinate inside its parent — containing a rod that rotates about its own
origin. Because the rotation lives on the rod and the position lives on the pivot, a
child arm inherits its parent's rotation for free through the cascade, which is the whole
mechanism of a real mobile. The bar is a 3px rounded rule; shapes hang from strings below
it at fractions of its length.

Three layouts, so Rehang gives a genuinely different piece: a four-branch one, a
five-surface one and a six-surface one with an extra level. Surfaces are discs, squares,
triangles (clip-path) and half-discs in the four Calder colours.

Each arm is integrated, not animated: restoring torque from gravity and its own length,
plus a forcing term that pushes it at its own frequency and phase so the piece never
repeats a pose, with damping applied as a per-frame power of the damping factor. Touching
a surface adds angular momentum to its arm — a click pushes it one way, a drag keeps
adding — and that momentum then propagates down the branch.

Controls: air current, gravity and damping sliders, plus A breeze (a gust and an
impulse to every arm), Still air (wind to zero, so the piece dies) and Rehang. Readout:
arms, surfaces, the largest current angle in degrees, and a rough energy figure summed
from angle times angular velocity.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the room with the sculpture hanging from a cord, a dock with
the three sliders and three buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on the
pivot, the period, the air, balance, colour and the touch, then a footer.

Interaction: the piece is already moving. Visitors drag the air current and watch the
rhythm change, drag the gravity to make everything faster or slower, kill the damping to
let it run, click or drag a surface to send a branch swinging, take a gust, or rehang the
whole thing into a different composition.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A hanging mobile in nested DOM where every arm is an integrated pendulum — gravity and
its own length set the restoring torque, an air current forces it at its own frequency,
and damping bleeds it off — hung from a pivot inside its parent so a child inherits its
parent's rotation for free, with clickable and draggable surfaces that add angular
momentum to their branch, three rehangable layouts in Calder's four-muted-primary
palette, and air, gravity and damping sliders plus a gust and a still-air button.

## Major visual techniques used
- Pivot-and-rod split, so translation and rotation live on different elements and
  nesting supplies the inheritance a real mobile relies on
- Children positioned as absolute coordinates inside the parent rod, so the whole tree
  is written once and only rotation is ever updated
- Shapes hung from strings whose lengths are a fraction of the surface size, so small
  things hang close and large things hang low
- Discs, squares, half-discs and a clip-path triangle as the four surface types, all
  flat colour with no shadows
- Integration rather than animation: restoring torque from gravity over arm length, a
  forcing term at per-arm frequency and phase, and damping as a power of the damping
  factor so it is frame-rate independent
- Phase-randomised forcing per arm, which is what stops the piece from ever repeating a
  pose
- Click and drag both add angular momentum to the arm that owns the surface, so input
  enters the simulation rather than overriding it
- Three layout functions returning different trees, chosen by an index so Rehang
  cycles compositions
- A 3px rounded bar and a 1px hairline string, which are the only two marks needed to
  make a metal rod and a thread
- Energy readout summed from angle times angular velocity across all arms, so the
  number is a property of the simulation
- Cream room with an inset shadow, so the sculpture reads as hung in a real space
- Shorter arms on lower branches, which is what keeps the composition inside the room

## Intended interaction model
Something to disturb. The mobile runs on its own the moment the page opens; visitors
change the air, the gravity and the damping, take a gust, let the air go still and watch
it stop, or grab a surface and swing a branch. Rehang rebuilds the piece as a different
composition with the same physics, and every reading in the panel comes from the same
integrator that moves the rods.
