# KLAVIER — A Room With 88 Keys

## Short title
Piano Keyboard

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a playable piano
keyboard instrument called KLAVIER. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN,
no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries.
All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid
HTML5. No audio samples are used: every note is synthesised with the Web Audio API.

Artistic direction: a dark wood parlour. The ground is near-black brown #14100d
with a warm radial glow at the top; the instrument itself is a wood-gradient
cabinet (light wood #5a4128 into #3a2a1c into near-black) with a rounded top, an
inset highlight, a hatched "fall board" strip above the keys, a strip of red felt
below them, and a gold highlight that fires at the exact x position of whichever
key you strike. White keys are ivory gradients (ivory #f6f1e4 into a shadow
#ded7c6) with three-sided inset shadows so each key has a shaded edge and a lifted
top; black keys are two-thirds height, 62% of a white key's width, centred on the
boundary, in a near-black vertical gradient with a faint bottom highlight, and sit
on z-index 3 above the whites.

The synthesis: the AudioContext is created on the first interaction (browser
policy). Each note connects to a per-voice gain, then to a master gain into a
DynamicsCompressorNode, which feeds both the destination and a reverb send — a
ConvolverNode loaded with an impulse response generated at runtime by filling a
1.8-second stereo buffer with noise decaying as (1 − i/len)^2.2. Each voice is four
sine oscillators at 1×, 2×, 3× and 4.2× the fundamental, each with its own gain
and its own exponentially decaying envelope — higher partials die faster, which is
what makes it read as struck rather than buzzed. The overall envelope is a 6 ms
attack to a velocity-scaled peak, a fall to 35% over 140 ms, then an exponential
release whose length comes from the Release slider.

The keyboard: one to five octaves built from C3, each white key positioned by
percentage width and each black key centred on its boundary, labelled with note
names in an 8px monospace. Keys flash a pressed state (translateY 2px, darker
gradient, inset shadow) on strike. Input comes from three sources — pointer down,
pointer enter while dragging (so you can slide across the keys), and the computer
keyboard, mapped as A W S E D F T G Y H U J K O L across two octaves with the black
keys on the W E T Y U O cluster. The demo button plays a preset figure at 230 ms
intervals and can be stopped mid-way.

Layout: a nav with a serif brand at 0.42em tracking. A centred hero with a gold
eyebrow, a serif headline with one italic gold word, and a paragraph about
synthesis. Then the instrument: the wood cabinet with the fall board, keys and felt
strip. Then a glass control tray with three sliders (Octaves 1–5, Release 10–90,
Reverb 0–100) plus a figure-playback toggle and a sustain toggle. Then a
three-cell column of large gold serif numerals (3 strings per note, 52 white and
36 black, 230 strings in total) with explanatory paragraphs. Then a bordered pull
quote about touch. Then a ruled monospace footer.

Interaction: strike keys with the mouse, touch or keyboard; slide across them; choose
how many octaves are visible; set the release length and reverb amount; toggle
sustain off to make every note damp itself; or press "Play a figure" and watch the
keys play themselves, with the gold fall-board flash tracking each strike. Key
labels hide below 560px.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A dark wood parlour instrument: a CSS-built keyboard cabinet with ivory and ebony
keys, a hatched fall board, red felt and a gold flash that fires wherever you
strike, over a Web Audio synthesis of four decaying sine partials per note with a
runtime-generated reverb — playable by mouse, touch, sliding drag or the computer
keyboard.

## Major visual techniques used
- Wood cabinet from a multi-stop vertical gradient with an inset top highlight
- Ivory keys from a two-stop gradient with three-sided inset shadows (edge shadow,
  bottom shade, lifted top) and a drop shadow
- Ebony keys at 62% height and 62% width, centred on the white-key boundary, on a
  higher z-index
- Percentage-positioned keys so the keyboard scales with the container
- Pressed key states: translateY(2px), darker gradient, inset shadow, 40ms transition
- Hatched fall board from a repeating-linear-gradient with an inset shadow
- Gold flash bar positioned by the struck key's bounding rectangle
- Red felt strip with a coloured box-shadow bloom
- Web Audio synthesis: four sine partials per voice with individual exponentially
  decaying envelopes, so higher partials die faster
- Runtime-generated convolution reverb from a decaying-noise stereo impulse buffer
- Per-voice gain with a 6 ms attack, 140 ms fall to 35%, and a slider-controlled
  exponential release
- DynamicsCompressorNode on the master bus
- Computer-keyboard mapping across two octaves (A–L naturals, W E T Y U O sharps)
  with key-repeat suppression via a held map
- Slide-across-keys playing via pointerenter while a button is held
- Slider controls for octave count, release and reverb; sustain and figure-playback
  toggles
- Serif display at 0.42em tracking against 8.5px tracked monospace labels
- Instrument layout with glass control tray; labels hidden under 560px, reading
  columns collapsing under 880px

## Intended interaction model
An instrument. Visitors strike keys with a mouse, a finger, or the A-to-L row of
their keyboard, slide across the keys to glissando, and shape the sound with octave
count, release and reverb sliders plus a sustain toggle — or hand the playing over
to the built-in figure and watch the keys move themselves. Nothing needs to be
submitted and there is no score to complete.
