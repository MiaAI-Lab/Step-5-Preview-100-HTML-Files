Six wings,
drawn from numbers

Nothing in this case is an image. Each wing is a cubic path, each band an arc clipped to it, each eyespot a pair of rings — and the beat of every wing is its own number, so no two insects flap alike.

The Collection

i

Wings

Two beziers for the forewing, three for the hindwing, a tail where the species has one. Scale the control points and the whole silhouette changes without redrawing anything.

ii

Bands

Arcs are drawn through the wing and then clipped by it, so a band always follows the outline. Count and radius are per species, never shared.

iii

Eyespots

Concentric rings with a pale pupil, clipped to the hindwing and offset toward the margin. They are the reason predators aim for the wrong end.

iv

Beat

Wingbeat is a per-card number written into a custom property. Rest it and the wings stop mid-cycle; release it and they leave the case entirely.

v

Host

A larva eats one plant family and nothing else, so the specimen card names its host before it names its colours.

vi

Paper

The case is a cream ground with hairlines and cinnabar, the way a specimen drawer is labelled — nothing here needs a dark room to be legible.
