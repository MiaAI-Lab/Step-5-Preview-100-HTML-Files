# THE WHEEL — a pot from one profile

## Short title
Pottery Atelier

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a potter's wheel where a vessel
is stored as a profile of radii at even heights, drawn as a revolved silhouette, glazed and
fired. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no
JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive,
valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a warm studio. Ground #efe9e0, canvas a cream radial gradient,
hairlines rgba(47,42,36,.2), ink #2f2a24 with muted #6a5f52, terracotta #b4552d as the
accent and a leaf green #4a6b52. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase
.2em–.4em kickers, monospace figures.

The pot is a Float32Array of twenty-two radii at even heights — exactly what a turner reads
off the wheel. The drawing is the revolution: the profile is walked down the right side and
back up the left to make a silhouette, filled with a horizontal gradient that is light at
the left edge, body colour at the centre and darkest at the right, which is the projection
a pot actually makes. The mouth is an ellipse, the interior is the profile pulled inward by
the wall thickness and filled with its own gradient, and two faint throwing rings sit on the
wall where a potter's fingers were.

Glazes: tenmoku, celadon, shino, copper red and clear. Each has a body colour, a shadow
colour and an edge colour, and each is mixed toward its shadow by the firing level; the
clear glaze shows the clay underneath instead. Firing slider moves the palette from cone
06 to cone 10.

Controls: five glaze swatches as aria-pressed buttons, firing, wall thickness, spin, Bowl,
Bottle, Cylinder and Collapse — which sags every radius toward the middle and turns the
vessel into a puddle. Dragging the profile itself moves a single radius, so a visitor can
pull a neck out or push a belly in.

Readout: profile points, height, maximum radius, and volume — computed by summing discs of
the interior radius, so a thicker wall really does give a smaller volume.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the wheel canvas, a dock of swatches, sliders and four shape
buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on the profile, the revolution, the
wall, glaze, firing and collapse, then a footer.

Interaction: the wheel is already turning. Visitors switch shapes to see how one rotation
gives three completely different vessels, drag the profile to pull a neck or a belly out by
hand, change the glaze and the firing to see the same pot in five surfaces, set the wall
thickness and watch the volume fall, and press Collapse to ruin it.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A potter's wheel where a vessel is twenty-two radii at even heights, revolved into a
silhouette with a left-to-right gradient, a mouth ellipse, an interior pulled in by the wall
thickness and a volume computed from discs of that interior — with five glazes (tenmoku,
celadon, shino, copper red, clear) each mixed toward its shadow by a firing level, Bowl,
Bottle, Cylinder and Collapse presets, a spin slider, and a draggable profile that changes
one radius at a time.

## Major visual techniques used
- Profile as a typed array of radii at even heights, walked down one side and up the other
  to build the silhouette, so the whole vessel is one data structure
- Horizontal gradient across the body — light at the left, body colour at the centre,
  darkest at the right — which is what a revolved surface really looks like
- Mouth drawn as an ellipse with a dark interior, so the vessel has an inside
- Interior as the same profile pulled inward by the wall thickness, which makes wall
  thickness visible and also computes the volume
- Volume by discs of the interior radius, so the number and the drawing agree
- Glaze as three colours mixed by the firing level, with the clear glaze falling through
  to the clay
- Firing slider mapped to cone numbers (06 up to 10), so the readout is the real
  vocabulary
- Draggable profile points, with the dragged one drawn larger and in the accent colour
- Spin as motion arcs whose length and alpha follow the spin slider, so the wheel reads
  as turning even when nothing else moves
- Collapse as a sag toward a common radius, which is literally what a wall does
- Shadow as an ellipse scaled to the base radius, so the pot sits on the wheel
- A parse that accepts both hex and rgb strings, so a mixed colour can be mixed again
  without breaking

## Intended interaction model
A wheel to throw on. Visitors pick a shape to see how one rotation gives three different
vessels, drag the profile to pull the form out by hand, choose a glaze and a firing to see
the same pot in another surface, set the wall and watch the volume drop, and collapse it
when they have gone too far. Every number in the panel is computed from the same profile
the drawing uses.
