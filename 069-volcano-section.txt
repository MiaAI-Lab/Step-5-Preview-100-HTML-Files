# THE VENT — a volcano that builds pressure

## Short title
Volcano Section

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a cross-section of an active
volcano with a chamber, a conduit and a vent, where pressure builds and releases and the
lava that comes out sets into rock on the flanks. No external dependencies whatsoever —
no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline,
works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: basalt at night. Ground #120f0e, canvas #0a0808, hairlines
rgba(240,231,226,.14), body #f0e7e2 with muted #a89a93, magma orange #ff8a3c with hot
amber #ffd05c and a warning #ff5a2a. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase
.1em–.4em kickers, monospace readouts and diagram labels.

The mountain is a profile from a seeded walk — a cone with a crater at the centre — and
everything is drawn against it with a groundY function that interpolates the profile, so
lava, bombs and labels all know where the surface is. Under it a chamber glows with a
radial gradient whose size and brightness follow how full it is, and the conduit is a
thick translucent line from the chamber to the vent.

The physics: pressure rises toward a set point set by how full the chamber is and how
much gas is dissolved, and when it passes one the vent opens for a length set by the gas
content, throwing bombs, ash and lava, and the pressure drops to a third. Lava particles
run downhill by sampling the slope of the profile either side of them, cool at a rate set
by the cooling slider, and when they stop they set: their trail is kept as rock and the
particle is dropped, so the edifice grows outward and stays built. Bombs are ballistic and
land as blocks. Ash is drawn as soft radial puffs that fade with their remaining life.

Controls: viscosity (how fast lava runs and how sticky the melt is), gas (how much is
dissolved and therefore how violent), cooling (how fast lava sets), Force the vent (a
pressure bump), Go quiet (drain the gas and seal), and clicking the mountain, which adds
magma to the chamber.

Readout: pressure as a percentage that turns hot above eighty, chamber fullness, the
number of eruptions, total lava length in metres summed from all live flows, and the
state — quiet or erupting.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the section canvas with a diagram caption, a dock with three
sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on the chamber,
viscosity, cooling, ash, seismic and quiet, then a footer.

Interaction: the system runs on its own and vents on its own schedule. Visitors click the
mountain to keep the chamber topped up, force the vent to see it now, set the viscosity
low to watch runny lava travel right down the flanks and set into a broad shield, or set
it high to see short sticky flows that never leave the summit. Go quiet stops everything.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A volcano cross-section on canvas where a seeded cone profile gives every particle a real
ground to land on, a magma chamber fills under a conduit, pressure builds toward a set
point driven by chamber fullness and dissolved gas until it exceeds one and the vent opens
— throwing ballistic bombs, fading ash and lava that runs downhill by sampling the
profile's slope, cools, and sets into rock that stays on the flanks — with viscosity, gas
and cooling sliders, a click that adds magma, and a readout of pressure, chamber fullness,
eruption count and total lava length.

## Major visual techniques used
- Mountain profile generated once from a seeded walk and interpolated by a groundY
  function, so every particle and label shares one definition of the surface
- Slope sampled either side of a lava particle to get its downhill direction, which is
  what makes the flow follow the mountain rather than a scripted path
- Lava cooling as a heat term that only drains once the particle has stopped moving, so
  fast flows travel before they crust over
- Set lava: the trail is transferred into a rock layer and the particle dropped, so the
  edifice accumulates instead of flickering
- Ash as radial gradients composited with 'lighter' and faded by remaining life
- Bombs as ballistic particles with a velocity streak, landing as small blocks
- Chamber and vent glow as radial gradients whose alpha follows fullness and venting
- Sky heated by the eruption by mixing the top gradient stop with the heat term
- Tremor line whose amplitude is the pressure, drawn as a sine along the bottom of the
  frame
- Diagram labels in monospace placed on the chamber, conduit and vent, so the picture
  reads as a section rather than a scene
- Eruption state surfaced once, in one place, and cleared when venting ends
- Fixed canvas resolution with CSS scaling, so the profile's shape holds at any width

## Intended interaction model
Something you feed and then watch fail. Visitors click the mountain to keep magma coming,
drag the gas up to make the next one violent, drop the viscosity to let lava run all the
way down, or force the vent and see whether the lid held. Go quiet lets it all relax, and
every number in the panel is the number the drawing is using.
