# CLOCKWORK — brass, steel and patience

## Short title
Clockwork

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a watch movement on a dark
plate, with a real gear train whose wheels are geometrically correct and whose mesh is
computed rather than drawn by hand. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no
web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works
offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: an enamel-black bench with brass on it. Ground #0a0c0f, panels
#11151a, hairlines rgba(233,228,216,.12), body text #e9e4d8 with muted #a39d8c, brass
#e0b063 as the one accent, and blued steel #4b6fa8 for the hands. Serif display type
throughout with uppercase .2em–.42em track kickers and eyebrow rules.

The movement: an SVG on a viewBox 620 × 460 containing six wheels, each generated at
load as a real tooth profile. The module (tooth size) is fixed at 5 user units, so a
wheel's pitch radius is exactly module × teeth / 2, its addendum radius one module
outside that and its dedendum radius 1.15 modules inside. Each wheel is drawn three
ways over itself: the toothed outline filled with a linear gradient (brass or steel),
then the same outline reused as a clip path so four spokes cut through the centre and
stop dead at the rim, then a hub with a ruby jewel in the middle.

The mesh is the point. Each wheel knows its parent and the angle of the line of
centres to it, and its rotation is solved as
angle_self = -(n_parent / n_self) * (angle_parent - phi) + phi + PI - step*0.75,
which is the standard external-gear relation with the half-tooth phase term that puts
a tooth of one wheel into a gap of the other instead of tooth against tooth. Over the
wheels sit three static finger bridges in blued steel with counter-sunk screws, and
over the centre wheel two hands dividing at 60:1 for the minutes and 12:1 for the
hours. A balance wheel with a spiral hairspring oscillates on its own sine.

Panel: a row per wheel with teeth count and a live rpm figure, an impulses slider that
sets the balance frequency, a Wind button that raises spring torque and lets it bleed
back to 1 the way a mainspring does, and a case-back button that fades the plate and
brings up a CSS rosette. Then two three-cell rows of notes on the train, the escapement,
the hands, jewels, torque and the case back, then a ruled footer naming the tooth counts.

Interaction: the train runs from the moment the page opens. The impulses slider changes
how fast the balance swings; winding makes everything speed up and then slowly relax
back to the base rate; opening the case back dims the movement and reveals the rosette.
The rpm readout follows the same ratio arithmetic as the gears, so it goes up and down
with the spring and inverts across the ratios.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A watch movement on a black plate where six wheels with real tooth profiles turn in
pairs at the ratios their tooth counts demand — the phase of every mesh is solved from
the line of centres so teeth fall into gaps — overlaid with steel bridges and two
hands, while a balance wheel with a hairspring oscillates beside it, a Wind button
charges the spring and lets it bleed away, and a per-wheel readout reports live rpm.

## Major visual techniques used
- Tooth profile generated as a path from a fixed module, so pitch radius, addendum
  and dedendum are all derived rather than guessed
- The gear outline reused as a clip path so the four spokes are cut by the rim
  exactly instead of overflowing it
- Brass and steel as linear gradients with a hard dark stroke on the tooth flanks
- External gear relation solved per wheel from the parent's angle and the line of
  centres, including the half-tooth phase that alternates tooth against gap
- Recursive tree of wheels so each one inherits its driver's speed and direction
- Motion work divisions applied on top: minute hand at 60× , hour hand at 12×
- Balance wheel drawn as a rim, a cross, a hub and a spiral hairspring, oscillating
  on a sine whose frequency is the impulses slider
- Static finger bridges with counter-sunk screws laid over the train to give the
  plate its depth
- Mainspring model: winding raises torque, and a decay term bleeds it back to 1
- Case back as a CSS rosette built from repeating-conic-gradient with ::before and
  ::after cut-down discs, revealed by fading the plate behind it
- Gem reds used only on pivot jewels, so a single hue carries the accent role
- Two-column case collapsing to one column under 900px, menu links hidden under 780px

## Intended interaction model
Something to wind and watch. The train is already turning when the page loads; the
impulses slider changes the beat, the Wind button speeds the whole movement up and
then lets it fall back, and the case-back button slides the rosette in over the plate.
Every number in the panel is computed from the same tooth counts that drive the
drawing, so the readout cannot disagree with what is on screen.
