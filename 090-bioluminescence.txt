# PLANKTON — a bay that lights up when you touch it

## Short title
Bioluminescence

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a bay of dinoflagellates that
flash when disturbed and never otherwise, with the flash spreading as a wavefront through
the bloom. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images,
no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking,
responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a black bay. Ground #04070e, canvas #02040a, hairlines
rgba(230,240,255,.14), body #e6f0ff with muted #8d9ab8, aqua #5ff0d0 as the accent and a
cold blue #8fbcff for the figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase
.2em–.4em kickers, monospace figures.

The bloom is a set of cells, each with a position, a drift and a lit value between zero
and one. Nothing is drawn glowing until a cell's lit value is positive: the body of the
bloom is a dull brown scatter that you would never notice.

A disturbance is a wavefront — a circle that expands at a speed set by the spread slider,
whose life decays — and it lights every cell it crosses, within a band of sixteen pixels
either side of its radius. So the flash is not a picture of a wave, it is a wave: the cells
it has reached are bright and the rest are dark, and the boundary between them moves
outward. The cells at the point of contact light immediately.

Four species, each with its real emission wavelength in nanometres, its own quantum yield,
and its own tint: Lingulodinium at 474, Noctiluca at 470, Pyrocystis at 476, Vargula at
464, Periphylla at 465. Four waters — calm bay, rippled, surf and moonlit — each with its
own current and its own level of ambient glow, and the moonlit one draws a moon.

The species' wavelength is also drawn: a real spectrum bar computed from wavelength to
RGB through the standard piecewise approximation, so 474 nm really is blue and the species
label under it is the number the bar was centred on.

Controls: five species, four waters, cell density, spread, afterglow, Strike a paddle and
Still water. Readout: cells lit, total cells, wavelength, species, quantum yield and
whether the bay is currently lit or merely mechanical.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the bay canvas with its caption, a dock of two chip rows, three
sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on the chemistry, the
trigger, blue, the bloom, afterglow and still water, then a footer.

Interaction: the bay is dark and stays dark. Drag through it and every cell you touch
lights, and the front spreads outward at the speed you set; strike a paddle for a single
impulse; raise the density until the bay is visibly brown in the daylight and bright blue
in the flash; switch the species and the tint and the wavelength change together; and press
Still water to put it back to black.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A dark bay of density-controlled cells that are drawn as a dull brown scatter until a
disturbance expands a wavefront through them at a spread-set speed, lighting every cell
within sixteen pixels of its radius so the flash is the boundary between lit and unlit
cells moving outward — with five species at their real emission wavelengths and quantum
yields, each with its own tint, four waters with different currents and ambient glow, a
real wavelength-to-RGB spectrum bar, and a drag that acts as a hand through the water.

## Major visual techniques used
- Cells lit by an expanding wavefront band, so the flash is a real propagating front rather
  than a painted glow
- Body of the bloom drawn as a dull brown scatter, so the unlit state is genuinely dark
- Additive radial gradients per lit cell with a white core into the species' tint, which is
  what a point source in water looks like
- Cell emission decaying per cell at a rate set by the afterglow slider
- Wavefronts also drawn as thin bright rings, so the front is visible even in sparse water
- Five species as data: wavelength, quantum yield and tint, so the readout and the drawing
  change together
- Wavelength-to-RGB through the standard piecewise approximation, so the spectrum bar is
  real and 474 nm really is blue
- Four waters with different currents and ambient glow, including a moonlit one
- Cells drifting on the water's current and wrapping at the edges, so the bloom is alive
  even when nothing is lit
- Point of contact lighting immediately plus the expanding front, which is the two-stage
  flash a real disturbance produces
- Still water clearing the waves and the lit cells, so the bay can be returned to black
- Aqua accent on a near-black ground, so the only saturated colour in the page is the light
  itself

## Intended interaction model
Water you disturb. Visitors drag a hand through the bay and the cells they touch light,
then the front spreads outward and lights the rest; they strike a paddle for one impulse,
set the density so the bloom is thick or thin, set the spread so the front is fast or slow,
choose a species and see both the tint and the wavelength change, and press Still water to
put the bay back to black. Nothing glows until something touches it.
