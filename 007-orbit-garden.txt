# ORBIT GARDEN — Plant a Body

## Short title
Orbit Garden

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive n-body
gravity sandbox called ORBIT GARDEN. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN,
no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries.
All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking the file, responsive,
valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a quiet, luminous instrument for playing with gravity. Deep
void background (#04040c → #0b0a1e) painted once into an offscreen canvas, with
two large soft nebula washes (violet top-left, teal bottom-right) and a sparse
star field of varying alpha. A warm amber star pulses at the centre with a
nine-times-radius radial glow. Bodies are little glossy spheres — radial gradients
offset toward a light source, with an HSL hue each and a thin luminous halo ring —
and they leave long comet trails: one soft full-length polyline stroke plus a
brighter short head stroke, both in their own hue.

Physics: a real pairwise n-body integrator. Every body is accelerated by every
other body and by the central star using G·m/r², positions and velocities are
integrated once per frame, bodies that touch merge (conserving momentum and mass,
growing in radius as the cube root of mass), and bodies that fly far off-screen are
culled. Seeding drops three to five planets at increasing radii with near-circular
initial velocities.

Layout: a full-bleed canvas with a floating glass control panel pinned to the
bottom centre, a typographic HUD top-left (a light-weight 200 sans with one
gradient-clipped word), and a large monospace body count top-right.

Typography: one system sans used at weight 200 for the display line against 9px
wide-tracked monospace for every label, value and count — the contrast of a huge
low-contrast headline with tiny mechanical type is the whole typographic idea.

Interaction: press anywhere and drag. While dragging a dashed amber aim line with
an arrowhead shows the impulse you are about to impart — length equals speed,
direction equals heading — and a dot marks the release point. Let go and a body is
born with that velocity; too slow and it spirals into the star, just right and it
settles into an ellipse, too fast and it escapes forever. Sliders set the new
body's mass (0.1–4.0), the gravitational constant (0.10–2.60) and the trail
length in samples. "Seed a system" clears and plants a fresh stable arrangement;
"Clear" empties the sky. All input is pointer-based with pointer capture, so it
works identically with touch and mouse. The canvas is DPR-aware and the background
is repainted on resize.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A dark, glowing gravity toy: a pulsing amber star at the centre of a nebula-washed
void, into which the visitor flings glossy, hue-coded planets that trace long
comet tails, merge on contact and obey a real pairwise n-body integration —
controlled by a single floating glass tray with three sliders and two buttons.

## Major visual techniques used
- Offscreen background canvas painted once (gradient + nebula washes + star field)
  and blitted each frame for a cheap, stable backdrop
- Pairwise n-body gravity with momentum-conserving merges and cube-root radius
  from mass
- Comet trails from a flat coordinate array, drawn as one soft full-length stroke
  plus a brighter short head stroke
- Glossy spheres from offset radial gradients plus a thin halo ring
- Nine-times-radius radial star glow with a low-frequency pulse
- Dashed aim line with a rotated triangular arrowhead showing the drag impulse
- Floating glass control tray with backdrop-filter blur
- Extreme typographic contrast: 200-weight display sans vs 9px tracked monospace
- Gradient-clipped single word inside a light headline (background-clip:text)
- Pointer events with setPointerCapture for unified mouse/touch dragging
- DPR-aware canvas sizing with debounced resize rebuild

## Intended interaction model
A physics toy with a single gesture. The visitor presses, drags to aim, and
releases to launch; the aim line makes the consequence visible before committing.
Sliders change the rules for the next launch, and the two buttons offer a preset
system or an empty sky. Nothing else competes for attention — the page is a
sandbox that keeps running on its own once seeded.
