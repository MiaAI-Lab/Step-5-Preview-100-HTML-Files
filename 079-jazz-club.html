i

The changes

Twelve bars of a blues in B♭: the same four bars twice, a turnaround, and the same three again. The chart does not move; the solo over it never repeats.

ii

Tension

Tonic, third, fifth and seventh of the chord are safe. Anything else is a colour, and the more of them you stack the further the line is from the chord underneath it, which is the number in the corner.

iii

Chromatic

The chromatic slider is how often the walk steps outside the scale. At zero it is a nursery rhyme; at the top it is a line that is only a line because you keep going.

iv

Spotlight

The soloist is a silhouette in a cone of smoke, and the cone's width and the smoke's drift are what make a small drawing read as a room with air in it.

v

The staff

Notes are written onto a ruled staff as the bar passes, left to right, and cleared at the next chorus. It is the same data the drawing uses, not a decoration.

vi

A chorus

One chorus is twelve bars, which is one trip round the changes. Take another and the chart resets, the key stays, and the line is different.
