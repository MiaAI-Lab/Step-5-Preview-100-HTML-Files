# WARDIAN CASE — a closed world you can keep

## Short title
Terrarium

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a sealed Wardian case that
runs as a small closed ecosystem, with the water cycle made visible on the glass and
every meter derived from the same simulation. No external dependencies whatsoever — no
CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline,
works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a dark greenhouse. Ground #0f1512, panel #131a15, hairlines
rgba(226,236,223,.14), body #e2ecdf with muted #9db09b, one green accent #8fd694 and a
pale lime #c8e6a0 for figures. Serif headings, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers, and roman
numerals on the note cells.

The case: a canvas at 760 × 900 inside a rounded jar div with a brass lid drawn as a
three-stop linear gradient. The air is a vertical gradient whose top colour is driven
by the light slider, plus a radial sun shaft composited in 'lighter' so the light level
actually changes the picture. The soil is a sine-folded silhouette with leaf-litter
speckle, and the plants are random spines of twenty-six segments that grow segment by
segment, sprouting alternating leaves along whatever has grown so far; their hue slides
toward yellow under stress. Fruit sits on the soil with a shadow and a leaf stem.

The water cycle: droplets spawn high on the glass at a rate set by the light level,
grow while they are in the upper half, accelerate under gravity scaled by their own
size, and are removed at the soil line where they add moisture back. So the case
literally rains on itself, and the rain stops when you turn the light down.

Flies walk the glass: hunger drives them toward the fruit, they eat, they die of
starvation or age and are composted back into moisture, and they reproduce when they
are well fed — so population follows food rather than a clock.

Panel: pour water, drop in fruit, release a fly, start a new case, a light slider, and
five meters (moisture, oxygen, leaf area, population, day). Layout: nav, centred hero,
a two-column bench of jar and panel, then two three-cell note rows (water cycle, growth,
mouths, balance, brass, time), then a ruled footer. One column under 900px, links hidden
under 780px.

Interaction: the case runs on its own. Visitors pull the light slider and watch the air
change and the rain speed up or stop, pour water to restore moisture, drop fruit to
feed a population, release flies and watch the oxygen meter fall, and start a new case
when the balance is beyond saving. One second here is a quarter hour in the case, and
the day counter is what the meters are measured against.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A sealed Wardian case running as a closed ecosystem on canvas: plants grow segment by
segment toward a light you control with a slider that also tints the air and drives the
rain of condensation droplets on the glass, moisture evaporates and returns, flies walk
the interior, eat the fruit, die and are composted into the soil, and five meters —
moisture, oxygen, leaf area, population and day — report the same numbers the drawing
is built from.

## Major visual techniques used
- Air gradient whose top colour is computed from the light slider, so the control
  changes the whole picture rather than a readout
- Radial sun shaft composited with globalCompositeOperation 'lighter'
- Soil silhouette from summed sines instead of a straight line
- Plant growth as progressive draw of a pre-generated spine, so growth is a length
  change rather than a new random shape each frame
- Leaves as paired quadratic bladders along the grown part of the spine, alternating
  side by index parity
- Hue slide toward yellow under stress, computed from the same stress value that
  drives growth
- Water cycle as a particle system: spawn rate from light, growth in the upper half,
  gravity scaled by radius, absorption at the soil line returning moisture
- Sun shaft and vignette as radial gradients rather than filters
- Flies drawn as a body plus translucent wing strokes whose opacity oscillates
- Population as a consequence of food and hunger, not a spawn timer
- Dead flies composted into moisture, closing the loop rather than just vanishing
- Canvas sized once in markup and scaled by CSS, so the aspect ratio holds at any
  width without a resize handler
- Meters written to the DOM at the end of each frame, in the same order they are
  computed

## Intended interaction model
A thing to keep alive. Visitors set the light, pour water, add fruit and release flies,
then watch the meters tell them whether the case is still working — the rain stops when
the light stops, the plants yellow when the moisture runs out, and the population
follows whatever fruit is left. Nothing is scheduled to happen; everything on screen is
a consequence of the few numbers the panel exposes.
