An ice cream is
mostly air

A scoop is a foam: a lattice of ice crystals, fat and milk solids with air whipped through it. The air is a quarter of the volume by law and up to a half by practice, and it is the reason a scoop can be lighter than the cream that made it.

i

Overrun

The extra volume the churn whips in, as a percentage of the mix. At zero the scoop is the density of cream; at a hundred it is half air and half everything else, and the same scoop weighs a third less.

ii

Freezing

Ice cream is scoopable between minus sixteen and minus twelve. Pulled colder it is a brick; left warmer it melts and the foam collapses, and the air leaves for good.

iii

The scoop

A scoop is a hemisphere with a curl, and the curl is what makes it come away from the tub cleanly. Drag it out and the weight on the scale falls as the air holds it up.

iv

The ticket

Weight, volume, air volume, mix volume and price are all computed from the scoop size and the overrun. A bigger scoop is not a heavier one once the air is in.

v

Nutrition

The bars are the mix, not the air: fat, sugar and protein per hundred grams of the mix itself, so they do not change with the overrun — only the weight does.

vi

Cones

Onto a cone drops the scoop onto a waffle and adds one to the count. The cone is drawn with a lattice and a rolled rim, because that is what it is.
