Penrose stairs · impossible geometry · non-euclidean portals

You have been walking
for a very long time

Every door you go through puts you back where you started, a little further on. The stairs always rise and never arrive. The light is always the same distance ahead. This is the corridor: it is drawn honestly, room by room, and it does not close.

i

The Penrose stairs

Four flights, each turning a quarter, each rising as it turns — so that walking them you climb forever and rise a total of zero. The illusion is drawn here as a loop of four segments whose projected rises never sum, which is exactly how the original was made.

ii

Two-point perspective

A corridor is two vanishing points on one horizon. Rotate the view and both slide along it, and the walls stop being parallel — which is the only honest way to draw a room that has no outside.

iii

A portal that does not go anywhere

Every door on this corridor is a frame into the same room, offset by one unit of perspective. The frame is real; what is behind it is not a room but the next copy of the one you are in, smaller by a fixed ratio.

iv

Wolfram's rule

The lights along the walls follow a one-dimensional cellular automaton, so the pattern is long-periodic and never simply repeats — the same reason the corridor feels like it is going somewhere.

v

Field of view

Widen the view past about ninety degrees and the perspective becomes wide-angle, then fisheye: straight lines bow outward and the corridor starts to breathe.

vi

Turning round

Turn round and the room behind you is drawn in the same projection, from the same rules. Nothing is mirrored. Nothing is cheaper. The corridor simply does not care which way you face.
