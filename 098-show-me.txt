# Show Me — when the sound becomes the shape

## Short title
Show Me — when the sound becomes the shape

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A warm paper stage where sound is drawn as it is heard: live audio from the microphone is
sampled with the Web Audio API, its spectrum is read frame by frame, and every measured
feature drives a visible one. Amplitude is radius, so loud is large. The spectral centroid
— the weighted mean of the spectrum — is hue, so a bright sound is bright in colour. Pitch
is angle, mapped onto the circle of fifths, so a note's place on the staff and its place in
the drawing are the same place. Timbre is texture: overtones drive bristle count, edge
roughness and grain, so a clarinet and a flute of the same pitch never look alike. One,
two or twelve channels can be drawn at once from the same analysis, each a different
projection of the same measurement. A built-in sequence plays so the page makes sound
without any permission, and a seed of deterministic noise keeps the grain from flickering.
The type is a quiet old-style serif on paper with one ink accent; the notes column explains
what a centroid is.

## Major visual techniques used
- Web Audio AnalyserNode: FFT spectrum, RMS amplitude, spectral centroid, zero-crossing
- Pitch tracked by harmonic product spectrum and mapped to angle on the circle of fifths
- Amplitude mapped to radius, centroid to hue, overtones to bristle and grain
- A deterministic noise field so grain is stable frame to frame
- Multi-channel rendering: one, two or twelve projections of the same measurement
- Canvas 2D with additive strokes and paper-toned compositing, DPR-aware
- Built-in oscillator sequence so the page is self-sufficient without a microphone

## Intended interaction model
A microphone button asks for the input and switches the analysis to live sound; a sequence
button plays the built-in phrases for the same analysis. Chips choose how many channels
are drawn and which shape grows; sliders drive detail, length, speed and bristle. The
drawing responds continuously — nothing is a replay. A readout reports pitch, note,
centroid, channels and frame rate, and the notes column names the mapping in words.
