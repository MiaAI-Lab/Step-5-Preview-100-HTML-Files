# ENGINE ROOM — two hundred years of gauges

## Short title
Steam Gauges

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an engine-room wall of
nineteenth-century instruments — brass dials, a mercury U-tube and a sight glass — all
driven by throttle, load and wear, with hysteresis built in. No external dependencies
whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and
JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: brass on pig iron. Ground #12100c, hairlines rgba(240,231,212,.14),
body #f0e7d4 with muted #a2947a, brass #e0a63c as the accent and a machinery teal
#8fd0c0 for the figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em
kickers, monospace figures.

The wall is a riveted plate with horizontal seams, staggered rivet dots and a light from
the upper left. On it: three brass dials, a mercury U-tube with a printed scale, a sight
glass full of coolant, and a brass plaque. Every dial is a radial-gradient case with a cream
face, a red arc at the limit, ticks and printed numbers computed from its own minimum,
maximum and tick interval, a label and a unit, a needle with a counterweight and a drop
shadow, and a specular sweep clipped to the glass.

The physics: throttle and load set targets for boiler pressure, engine speed, vacuum, lube
oil pressure and coolant temperature. Each needle chases its target at a rate scaled by the
wear, so a worn gauge lags on the way back and an old gauge is only honest at the end of a
run — which is the mechanism the copy describes. Wear also comes back as the engine runs,
so nothing stays calibrated.

The mercury column rises with the vacuum reading at its own slow rate, and the sight glass
level follows the coolant temperature. Blow the safety lifts the valve: the boiler reading
drops, the wall steams, and the readout says venting. Service the lot resets the wear.

Controls: throttle, load, wear, Blow the safety and Service the lot. Readout: boiler
pressure, rpm, vacuum, lube oil, coolant and a condition word from the wear, with the
boiler figure turning red past its red line.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the wall canvas with its caption, a dock of three sliders and
two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on the Bourdon tube, hysteresis,
the safety valve, the column, the glass and service, then a footer.

Interaction: open the throttle and everything moves together — boiler first, then the
engine, then the heat. Drag the wear up and watch the needles stop following; service the
lot and they snap back. Blow the safety and the boiler vents and the wall steams while the
gauge tells you what you already know.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
An engine-room wall on canvas of riveted iron, three brass dials with computed ticks and
numbers, a mercury U-tube and a sight glass of coolant, all driven by throttle, load and
wear — where each needle chases its target at a rate scaled by the wear so a worn gauge
lags on the way back, the mercury column rises slowly with the vacuum, blowing the safety
vents the boiler and steams the wall, and the readout names the condition from the same
wear number.

## Major visual techniques used
- Riveted plate as horizontal seams with staggered rivet dots, stamped every 64 pixels
- Each dial as a radial-gradient case with a cream face, a computed tick ring and printed
  numbers derived from its own minimum, maximum and interval
- Red arc at the limit drawn from the same fraction the needle uses
- Needle with a counterweight, a drop shadow and a specular sweep clipped to the glass
- Hysteresis by scaling each needle's chase rate with the wear, which is the actual
  mechanism of a worn instrument
- Wear that regenerates from throttle use, so calibration is never permanent
- Mercury column stroked as a thick path with a meniscus highlight, rising at its own
  slow rate rather than tracking exactly
- Sight glass as a thick translucent stroke with a coloured fill inside and a shine
- Safety valve as a state that drops the boiler target and emits drifting steam circles
- Brass plaque with two lines of engraved text, centred with textAlign and textBaseline
- Condition word derived from the wear number, so the readout and the physics agree
- Boiler readout class toggled red past the red line

## Intended interaction model
An engine to work. Visitors open the throttle and watch the instruments move in the order
they actually do, drag the wear up to make them lie, service the lot to make them honest
again, and blow the safety to vent the boiler. Every number on the wall is the number in
the readout.
