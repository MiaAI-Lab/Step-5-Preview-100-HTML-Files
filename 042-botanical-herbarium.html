Cabinet III · drawers 12 – 16 · pressed 1873–1901

A hundred and
four summers,
flattened.

Each sheet is a single plant, pressed between blotting paper for six weeks, sewn down with linen thread, and labelled in copperplate. Nothing on these sheets was drawn. Everything was real.

The method · reproduced from the collector's notebook

Gathered in flower, never in rain.

The whole plant is taken — root, stem, leaf and flower — and laid on a sheet of absorbent paper with the parts spread so that none overlap. The press is tightened every third day. Six weeks later the plant is dead, permanent, and exactly the shape it was on the day it was found.
