# FROST ON GLASS — circles of confusion

## Short title
Bokeh Frost

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a dark street of lights behind a
frosted window, where every out-of-focus point of light is drawn as the polygon silhouette
of a real camera diaphragm, sized by a real circle of confusion. No external dependencies
whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and
JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a cold, blue-black night. Ground #0c1014, hairlines rgba(232,240,246,.14),
body #e8f0f6 with muted #93a4b2, frost blue #9fd8f0 as the accent and warm white #ffd8a0
for the figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers,
monospace figures.

Behind the glass are five kinds of light. Streetlights, warm orange. Neon, pink. Car
lights, near-white. Candles, deep amber. Phosphor, cyan. Each kind has its own count,
colour and size range.

The optics are real. The aperture slider runs f/1.4 to f/22 logarithmically, and a lens's
fastest stop lengthens with its focal length, so an 85 mm lens cannot open to f/1.4. The
circle of confusion is computed from a 0.05 mm diameter of confusion on a 44 mm vertical
frame. The hyperfocal distance is f²/(Nc) + f, and the depth of field near and far limits
follow from it. Each light is filled as a polygon with as many sides as the diaphragm has
blades — five, six, seven, nine or eleven — so the bokeh is the silhouette of the aperture
itself.

The frost is 120 hexagonal plates, largest first, each with a rim and three to five
dendrite branches with side spurs, because ice's crystal structure is hexagonal. Haze and
depth shading follow the sun of the picture, and the plane of focus is a faint band across
the canvas at the focus distance.

Dragging the canvas wipes the frost away in the width of the drag, and the rings behind
the wipe come back. Controls: five blades, five light kinds, focal length, aperture,
focus distance, Re-frost the glass, Wipe a patch. Readout: lights, circle of confusion
radius, blades, effective aperture, hyperfocal distance, depth of field.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the glass canvas with its caption, a dock of two chip rows,
three sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on the circle
of confusion, the shape of the hole, frost, diffraction, depth of field and wiping, then
a footer.

Interaction: the lights are already smeared into big rings. Visitors pull the aperture
down to f/22 and watch them turn into pentagons and hexagons, lengthen the focal length
and watch the lens refuse to open past its own fastest stop, drag across the glass to wipe
the frost and see the rings sharpen in the wiped path, switch the light kind, and press
Wipe a patch to clear the whole thing.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A dark street of lights behind a frost-blue window on canvas, where each out-of-focus
point is filled as a polygon with as many sides as the diaphragm has blades — five, six,
seven, nine or eleven — and sized by a circle of confusion computed from a 0.05 mm
diameter on a 44 mm frame, under an aperture that runs f/1.4 to f/22 and a lens whose
fastest stop lengthens with its focal length — while 120 hexagonal frost plates with
dendrite branches sit over the top, and dragging the canvas wipes them clear.

## Major visual techniques used
- Aperture slider mapped f/1.4 to f/22 logarithmically
- A lens whose fastest stop lengthens with focal length, so the wide end closes up as you
  zoom in
- Circle of confusion from a 0.05 mm diameter of confusion on a 44 mm vertical frame
- Hyperfocal distance as f²/(Nc) + f, with the depth of field limits derived from it
- Each light filled as a polygon with as many sides as the diaphragm has blades, so five
  blades give a pentagon and eleven a near-circle
- A radial gradient from white through the light's colour to a bright rim, which is the
  edge of the blades
- A diffraction ring around any bokeh larger than four pixels
- Frost as 120 hexagonal plates, largest first, with a rim and dendrites with side spurs,
  because ice's crystal structure is hexagonal
- The plane of focus drawn as a faint band at the focus distance
- Capillary wipe: a soft clip along the drag path clears the frost there
- Five light kinds — streetlights, neon, car lights, candles, phosphor — each with its own
  count, colour and size range
- Parallax drift on the lights so the picture is never static

## Intended interaction model
A window at night. Visitors stop down the aperture and watch the rings contract into
polygons; switch blades and see the silhouette change shape; lengthen the focal length and
watch the lens refuse to open past its own fastest stop; drag across the glass to wipe the
frost and see the rings sharpen in exactly the path they wiped; switch the light kind; and
press Wipe a patch to see the whole street at once.
