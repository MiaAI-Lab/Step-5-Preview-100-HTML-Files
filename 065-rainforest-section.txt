# RAINFOREST SECTION — four floors of light

## Short title
Rainforest

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an annotated cross-section of
a rainforest in four strata, where the light that reaches each floor is a function of the
hour you set and the weather you turn on. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN,
no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works
offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a wet green section on near-black. Ground #0a1310, hairlines
rgba(230,242,234,.14), body #e6f2ea with muted #93a89c, one green accent #8fd694 and
amber #ffd166 for figures and species names. Serif headings with italic accent words,
uppercase .2em–.4em kickers, monospace readouts.

The section is an SVG on a 1000 × 620 viewBox built at load from a single seed: a sky
gradient, four strata whose top edges are quadratic waves that flatten with depth, two
emergent trees that break the roof, sixteen canopy crowns, twenty-two understory trunks
each carrying a broad leaf on a random scale and rotation, and thirty ferns along the
floor. Everything comes from a mulberry32 generator, so the section is stable across
reloads and completely different if the seed changes.

The light is the point. Eight key hours — night, first light, dawn, morning, noon,
afternoon, dusk, night — hold a pair of RGB triples each, and any hour between them is
interpolated. Dragging the slider rewrites both gradient stops and re-filters the whole
section with brightness and saturation derived from how far the hour is from solar noon,
so the strata are literally re-lit rather than tinted. The readout reports the percentage
of light that reaches the floor.

The weather runs on a canvas laid over the section: rain spawns at a rate set by the
rainfall slider, most drops are caught by the canopy line and removed, and the few that
get through slow to a drip. Mist is a set of drifting radial gradients; light shafts are
four quadrilaterals from the canopy to the floor whose opacity flickers on a sine, the
way light through leaves does. Leaves drift down whichever way the weather is going.

Six species hotspots are absolutely positioned over the section at real strata depths —
harpy eagle, sloth, morpho, poison dart frog, toucan, leafcutter ant — and each updates
a card with the binomial and a note on why it lives at that height.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the section with its card and hotspots, a dock with the hour
and rainfall sliders and three toggles, a readout row, two three-cell note rows on the
four strata and on the light, then a footer. Links hidden under 780px.

Interaction: drag the hour and the section is re-lit from night to noon to dusk; start
the rain and set how hard it falls and watch how much of it gets through the roof; turn
on mist and light shafts; tap any hotspot to read about the animal that lives there.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A rainforest cross-section on an SVG built from one seed into four strata — emergent,
canopy, understory, floor — where dragging the hour rewrites the sky gradient and
re-filters the whole section so each floor is actually re-lit, and where a canvas
overlay rains on the canopy at a rate you set, catches most of it at the roof, lets a
little through as drips, adds drifting mist and flickering light shafts, and six species
hotspots at their real depths explain who lives where.

## Major visual techniques used
- Section generated from a mulberry32 seed, so the same file always produces the same
  forest and a seed change produces a different one
- Stratum top edges as quadratic waves whose amplitude flattens with depth, giving the
  floors their own horizons
- Hour-keyed colour interpolation across eight keyframes, so any hour is a real mix
  of the two nearest keys rather than a filter thrown at a fixed picture
- Whole-section relighting as a single brightness/saturation filter derived from the
  distance from solar noon, which is what makes the floors behave differently
- Floor light percentage computed from the same lift term as the filter, so the readout
  and the picture cannot disagree
- Rain on a canvas overlaid on the SVG, with a canopy line that removes most drops and
  slows the survivors to a drip
- Mist as drifting radial gradients cleared and redrawn each frame, so it never streaks
- Light shafts as quadrilaterals whose opacity flickers on per-shaft sines
- Understory leaves placed with a random scale and rotation from the same seeded stream
- Hotspots as absolutely positioned buttons at real stratum depths, driving a single
  card so only one species is ever open
- Canvas backing store sized to DPR with CSS scaling, so the weather stays aligned with
  the SVG underneath at every width
- Fixed internal SVG viewBox with preserveAspectRatio, so the strata and the hotspot
  percentages stay in agreement

## Intended interaction model
A diagram you can sit in. Visitors drag the hour to watch the light get taken apart
floor by floor, set the rainfall and start the rain to see how little of it lands, switch
on mist and shafts, and tap the animals to find out why each one lives at the height it
does. The controls change the physics of the picture rather than decorating it.
