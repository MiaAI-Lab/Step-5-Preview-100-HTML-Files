i

Character

Every light has a character: how many flashes, how long, how long between. A skipper reads it off a card and knows exactly which headland he is looking at, which is the whole reason the pattern is kept by law.

ii

Sea state

Swell height is not the wind. Long waves outlive the storm that made them, and they arrive from a direction that has nothing to do with the wind blowing now.

iii

Visibility

Rain takes the light apart before it reaches anything. The nominal range is what the lens does on a clear night; the range you get is what the weather leaves.

iv

Steam

The glass fogs from inside because the lamp room is warm and the outside is not. A keeper climbs the tower every watch to wipe it, which is why the lamp is never quite the number on the chart.

v

Bearing

The number in the corner is where the beam is pointing, in degrees from north. Take a bearing on the flash and you have a line of position; two lights give you a fix.

vi

Storm

Everything here is driven by three sliders and one clock. The boat is only visible when the beam crosses it, which is how it would actually be.
