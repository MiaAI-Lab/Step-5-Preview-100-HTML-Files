i

Inharmonicity

A tube's partials sit at 1, 2.76, 5.40 and 8.93 times the fundamental instead of 1, 2, 3 and 4. Every interval it plays is therefore a little out, and the ear reads that as metal rather than as pitch.

ii

Timbre

Bamboo is thin and dry and gone in a second. Aluminium is bright and rings for ages. Copper is low and dark. The clang slider is how much of the upper partials you hear, which is the difference between a wind chime and a bell.

iii

The scale

Chimes are usually hung to a pentatonic scale, because a scale with no semitones can sound wrong by almost any combination. The keys row switches between pentatonic, whole-tone and the Japanese hirajoshi.

iv

The wind

Each tube is a pendulum driven by the same wind, and they swing at their own period, so the set never strikes the same phrase twice. The wind is a number and a noise, and the striking is not.

v

Decay

Decay is how fast a struck tube goes quiet, and it depends on the material far more than on how hard you hit it. Aluminium holds; bamboo does not.

vi

Hang a set

Eight tubes in one scale, hung longest to shortest, so the low ones swing slowly and the high ones chatter. The same geometry every time, tuned differently.
