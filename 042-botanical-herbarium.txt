# HERBARIUM — Pressed Specimens, 1873–1901

## Short title
Botanical Herbarium

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a digitised herbarium of
fictional pressed plant specimens, called HERBARIUM. No external dependencies
whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no
JavaScript libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by
double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: Victorian pressed-plant sheets. Warm paper #f3eddd with a
deeper #e9e0ca on hover, ink brown-black #23201a, stem green #4a5f3a, leaf
#6b8a4e with a lighter variant for alternating leaves, root brown #8a6a4a, and one
oxblood accent #a33a2a used for the eyebrow and the Latin names. A full-viewport
paper-fibre texture from an inline SVG feTurbulence data-URI sits over everything
in multiply blend, and a single vertical hairline down the exact centre of the
viewport suggests the fold of an opened book. Each sheet carries two diagonal strips
of faded archival tape at its top corners.

The plants are built in the DOM from a small vocabulary of divs. A vertical stalk
with a green gradient; leaves placed alternately along it as asymmetric ellipses
(border-radius 50% 50% 50% 50% / 60% 60% 40% 40%) rotating out from the stalk's
centre at 38–60°, with every second leaf pair in a lighter green; a flower head
positioned at the top of the stalk, made of N petals each an ellipse rotated around
the centre at its own radius with a per-petal opacity variation, plus a small
radial-gradient core; and five roots dropping below the stalk, each a thin brown
gradient bar rotated out at a different angle with its own length and opacity. Nine
specimens are defined — yarrow, oxeye daisy, cornflower, field poppy, tansy,
viper's bugloss, heather, chicory and foxglove — each with its own petal count,
petal colours, core colour, leaf count, leaf size, stalk height and whether it has
the lighter alternate leaves. Everything is assembled from the same function, so the
nine sheets differ only by their parameters.

Layout: a nav with a serif brand at 0.32em tracking and two links. A centred hero
with an oxblood eyebrow, a serif headline with one italic stem-green word, and a
paragraph about pressing. Then a nine-sheet grid built from 1px gaps, each sheet
with its tape strips, the plant in a 3:4 box, and a caption giving the common name
in italic serif, the Latin name in oxblood monospace, the year, month and county,
and the sheet number with the six-week pressing note. Then a second hero-style
block for the method. Then a ruled monospace footer.

Interaction: hovering a sheet lifts it 6px and warms the paper to the deeper tone,
which reads as picking the sheet up off the drawer. Nothing else is interactive —
the plants do not move, because pressed plants do not move. The grid drops 3 → 2 → 1
columns.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A digitised herbarium of nine pressed plants assembled in the DOM from a shared
vocabulary of divs: a green-gradient stalk, alternating asymmetric leaf ellipses
rotating out from it, a radial crown of N petals with a small core, and five
tapering roots — mounted on warm paper with a fibre texture, a centre fold, corner
tape strips, and captions in italic serif, Latin name in oxblood monospace, and
county of origin.

## Major visual techniques used
- Plants assembled from divs: gradient stalk, asymmetric leaf ellipses, radial petal
  crowns and tapered root bars
- Leaf rotation from the stalk centre at 38–60°, with alternating lighter leaves and
  a scaleX(-1) mirror for the opposite side
- Petal crowns from N ellipses each rotated around a shared transform-origin at its
  own radius, with per-petal opacity variation
- Small radial-gradient cores set via CSS custom properties per specimen
- Roots as thin vertical gradient bars rotated at different angles with individual
  lengths and opacities
- Warm paper ground with an SVG turbulence fibre texture in multiply blend
- Vertical centre-fold hairline suggesting an opened book
- Archival tape strips as rotated translucent rectangles hanging over the sheet
  corners
- Nine specimens parameterised through one builder function
- 1px-gap sheet grid with the hairline colour showing through
- Caption hierarchy: italic serif common name, oxblood Latin name, 8.5px tracked
  monospace metadata
- Single oxblood accent against a warm paper/green palette
- Hover lift and paper-warm on the sheets
- 3 → 2 → 1 responsive column collapse

## Intended interaction model
A collection to be looked through. Visitors hover a sheet to lift it and read its
label; there are no controls and nothing animates, which is the point — a herbarium
is a collection of things that have been stopped. The page's only responsiveness
beyond layout is the grid collapsing.
