# CIRCUIT BENCH — three wires, one Bloch sphere

## Short title
Quantum Circuit

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a small quantum circuit bench
where gates are placed on three wires across eight steps and the resulting state is drawn
as a Bloch vector per wire. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no
external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by
double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a dark instrument. Ground #080b14, panel #0b1020, hairlines
rgba(232,237,251,.14), body #e8edfb with muted #98a1bd, mint #7ef0e0 for the selected wire
and gate marks, pink #ff9ad5 for measurement, and amber #ffd166 for the probability
figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers, monospace
figures and ket notation.

The bench: three wires of eight cells each, built in the DOM at load. Pick a gate from the
palette — X, Y, Z, H, S, T, R (a forty-five degree rotation), CNOT (drawn as +) and M —
and click a cell to place it; click the same cell again to remove it. Clicking a wire also
selects it, which is what the Bloch sphere highlights.

Every gate is implemented as a rotation of a Bloch vector rather than as a matrix, which is
exact for single-qubit work: X and Y reflect the vector to the antipode, Z and S and T turn
it about the vertical axis, H drops it onto the equator, R tilts it forty-five degrees, and
M collapses it — the probability of |1⟩ is the square of the sine of half the polar angle,
and the vector then jumps to a pole and stays there.

Run walks the eight steps in order and applies every gate on every wire, respecting
measurement: once a wire has been measured it is frozen. The Bloch sphere is drawn on
canvas — a circle plus two flattened ellipses for the equator and meridian, the poles
labelled |0⟩ and |1⟩ and the equator's ends labelled |+⟩ and |-⟩ — with one vector per
wire, the selected one drawn brighter and heavier, and measured wires annotated with the
outcome. Beside it, one probability bar per wire with the live |1⟩ percentage.

The two-qubit control is a simplification and says so on the page: a CNOT reads the wire
above it and flips this one when that one is mostly pointing down.

Layout: nav, centred hero, a two-column bench of circuit and gate panel, a two-column row
of Bloch sphere and probability bars, then two three-cell note rows on the sphere,
rotations, measurement, the one-qubit simplification, phases and idempotence, then a
footer. One column under 900px, links hidden under 780px.

Interaction: place gates and press Run. Visitors can build the textbook X X pair and watch
it cancel, build H S H and see the phase come back, put an M on a wire and freeze it, and
use CNOT to read one wire and flip the next. Clear the bench returns every vector to the
north pole.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A circuit bench with three wires of eight cells where gates chosen from a palette of X, Y,
Z, H, S, T, R, CNOT and M are placed on steps and applied in order as rotations of a Bloch
vector per wire — with measurement collapsing a wire to a pole and freezing it — drawn on a
canvas sphere with the poles and the equator's ends labelled, one vector per wire with the
selected one brighter, and a live |1⟩ probability bar for each.

## Major visual techniques used
- Gates as rotations of a Bloch vector rather than matrix multiplication, which is exact
  for the single-qubit gates and visibly maps each gate onto the sphere
- Polar angle and azimuth as the whole state, with the |1⟩ probability derived as the
  square of the sine of half the polar angle
- Measurement as a probabilistic collapse followed by a freeze, so later gates cannot
  reach a measured wire
- CNOT simplified honestly: it reads the wire above and flips this one if that one is
  mostly down, and the page says that is what it does
- Bloch sphere drawn as a circle plus two flattened ellipses, which reads as a sphere in
  two dimensions with no projection maths
- Vector projection from the same polar angle and azimuth used for the physics, so the
  picture and the simulator cannot disagree
- Selected wire highlighted by both weight and colour, with the other two dimmed
- Measured wires annotated with the outcome at the vector's tip
- Circuit cells as a flex row per wire with a shared line element, so the grid is real
  DOM and scrolls on narrow screens
- Clicking the same cell with the same gate removes it, and clicking another cell with a
  different gate replaces it
- Probability bars with a CSS transition on width, so a run animates into its result
- Gate palette using aria-pressed for the single selected state

## Intended interaction model
A bench to run. Visitors pick a gate, click cells along the three wires, and press Run to
see every vector move at once; measurement freezes a wire, CNOT reads the one above it,
and Clear resets the bench to three vectors at the north pole. Everything on screen is
computed from the gates placed, with no animation of its own.
