# FALLING — A Box of Sand That Behaves

## Short title
Falling Sand Physics

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a falling-sand cellular
automaton toy called FALLING. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web
fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries. All CSS
and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a dark lab bench. Ground #0e1116, panels #161c24, hairline
rules at 12% white, off-white text #e4eef6, and the palette of the six materials
themselves doing all the colour work: sand #e0b25e, water #3d8fd0, stone #7d8794,
fire #e8632a, plant #4fae5e, oil #4a3a2a. Typography is a serif for the headline and
the figures, a system sans for paragraphs, and 8.5–9.5px wide-tracked monospace for
every label and readout.

The simulation runs on a Uint8Array of 3-pixel cells, stepped bottom-up with the
horizontal scan direction flipped every other frame (which removes the directional
bias that makes naive falling sand drift to one side). Six materials with genuinely
different rules:
- Sand: falls straight into empty space, water or fire (swapping with whatever it
  displaces), otherwise tries both diagonals. This is what makes it cone.
- Water: falls into empty space, otherwise spreads diagonally then sideways, so it
  levels out.
- Oil: falls and spreads like water but slower and more reluctantly sideways.
- Fire: carries a decaying lifetime in a parallel Float32Array; burns oil and plant
  on contact by converting them to fire with full life; is extinguished by water;
  and rises into empty space with a decaying copy of its life.
- Plant: grows into adjacent empty cells at 2% per frame, but only if water is
  within its 3×3 neighbourhood — so plant spreads from a water source and stops at
  dry ground.
- Stone: static.

Rendering is a single putImageData per two frames, with the canvas scaled up by CSS
and image-rendering: pixelated so each cell is a crisp square. Each material's
colour is given a per-cell deterministic jitter (from x·7 + y·13 style hashes) so a
field of sand shimmers instead of being flat.

Layout: a nav with a monospace brand. A centred hero with a sand-coloured eyebrow,
a serif headline with one italic sand-coloured word, and a paragraph explaining the
rules. Then a lab section — a 16:11 box holding the canvas, beside a 260px sidebar
with a six-cell material picker (each cell bordered in its own colour when
selected), brush-size and gravity sliders, an "Empty the box" button, and a live
stats block of occupied cells and step count. Then a three-cell notes row with large
sand-coloured serif figures explaining sand, water and fire. Then a ruled monospace
footer.

Interaction: drag inside the box to pour the selected material in a circular brush
whose radius is set by the brush slider (2–46). Choose among sand, water, stone,
fire, plant and oil. The gravity slider multiplies the number of simulation steps
per frame from 0.5× to 4×, so the whole world can be sped up or slowed. "Empty the
box" clears the grid. The page seeds itself with a rolling sand dune, a stone
layer, a few stone columns and three plant patches so there is something to play
with immediately. The lab sidebar stacks under the box below 900px.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A dark-bench sandbox where six materials obey genuinely different rules — sand
cones, water levels, oil creeps, fire burns fuel and rises, plant only grows near
water, stone does nothing — stepped bottom-up with alternating scan direction to
kill directional bias, drawn as crisp per-cell-jittered pixels and paintable with a
variable brush and a gravity multiplier.

## Major visual techniques used
- Uint8Array grid of 3px cells stepped bottom-up with alternating scan direction to
  remove directional drift
- Six-material cellular ruleset: straight-then-diagonal fall, spread-and-level for
  liquids, burning with a decaying lifetime parallel array, conditional plant growth
- Fluid displacement by swap rather than overwrite (sand swaps with water and fire)
- Fire lifetime stored in a separate Float32Array so fire can fade and rise
- Plant growth gated on a water neighbour within the 3×3 neighbourhood
- Single putImageData per two frames with image-rendering: pixelated for crisp cells
- Per-cell deterministic colour jitter from x/y hashes so material fields shimmer
- Alternating two-colour jitter in the image-data build loop
- Circular brush painting with a material-specific fill rule (fire only lights fuel
  or empty air; solids only fill empty or fire)
- Gravity multiplier as steps-per-frame repetition from 0.5× to 4×
- Material picker with per-material border colour on the selected state
- Live stats of occupied cells (sampled every 20 frames) and step count
- Self-seeding initial terrain: dune, stone floor, stone columns, plant patches
- Serif headline with one sand-coloured italic word against 8.5px tracked monospace
- 16:11 lab box with a 260px sidebar; stacking below 900px

## Intended interaction model
A toy. Visitors pick a material, set a brush size and a gravity, and drag in the box
to pour it, watching sand cone, water find its level, fire spread through oil and
plant, and plant creep outward from any water it touches. "Empty the box" starts
over. There is no goal; the pleasure is in the materials behaving correctly.
