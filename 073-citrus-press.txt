# GROVE &amp; PRESS — citrus you have to work for

## Short title
Citrus Press

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a citrus press where the fruit
is drawn from its real anatomy, the variety and size set the yield, and holding a button
squeezes it into a glass. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no
external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by
double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a warm room. Ground #fff8ec, panels #fffdf6, hairlines
rgba(43,33,24,.2), ink #2b2118 with muted #6b5c48, one accent orange #ff6a13 and a leaf
green #3f7d3a. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers,
monospace figures. Nothing here is dark.

Five varieties — Valencia orange, Eureka lemon, Ruby grapefruit, Tarocco blood orange and
Key lime — each with its own rind, rind shadow, pulp and pith colours, its own segment
count and its own real yield in millilitres. The variety panel is aria-pressed chips with
a colour swatch and a two-word tasting note.

The fruit is SVG. Whole, it is a shaded sphere with a peel pore texture — seventy dots
placed on a golden-angle spiral so they read as peel — a dimple at the top, a stem and a
leaf. Halved, it is rind, pith, pulp and then the segments: the real number of them for
that variety, each a filled wedge, each with a vesicle line down the middle, around a
white core. Hold the squeeze button and a dark ram slides down onto the halved fruit while
the pulp darkens.

The glass is a clipped SVG: juice fills from the bottom at a rate set by how hard you
press, with pulp flecks, a meniscus, ice cubes once it is more than half full, and a twist
of zest on the rim. The recipe rows underneath recompute from the same variety and size:
juice, zest, sugar (more for the sharp ones), ice, segment count, and fruits used today.

Controls: the variety chips, a hold-to-squeeze button, a size slider from 70 to 130
percent, and Halve it. Readout: pressed percentage, variety, yield in millilitres, whether
the glass is empty, pouring or full, and fruits pressed today.

Layout: nav, centred hero, a two-column press of fruit and panel, a readout row, a
two-column recipe of amounts and glass, then two three-cell note rows on segments, yield,
rind, the press, colour and season, then a footer. One column under 900px, links hidden
under 780px.

Interaction: pick a variety, set the size, and hold the squeeze button — the ram comes
down, the fruit darkens and the glass fills to whatever that fruit actually gives, and the
recipe numbers follow. Halve it to see the anatomy, and change variety to change the whole
page's palette.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A citrus press on a warm cream page where five varieties each carry their own colours,
segment count and real yield, the fruit is drawn as a shaded sphere with a golden-angle
peel texture or as a halved cross-section with its real number of segments and vesicle
lines, and holding the squeeze button brings a ram down onto it while a glass fills to
that fruit's yield with pulp flecks, ice and a twist of zest, and the recipe rows recompute
from the same two variables.

## Major visual techniques used
- Fruit anatomy drawn in the right order: rind, pith, pulp, segments, core, so the
  cross-section is real rather than a stylised wedge
- Peel texture as seventy dots on a golden-angle spiral, which is the same distribution
  a real fruit's pores follow
- Segment count and wedge angles computed from the variety, not hard-coded per fruit
- Vesicle lines drawn down the middle of each segment, which is what makes pulp read
  as pulp
- Juice inside the glass clipped by the glass outline, so the fill can never leave
  the vessel
- Meniscus as an ellipse at the fill line, and pulp flecks scattered inside the clip
- Ice cubes appearing past a threshold of the fill, so the glass changes state
- Ram as a rounded rect translated by the press percentage, so the squeeze is visible
  from the top-down view
- Yield as millilitres derived from the variety and the size slider, so the readout and
  the glass agree
- Recipe rows recomputed from the same two variables as the drawing
- Variety chips as aria-pressed with a colour swatch and a tasting note
- Squeeze rate made a function of the size slider, so a bigger fruit presses slower and
  gives more

## Intended interaction model
A press you work. Visitors choose a variety, set the size, hold the button and watch the
ram come down and the glass fill to what that fruit really gives, halve the fruit to see
the anatomy, and read the recipe numbers as they change. Changing the variety repaints the
whole page's accents, because there is nothing here that is not derived from the fruit.
