# THE INTERPRETER'S CIRCLE — harmony you can see

## Short title
Interpreter's Circle

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: Paul Hindemith's circle of
twelve notes ordered by the closeness of each interval to the root, where a chord is a
vector with a direction and a pull, and where every instrument is drawn on canvas and
sounds from real partial amplitudes. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web
fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline
by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a warm printed score. Ground #f6f2ea, hairlines rgba(32,24,15,.16),
body #20180f with muted #5c5040, ink brown #8c5a1e as the accent and brass #c99a5c for
the instrument. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em kickers,
monospace figures.

Hindemith's circle is the table [0,7,4,3,10,2,9,5,8,11,6,1] — the root, then the fifth,
the major third, the minor third, and so on outward — so a note's position on the ring is
how far it sits from home. Tension is the mean of the chord's notes' positions divided by
eleven. A chord's vector is the sum of its notes' unit vectors on that circle, drawn as
an arrow from the centre; the resolution is that arrow's angle read as a step round the
circle.

The instruments are drawn as vector line art — a clarinet of cylinder, bell and mouthpiece;
a violin of two bezier waists; an oboe of body, flare and four key holes; a flute of a tube
with seven finger holes — and their timbres are stacks of partials with real amplitudes.
A clarinet's even harmonics are almost silent, a flute has almost nothing above the
second, an oboe is reedy and hollow, a violin is rich above the octave. The sound is Web
Audio: one sine oscillator per partial above each note's fundamental, with a slow vibrato
LFO, through a shared gain whose level is the volume slider.

Ten chords: major, minor, diminished, augmented, sus 4, dominant seventh, major seventh,
minor seventh, diminished seventh and a Neapolitan. Controls: ten chords, four instruments,
tension, volume, reed weight, Resolve it, Circle of fifths. Dragging over a note lights
it. Readout: chord, root, tension, vector angle, resolves to.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the circle canvas with its caption beside a control column,
then two three-cell note rows on the ordering, the vector, the additive timbre and the
partials, then a footer.

Interaction: the page opens on a C dominant seventh. Visitors switch chords and watch the
arrow swing and the tension climb, press Resolve it and hear the chord settle and a ring
expand out of the centre, flip to the circle of fifths to see the same twelve notes in the
other order, switch instruments and hear the timbre change wholesale because the partials
do, drag the reed weight to sharpen or hollow the sound, and touch a note to light it.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A warm-inked circle of twelve notes on canvas, ordered by Hindemith's interval table so a
note's place on the ring is its distance from the root — where each chord is an arrow
summed from its notes' unit vectors and its tension is the mean of their positions, four
vector-drawn instruments sit at the foot of the ring with their partial amplitudes printed
beneath them, and pressing a chord sounds it through Web Audio as one sine oscillator per
partial with a slow vibrato.

## Major visual techniques used
- Hindemith's circle as the table [0,7,4,3,10,2,9,5,8,11,6,1], so position is interval
  distance from the root, and a toggle to the circle of fifths for the other reading
- The chord's vector as the sum of its notes' unit vectors, drawn as a gradient arrow with
  a filled head from the centre of the ring
- Tension as the mean of the chord's notes' positions divided by eleven, blended with the
  tension slider
- Resolution read as the vector's angle in steps round the circle, and named as a major
  chord on that root
- Instruments drawn as vector line art: clarinet of cylinder, bell and mouthpiece; violin
  of two bezier waists; oboe of body, flare and four keys; flute of a tube with seven holes
- Partial amplitudes printed as a bar strip under the ring, scaled by the reed weight, so
  the timbre is visible as well as audible
- A wave drawn round each sounding note, with a lobe count equal to the note's pitch class
- Radial glow on each active note, with a hit envelope that decays
- Two dashed echo rings inside and outside the main ring
- Web Audio timbre: one sine oscillator per partial above each note's fundamental, with a
  slow vibrato LFO per partial, through a shared gain
- Ten chords including diminished seventh and a Neapolitan

## Intended interaction model
A score you can hear. Visitors pick a chord and watch the arrow swing and the tension
climb; press Resolve it and a ring expands out of the centre as the chord settles; flip to
the circle of fifths to see the same twelve notes in the other order; switch instrument and
hear the timbre change wholesale because the partials do; drag the reed weight to sharpen
or hollow the sound; and touch a note to light it.
