# WIND CHIMES — hang tubes until they strike you

## Short title
Wind Chimes

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a set of hanging tubes driven by
the wind and integrated as pendulums, struck when they swing into their clapper, each
sounding its own inharmonic partials. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web
fonts, no external images, no JS libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline
by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a garden at night. Ground #0d1210, canvas #080d0b, hairlines
rgba(230,240,234,.14), body #e6f0ea with muted #93a49b, brass #c8b06a as the accent and a
mint #8fd0b0 for the figures. Serif headings with italic accent words, uppercase .2em–.4em
kickers, monospace figures.

The set hangs from a bar: eight tubes, longest and lowest on the left to shortest and
highest on the right, each with its own cord, gradient body, hole at the top, a highlight
and a clapper. Between them a moon and wind streaks.

Each tube is integrated as a pendulum with its own period, driven by a sine at its own rate
and phase, so the set never twice strikes the same phrase. When the swing passes a
threshold it hits the clapper and rings.

The sound is synthesised from the material's real inharmonic ratios. Every material has its
own set — aluminium at 1, 2.76, 5.40 and 8.93; copper at 1, 2.74, 5.34 and 8.80; bamboo
quieter and shorter; steel and brass and glass each slightly different — and each partial
is a separate oscillator with its own envelope whose duration falls off across the series.
The clang slider mixes the upper partials toward square waves and adds a noise transient,
which is the difference between a wind chime and a bell.

Four scales: pentatonic, whole tone, the Japanese hirajoshi and pelog, each with its own
base frequency and semitone pattern. Six materials. Controls: scale, material, wind, decay,
clang, A gust and Hang a set. Readout: tubes ringing, the last strike's frequency in hertz,
the longest tube, and the three overtone ratios for the current material.

Layout: nav, centred hero, the garden canvas with its caption, a dock of two chip rows,
three sliders and two buttons, a readout row, then two three-cell note rows on
inharmonicity, timbre, the scale, the wind, decay and hanging a set, then a footer.

Interaction: the set is already swinging and already ringing. Visitors raise the wind and
the strikes come faster, click a tube to ring it directly, drag across the set to push the
wind by hand, take a gust, switch the material to hear the ratios change, switch the scale
to retune everything, and move decay and clang to go from a dry bamboo to a long clanging
bell.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A hanging set of eight tubes on canvas, each integrated as a pendulum with its own period
and phase and driven by a wind sine, striking its clapper past a swing threshold and
sounding the material's real inharmonic partials — aluminium at 1, 2.76, 5.40 and 8.93,
copper, bamboo, steel, brass and glass each with their own set — as separate oscillators
with envelopes that fall off across the series, with a clang slider that mixes them toward
square waves and adds a noise transient, four scales, and a readout that reports the
current material's overtone ratios.

## Major visual techniques used
- Tubes as gradients across their width, with a hole at the top and a highlight down the
  side, so they read as hollow metal
- Each tube swung by rotating its own drawing about its suspension point
- Clapper drawn as a small circle offset by the swing, so the strike is visible
- Strike flash as a white band alpha-fading on the tube, which is what a hit looks like
- Pendulum integration with a period derived from the tube's length, so long tubes swing
  slowly and short ones chatter
- Strike threshold with hysteresis — struck on the way out, released on the way back — so
  a tube rings once per swing rather than every frame
- Each inharmonic partial as its own oscillator with a duration that falls off across the
  series, which is what makes a tube sound like a tube
- Clang as a shift toward square waves on the upper partials plus a decaying noise
  transient
- Material ratios as data, so switching material changes the sound and the readout together
- Four scales as semitone arrays, so retuning is one swap and every tube re-derives
- Wind streaks as quadratic curves whose alpha is the wind, so the air is visible
- Click-to-strike by nearest tube within ninety pixels, and drag-as-wind by pushing
  velocity on tubes you pass over
- AudioContext created and resumed only inside a user gesture

## Intended interaction model
A set that plays itself. The wind is already blowing and the tubes already ring; visitors
raise the wind, take a gust, click a tube, drag through the set to stir it, change the
material to hear the ratios change and the readout follow, switch the scale to retune, and
move decay and clang from dry bamboo to long clanging metal. The page is never silent
unless the wind is at zero and nothing is touched.
