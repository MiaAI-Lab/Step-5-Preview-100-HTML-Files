i

Pigment

Red ochre is iron oxide ground on stone, charcoal is burnt wood, and the white is kaolin. All three are ground with fat or saliva into a paste that bites into the rock face and stays.

ii

The stencil

A hand outline is made by blowing pigment through a tube at a hand held flat to the wall, so the result is negative space: the wall is the figure, not the hand.

iii

Overdrawing

The animals are painted over each other, sometimes by centuries. Where a bison overlaps a horse, the later animal is the one that survives — and the earlier one only appears where the later artist left a gap.

iv

Light

Everything here is one image and one light. The rock and the paintings are drawn once; the torch is a hole cut in a field of black, flickering on noise, and the fuel is a number that only goes down.

v

Flicker

A flame is not a lamp. The radius is modulated by two out-of-phase sines, so the lit area breathes the way a torch does and never quite settles.

vi

Paint

Pick a pigment and drag on the wall to add to it. Your strokes are the same colour, the same opacity and the same brush as the ones that are already there, because they are the same three colours.
