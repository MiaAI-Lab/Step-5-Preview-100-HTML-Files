# The Corridor — rooms that do not agree

## Short title
The Corridor — rooms that do not agree

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A cold-bluish architectural drawing of a corridor that cannot exist: the walls are drawn in
two-point perspective, and the vanishing points move with you so that walking forward
always reveals another room. Penrose stairs turn through four right angles and arrive where
they started; a portal in the far wall returns you to the room behind you, so the corridor
loops without ever bending. Room after room is generated from a Wolfram cellular-automaton
rule read as a wall plan, so the sequence of rooms is deterministic and the same journey
always produces the same architecture. A single oil lamp throws a moving pool of light down
the corridor, the field of view narrows and widens with speed, and the readout reports the
room number, the depth walked, the turn, the rule and the frame. The palette is one cold
blue on near-black with a sodium-lamp amber for the light; the notes column explains
impossible geometry in the same quiet editorial register as the drawing itself.

## Major visual techniques used
- Two-point perspective projection with vanishing points driven by the walker's heading
- Penrose stairs built from four right-angle turns with a height discontinuity at the loop
- Wolfram elementary cellular automaton generating each room's wall plan from one rule
- A moving point light with radial falloff, pooled light on the floor and on the ceiling
- Field of view that eases with speed, and depth fog by corridor distance
- Non-Euclidean portal: an axial coordinate that wraps on itself, so the loop is exact
- DPR-aware canvas sizing from the layout box, hairline rules, monospaced readout

## Intended interaction model
Drag or use the arrow keys to walk forward, back and to turn; the field-of-view, depth and
lamp sliders reshape the projection live; toggle chips switch the Penrose stairs, the
portal, the cellular-automaton plan and the lamp on and off. The walk is continuous — the
corridor is one coordinate wrapped onto itself — and a readout reports the room, the
distance walked, the heading, the rule in force and the frame count.
