A gauge is a machine
that forgets

Before plastics, before electronics, every number on a wall arrived by something flexing, something boiling, or a column of mercury climbing a glass tube. Nothing on this wall is digital, and nothing on it moves unless something real does.

i

The tube

A Bourdon tube is a flat coil that tries to straighten when pressure goes in. That is the whole mechanism: pressure becomes a curl, the curl pulls a linkage, and the linkage swings a needle past a printed scale.

ii

Hysteresis

Worn gauges do not come back down the way they went up. The wear slider is how far the needle lags on the way back, which is why an old gauge is read at the end of a run and not during it.

iii

Safety valve

Above the red line the valve lifts and the boiler vents. The needle does not stop at the red line; it drops, and the steam goes somewhere less useful.

iv

The column

The vacuum gauge is a mercury column: a U-tube with a scale behind it. It is honest in a way a needle never is, because you can see the liquid move.

v

Glass

The coolant level is sight glass — a thick tube with the liquid inside it and a shadow on the wall behind. Some things need a number and some need a window.

vi

Service

Service the lot resets the wear, but wear also comes back as the engine runs, so the gauges drift again. Nothing here stays calibrated for long.
