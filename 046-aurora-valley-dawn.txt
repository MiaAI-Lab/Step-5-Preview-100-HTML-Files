# FIRST LIGHT — A Valley at 04:50

## Short title
Aurora Valley Dawn

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an atmospheric valley
scene at first light, called FIRST LIGHT. No external dependencies whatsoever — no
CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript
libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive,
valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: the two minutes when the sun is just below the ridge. The CSS
room behind the canvas is a five-stop vertical gradient from night navy #1a2350
through blue-violet #2b3a68 and violet #57497a into rose #9a6a78 and warm
#d99a72; its brightness and hue are recomputed every frame from the sun altitude so
the entire page warms as the sun rises. On the canvas, a four-stop sky is drawn by
interpolating between a night palette and a day palette along the same altitude
value — every channel mixed separately, so at −14° the sky is deep indigo and at
+6° it is a pale warm blue.

The sun sits low, its radius and glow scaled by altitude, drifting slowly on a sine;
below +0.4° it is not drawn at all. Nine ridges are generated as twenty-one-point
double-sine silhouettes, drawn far first. Each ridge's colour is a mix between its
near-colour (dark violet-green) and pale haze, weighted by distance and the Haze
slider — real atmospheric perspective, so the far ridges are the palest things in
frame. Each ridge is filled with a vertical gradient whose top stop brightens by
the amount of sun that reaches it, and clipped to itself a soft dark wedge is drawn
just below the crest — that ridge's own shadow cast onto the valley beneath it. When
the sun is high enough, a warm hairline runs along each crest. Seven bands of
cool-white mist sit in the valley bottoms in additive blend, drifting on a sine.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas over the room gradient with a floating UI overlay
whose pointer-events pass through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace
station label above an italic serif headline with one line in warm amber, plus a
short paragraph. Top-right is a monospace meter stack showing sun altitude, ridge
count and a contrast value derived from the haze. Bottom is a glass tray with three
sliders — Sun (altitude −14° to +6°), Haze (0–100) and Ridges (4–12) — plus a Mist
toggle and a Dawn button that animates the sun upward through a full day-night
cycle.

Interaction: the sun slider is the master control — pulling it up climbs the sun
over the ridge line and lights every crest, pushing it down drops the frame into
night. Haze controls atmospheric depth; Ridges adds or removes layers, each
regenerated with its own silhouette. Mist toggles the valley bands. Dawn runs the
sun automatically from night through dawn to day and loops. The CSS room gradient
tracks the sun the whole time.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A valley at first light where the sun is the master control: nine double-sine
ridges drawn far-first with true atmospheric perspective (each a mix of its near
colour toward pale haze), each filled with a gradient that brightens on the crest,
each casting a soft shadow wedge onto the layer beneath, warm hairlines lighting up
the crests as the sun climbs, cool mist bands drifting in the valleys, and the
entire page gradient warming as the sun rises.

## Major visual techniques used
- Per-frame channel interpolation between a night palette and a day palette across
  four sky stops, driven by sun altitude
- Sun disc and wide radial glow whose radius and alpha scale with altitude, drifting
  on a sine, hidden below +0.4°
- Double-sine ridge silhouettes from twenty-one points, generated per ridge
- Atmospheric perspective: each ridge's colour mixed toward pale haze by distance
  and the Haze slider
- Vertical ridge gradient whose top stop brightens by how much sun reaches it
- Self-clipped shadow wedges: the ridge path used as a clip, then a gradient strip
  repeated along the crest below it
- Warm hairline crest strokes that appear only when the sun is high enough
- Seven additive cool-white mist bands drifting on sines in the valley bottoms
- CSS room gradient whose brightness and hue-rotate are recomputed per frame
- Live sun-altitude, ridge-count and haze-derived contrast readouts
- Dawn button that increments the sun slider continuously and loops
- Ridge slider that adds or removes layers, each regenerated with its own silhouette
- Italic serif headline with one amber line against 8.5px tracked monospace
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A scene with one master dial. Visitors pull the sun slider to climb the sun over the
ridges and watch every crest light, every shadow cast and the whole page gradient
warm; or press Dawn to let it happen by itself. Haze and Ridges reshape the
atmosphere, and Mist toggles the valley bands. The overlay never traps the pointer
except on the controls.
