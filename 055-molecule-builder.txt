# LAB BENCH — Molecule Builder

## Short title
Molecule Builder

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: a laboratory bench where a
visitor builds molecules atom by atom on canvas, with real valence rules. No external
dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no JS libraries.
All CSS and JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid
HTML5.

Artistic direction: a dark workbench. Panel #141b21 on ground #0d1216, hairlines at
rgba(200,230,220,.13), text #e4f2ec. One accent, phosphor mint #5cf2c0, with amber and
red reserved for formula subtitles and valence warnings. The bench surface is a very
faint 34px CSS grid over a mask-image radial fade, so it reads as graph paper lit only
at the centre. Behind the canvas draw a mint radial wash at 5% so it looks like a
spotlight.

Element shelf: eight element chips (H, C, N, O, S, P, Cl, Na), each with an element
ball rendered as a radial-gradient sphere in the element's own colour with a
14px glow in currentColor, plus a valence number. Bond order buttons (single, double,
triple) and three view modes: wire (a dark ring in the element colour with the symbol
inside), ball & stick (a shaded sphere built from a canvas radial-gradient with a
white 62% highlight at 0, the element colour at 0.45 and black 42% at the rim, with a
bold symbol) and space fill (the same sphere at 1.28 radius, no symbol).

Bonds are drawn as 2px lines in a pale iron colour, offset perpendicular to the bond
axis by 4px each for double and triple bonds, and shortened at each end by the atom
radius so they never poke into a sphere. The live readout counts atoms and bonds,
derives an empirical formula in Hill order (C, H, then everything else), and reports
over-valence in red.

Layout: nav with serif brand. Centred hero with mint eyebrow and an italic mint word.
Then the bench: a two-column grid of element/bond/view shelf and canvas + readout.
Then three tools (Empty the bench, Build ethanol, Free rotation). Then a three-cell
recipe row with italic mint titles and amber formulas. Then a three-cell notes row
with large mint serif figures. Then a ruled mono footer. Stacked to one column at
820px with the shelf border moving to the bottom.

Interaction: click empty bench to place the currently selected element; drag an atom
to reposition; drop one atom onto another to bond them at the chosen order; click a
bond to break it (atoms stay). A click without a drag arms the source atom as a
glowing green link origin, so a second click on another atom bonds the pair; the rope
between them is drawn as a dashed mint line while dragging. Over-valence is allowed
but flagged in the readout and by a transient toast. Hovering an atom tints its ring
mint; hovering a bond gives a not-allowed cursor. "Build ethanol" lays out nine atoms
with four single bonds each where chemistry demands it; "Free rotation" adds a slow
global turn that also breathes the shading of the spheres. The bench opens seeded
with a water molecule.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A lab bench on a faint grid: an eight-element shelf, single/double/triple bond order
and three rendering modes (wire rings, shaded ball-and-stick spheres, space fill)
driving a canvas where atoms are placed, dragged, clicked and dropped onto each other
to form bonds at the chosen order — click a bond to break it, and the readout below
counts atoms and bonds, derives an empirical formula in Hill order, and turns red the
moment any atom goes past its valence.

## Major visual techniques used
- Element balls as radial-gradient spheres with a 14px glow in currentColor
- Canvas spheres from a radial-gradient with a white 62% highlight, the mid colour at
  0.45 and 42% black at the rim, so they read as lit three-dimensional balls
- Bond order drawn by offsetting parallel strokes 4px perpendicular to the bond axis
- Bonds shortened at both ends by each atom's radius so they never overlap a sphere
- Lab bench graph paper from two 1px linear-gradients faded with a radial mask-image
- Wire view as a dark disc, a 2px ring in the element colour and a centred symbol
- Empirical formula assembled in Hill order from a live atom census
- Over-valence allowed, then flagged red, which is what makes the valence check a
  live feature rather than dead code
- Click-to-arm link origin with a dashed mint rope drawn while dragging
- Pointer capture on the canvas so drags survive leaving the element
- Hover states: mint ring on atoms, not-allowed cursor on bonds
- A slow global rotation that also breathes the shading of the spheres
- Bench seeded with a water molecule so the canvas is never empty
- Two-column bench collapsing to one column at 820px with the shelf border moved to
  the bottom

## Intended interaction model
A place to build. Visitors pick an element and tap the bench to place it, drag atoms
around, drop one onto another to bond them, choose double or triple before bonding,
and click any bond to snap it. The readout responds in real time — atoms, bonds,
formula, and a red warning when chemistry objects — and the recipe cards tell you
what water and ammonia look like before you build them.
