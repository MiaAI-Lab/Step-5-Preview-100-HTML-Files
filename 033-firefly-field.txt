# HOTARU — A Field at Dusk

## Short title
Firefly Field

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive firefly
field called HOTARU. No external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no
external images, no external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries. All CSS and
JavaScript inline, works offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a meadow at last light. The ground is a vertical gradient from
night blue #050d14 through deep teal #08161a to a green-black field floor #0d2016,
with two soft green radial washes low in the frame suggesting distant hedges. A
swaying grass silhouette is drawn on canvas as a few hundred quadratic curves, one
per blade, bending on a per-blade sine phase. Everything else is the beetles.

Each firefly carries position, velocity, a radius, a flash phase, a wander speed
and a bob phase. Its motion is a slow wander: two sine terms at different
frequencies and phases add small continuous accelerations, velocity damps at 0.985
and is clamped, so the flight is meandering rather than ballistic. The pointer
repels: within 150 pixels, each beetle is pushed directly away from the cursor with
a force that falls to zero at the boundary — so you part the swarm as you move
through it. Beetles are constrained to the lower 60% of the screen and bounce off
the edges.

The flash is the whole point. Each beetle's phase advances at a rate set by the
Rate slider; a flash value is computed as (sin(phase)+1)/2 raised to the 14th power,
which produces a very short bright pulse with a long dark gap. Above a threshold
the beetle draws a wide radial halo (white core through amber to transparent) plus
a tiny white dot, all in globalCompositeOperation 'lighter' so overlapping flashes
bloom; below it, the beetle draws only a faint amber body so the swarm remains
visible between flashes. "Synchronise" snaps every phase to an arithmetic sequence,
which makes the whole field pulse together for a moment before drifting apart again.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas over the gradient field with a floating UI overlay
whose pointer-events pass through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace
label above an italic serif headline with one line in firefly amber, plus a short
paragraph. Top-right is a monospace meter stack showing how many beetles are
currently lit, the distance to the nearest one, and a running phase value. Bottom
is a glass tray with three sliders — Count (20–600), Rate (flash speed) and Wander
(drift strength) — plus Synchronise and Dawn buttons, and a monospace hint.

Interaction: move the pointer through the field to part the swarm and see the
nearest-beetle readout respond. The three sliders change the population, flash rate
and drift live — Count adds or removes beetles without disturbing the others. Dawn
runs a loop that brightens the whole frame with a warm gradient and then returns to
dusk. On narrow screens the paragraph, meters and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A meadow at dusk where hundreds of fireflies meander on their own sine-driven
wander, flee your cursor with a distance-weighted push, and flash in short bright
pulses computed from a phase raised to the fourteenth power — haloed and additively
blended so overlaps bloom — with synchronise, dawn, and count, rate and wander
sliders.

## Major visual techniques used
- Two-sine wander acceleration with damping and clamping for meandering flight
- Distance-weighted pointer repulsion (falls to zero at 150 px) so the swarm parts
- Flash pulse from (sin φ + 1)/2 raised to the 14th power — short bright, long dark
- Additive compositing ('lighter') so overlapping halos bloom into brighter light
- Three-stop radial halos (white core → amber → transparent) plus a white core dot
- Faint amber body drawn between flashes so the swarm stays visible
- Phase arithmetic sequencing for the synchronise gesture
- Per-blade quadratic grass silhouette with independent sine sway phases
- Vertical dusk gradient with two low radial hedge washes
- NaN and infinity guards plus velocity clamping after the interaction forces
- Live meters: lit count, nearest distance, running phase
- Sliders that add or remove agents without disturbing existing ones
- Italic serif headline with one amber line against 8.5px tracked monospace
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
An ambient scene you can disturb. Visitors move the pointer to part the swarm, then
shape the field with the count, rate and wander sliders, snap the flashes into
unison with Synchronise, or let Dawn wash the field in warm light and let it fade
back to dusk. There is nothing to complete — the page is a place to put a cursor.
