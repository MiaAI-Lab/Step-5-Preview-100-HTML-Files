# SWELL — Nine Hours of Open Water

## Short title
Ocean Swell

## The exact prompt used to generate this HTML file

Create a completely self-contained single-file HTML page: an interactive ocean
swell model called SWELL, framed as a mid-Atlantic weather station readout. No
external dependencies whatsoever — no CDN, no web fonts, no external images, no
external SVG files, no JavaScript libraries. All CSS and JavaScript inline, works
offline by double-clicking, responsive, valid HTML5.

Artistic direction: a low sun over open water. The sky is a vertical gradient from
teal-navy #062b3d through #0c4a63 and #2a7f92 into a warm horizon band of #e0a86a,
with the sun as a disc near the horizon at x=0.74 wrapped in a wide warm radial
glow. Below the horizon, six wave layers stack from 60% to 100% of the viewport
height, each a different wavelength, amplitude, speed and phase; the far layers are
translucent teal and the near ones are almost opaque deep blue, each drawn with a
vertical gradient fill and a rim stroke in foam white whose alpha and width increase
toward the viewer. On top of the water, forty elliptical glitter flecks twinkle in
screen blend along a glitter path under the sun.

The physics-ish model: each layer's surface is a sum of three sines — a primary at
the layer's wavelength, a secondary at 1.9× the frequency and 34% of the amplitude,
and a wind-chop term at 3.7× the frequency scaled by the Chop slider and multiplied
by a per-layer coefficient so near waves get choppier. The whole pattern travels
along a direction angle (dir), applied by projecting the screen x-coordinate onto
that direction with cos(dir) — so changing the angle changes the apparent wavelength
and travel direction of every layer at once. Amplitude scales with the Swell
slider, speed with the Period slider, and the whole stack lifts or drops with the
Tide slider.

Layout: a full-viewport canvas with a floating UI overlay whose pointer-events pass
through except on the controls. Top-left is a monospace station label above an
italic serif headline with one line in foam white, plus a short paragraph.
Top-right is a monospace gauge stack — significant wave height in metres, period in
seconds, and wind in knots — all computed live from the sliders. Bottom is a glass
tray with four sliders (Swell, Period, Chop, Tide) plus Storm and Calm preset
buttons, and a monospace hint.

Interaction: dragging horizontally steers the swell direction, easing toward the
drag with a 0.04 lerp so changing heading feels like a ship coming round. The four
sliders retune the running model live, and Storm and Calm write complete
configurations into all four sliders at once. The gauges recompute every frame.
On narrow screens the paragraph, gauges and hint hide.

## One-paragraph description of the intended design
A station readout for a sea that behaves like a sea: six stacked wave layers of
triple-sine cross swells travelling along a steerable heading, wind chop that
raises the near layers more than the far ones, a low sun with a glitter path of
twinkling flecks, and four sliders plus Storm and Calm presets that reshape the
whole ocean live.

## Major visual techniques used
- Layered wave surfaces from a sum of three sines at 1×, 1.9× and 3.7× frequency
  with decreasing amplitude
- Directional travel by projecting screen x onto cos(dir), so one angle changes
  heading and apparent wavelength for every layer at once
- Six depth layers with increasing opacity, rim brightness and rim width toward the
  viewer
- Vertical gradient fills plus rim strokes for translucent water
- Low sun as a disc wrapped in a wide warm radial glow over a five-stop sky
- Forty elliptical glitter flecks twinkling in screen blend along a sun path
- Eased direction steering (0.04 lerp) from horizontal drag
- Live gauge computation (significant height, period, wind) from slider values
- Storm and Calm presets that write all four sliders via synthetic input events
- Tide as a global vertical offset on the whole wave stack
- Italic serif headline against 8.5–9px tracked monospace labels and gauges
- Floating glass tray with backdrop-filter blur; extras hidden under 760px

## Intended interaction model
A weather instrument with a wheel. Visitors drag to change the swell heading and
use four sliders — height, period, chop, tide — to set the sea, or press Storm or
Calm for a complete configuration in one tap. The gauges report what the model is
currently doing, so the page reads like a real station feed rather than a toy.
