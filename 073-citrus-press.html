i

Segments

Every fruit here is drawn from its real anatomy: rind, pith, then eight or ten segments of pulp around a white core, each one a vesicle sac full of juice.

ii

Yield

How much juice a fruit gives depends on the variety and on how big it is, and those are the only two variables in this panel. A large Valencia is worth nearly twice a small lime.

iii

Rind

The oil sits in the coloured layer, which is why a twist over the glass does more than a squeeze. The zest in this recipe is measured in the same units as the juice.

iv

Press

Hold the button and the fruit deforms under the ram, the pulp darkens, and the glass fills to whatever the fruit gives. It stops when the fruit is spent, not when you want it to.

v

Colour

Blood orange goes red because of anthocyanin, which only develops when the fruit ripens on cool nights. The same species, the same segment count, a different pigment.

vi

Season

Everything here is winter fruit. Navels keep on the tree for months, which is why the press runs from November to April and closes for the summer.
